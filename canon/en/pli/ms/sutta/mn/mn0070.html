Kīṭāgirisutta At KīṭāgiriAt Kīṭāgiri

Evaṁ me sutaṁ—I have heard thatSo I have heard.

ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā kāsīsu cārikaṁ carati mahatā bhikkhusaṅghena saddhiṁ. on one occasion the Blessed One was wandering on a tour of Kāsi with a large Saṅgha of monks.At one time the Buddha was wandering in the land of the Kāsis together with a large Saṅgha of mendicants.

Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi: There he addressed the monks:There the Buddha addressed the mendicants:

“ahaṁ kho, bhikkhave, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjāmi. “I abstain from the nighttime meal.“Mendicants, I abstain from eating at night.

Aññatra kho panāhaṁ, bhikkhave, rattibhojanā bhuñjamāno appābādhatañca sañjānāmi appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañca. As I am abstaining from the nighttime meal, I sense next-to-no illness, next-to-no affliction, lightness, strength, & comfortable abiding.Doing so, I find that I’m healthy and well, nimble, strong, and living comfortably.

Etha, tumhepi, bhikkhave, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjatha. Come, now. You, too, abstain from the nighttime meal.You too should never eat at night.

Aññatra kho pana, bhikkhave, tumhepi rattibhojanā bhuñjamānā appābādhatañca sañjānissatha appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañcā”ti. As you are abstaining from the nighttime meal, you, too, will sense next-to-no illness, next-to-no affliction, lightness, strength, & comfortable abiding.”Doing so, you’ll find that you’re healthy and well, nimble, strong, and living comfortably.”

“Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṁ. “As you say, lord,” the monks responded to him.“Yes, sir,” they replied.

Atha kho bhagavā kāsīsu anupubbena cārikaṁ caramāno yena kīṭāgiri nāma kāsīnaṁ nigamo tadavasari. Then, as he was wandering by stages in Kāsi, the Blessed One eventually arrived at a Kāsi town called Kīṭāgiri.Then the Buddha, traveling stage by stage in the land of the Kāsis, arrived at a town of the Kāsis named Kīṭāgiri,

Tatra sudaṁ bhagavā kīṭāgirismiṁ viharati kāsīnaṁ nigame. And there he stayed in the Kāsi town, Kīṭāgiri.and stayed there.

Tena kho pana samayena assajipunabbasukā nāma bhikkhū kīṭāgirismiṁ āvāsikā honti. Now at that time the monks led by Assaji & Punabbasu were residing in Kīṭāgiri.Now at that time the mendicants who followed Assaji and Punabbasuka were residing at Kīṭāgiri.

Atha kho sambahulā bhikkhū yena assajipunabbasukā bhikkhū tenupasaṅkamiṁsu; upasaṅkamitvā assajipunabbasuke bhikkhū etadavocuṁ: Then a large number of monks went to them and, on arrival, said to them,Then several mendicants went up to them and said,

“bhagavā kho, āvuso, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjati bhikkhusaṅgho ca. “The Blessed One and the Saṅgha of monks abstain from the nighttime meal.“Reverends, the Buddha never eats at night, and neither does the mendicant Saṅgha.

Aññatra kho panāvuso, rattibhojanā bhuñjamānā appābādhatañca sañjānanti appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañca. As they are abstaining from the nighttime meal, they sense next-to-no illness, next-to-no affliction, lightness, strength, & comfortable abiding.Doing so, they find that they’re healthy and well, nimble, strong, and living comfortably.

Etha, tumhepi, āvuso, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjatha. Come now, friends. You, too, abstain from the nighttime meal.You too should abstain from eating at night.

Aññatra kho panāvuso, tumhepi rattibhojanā bhuñjamānā appābādhatañca sañjānissatha appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañcā”ti. As you are abstaining from the nighttime meal, you, too, will sense next-to-no illness, next-to-no affliction, lightness, strength, & comfortable abiding.”Doing so, you’ll find that you’re healthy and well, nimble, strong, and living comfortably.”

Evaṁ vutte, assajipunabbasukā bhikkhū te bhikkhū etadavocuṁ: When this was said, the monks led by Assaji & Punabbasu said to those monks,When they said this, the mendicants who followed Assaji and Punabbasuka said to them,

“mayaṁ kho, āvuso, sāyañceva bhuñjāma pāto ca divā ca vikāle. “Friends, we eat in the evening, in the morning, & in the wrong-time during the day.“Reverends, we eat at dusk, at daybreak, and at the wrong time of day.

Te mayaṁ sāyañceva bhuñjamānā pāto ca divā ca vikāle appābādhatañca sañjānāma appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañca. As we are eating in the evening, and in the morning, & in the wrong-time during the day, we sense next-to-no illness, next-to-no affliction, lightness, strength, & comfortable abiding.Doing so, we find that we’re healthy and well, nimble, strong, and living comfortably.

Te mayaṁ kiṁ sandiṭṭhikaṁ hitvā kālikaṁ anudhāvissāma? Why should we, abandoning what is immediately visible, chase after something subject to time?Why should we give up what is apparent in the present to chase after what takes effect over time?

Sāyañceva mayaṁ bhuñjissāma pāto ca divā ca vikāle”ti. We will eat in the evening, in the morning, & in the wrong-time during the day.”We shall eat at dusk, at daybreak, and at the wrong time of day.”

Yato kho te bhikkhū nāsakkhiṁsu assajipunabbasuke bhikkhū saññāpetuṁ, atha yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṁsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdiṁsu. Ekamantaṁ nisinnā kho te bhikkhū bhagavantaṁ etadavocuṁ: When they were unable to convince the monks led by Assaji & Punabbasu, those monks went to the Blessed One [and told him what had happened].Since those mendicants were unable to persuade the mendicants who were followers of Assaji and Punabbasuka, they approached the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and told him what had happened.

“idha mayaṁ, bhante, yena assajipunabbasukā bhikkhū tenupasaṅkamimha; upasaṅkamitvā assajipunabbasuke bhikkhū etadavocumha:

‘bhagavā kho, āvuso, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjati bhikkhusaṅgho ca;

aññatra kho panāvuso, rattibhojanā bhuñjamānā appābādhatañca sañjānanti appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañca.

Etha, tumhepi, āvuso, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjatha.

Aññatra kho panāvuso, tumhepi rattibhojanā bhuñjamānā appābādhatañca sañjānissatha appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañcā’ti.

Evaṁ vutte, bhante, assajipunabbasukā bhikkhū amhe etadavocuṁ:

‘mayaṁ kho, āvuso, sāyañceva bhuñjāma pāto ca divā ca vikāle.

Te mayaṁ sāyañceva bhuñjamānā pāto ca divā ca vikāle appābādhatañca sañjānāma appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañca.

Te mayaṁ kiṁ sandiṭṭhikaṁ hitvā kālikaṁ anudhāvissāma?

Sāyañceva mayaṁ bhuñjissāma pāto ca divā ca vikāle’ti.

Yato kho mayaṁ, bhante, nāsakkhimha assajipunabbasuke bhikkhū saññāpetuṁ, atha mayaṁ etamatthaṁ bhagavato ārocemā”ti.

Atha kho bhagavā aññataraṁ bhikkhuṁ āmantesi: Then the Blessed One told a certain monk,So the Buddha addressed one of the monks,

“ehi tvaṁ, bhikkhu, mama vacanena assajipunabbasuke bhikkhū āmantehi: “Come, monk. In my name, call the monks led by Assaji & Punabbasu, saying,“Please, monk, in my name tell the mendicants who follow Assaji and Punabbasuka that

‘satthā āyasmante āmantetī’”ti. ‘The Teacher calls you, friends.’”the Teacher summons them.”

“Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho so bhikkhu bhagavato paṭissutvā yena assajipunabbasukā bhikkhū tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā assajipunabbasuke bhikkhū etadavoca: “As you say, lord,” the monk answered and, having gone to the monks led by Assaji & Punabbasu, on arrival he said,“Yes, sir,” that monk replied. He went to those mendicants and said,

“satthā āyasmante āmantetī”ti. “The Teacher calls you, friends.”“Venerables, the Teacher summons you.”

“Evamāvuso”ti kho assajipunabbasukā bhikkhū tassa bhikkhuno paṭissutvā yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkamiṁsu; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdiṁsu. Ekamantaṁ nisinne kho assajipunabbasuke bhikkhū bhagavā etadavoca: “As you say, friend,” the monks led by Assaji & Punabbasu replied. Etha, tumhepi, āvuso, aññatreva rattibhojanā bhuñjatha. Come now, friends. You, too, abstain from the nighttime meal.You too should never eat at night.

Evaṁ vutte, kira, bhikkhave, tumhe te bhikkhū evaṁ avacuttha: When this was said, did you say to them,When they said this, did you really say to them:

‘mayaṁ kho panāvuso, sāyañceva bhuñjāma pāto ca divā ca vikāle. ‘Friends, we eat in the evening, in the morning, & in the wrong-time during the day.‘Reverends, we eat at dusk, at daybreak, and at the wrong time of day.

Te mayaṁ sāyañceva bhuñjamānā pāto ca divā ca vikāle appābādhatañca sañjānāma appātaṅkatañca lahuṭṭhānañca balañca phāsuvihārañca. “Evaṁ, bhante”. “Yes, lord.”“Yes, sir.”

“Kiṁ nu me tumhe, bhikkhave, evaṁ dhammaṁ desitaṁ ājānātha yaṁ kiñcāyaṁ purisapuggalo paṭisaṁvedeti sukhaṁ vā dukkhaṁ vā adukkhamasukhaṁ vā tassa akusalā dhammā parihāyanti kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhantī”ti? “Monks, have you ever understood me to teach the Dhamma in this way: ‘Whatever a person experiences—pleasant, painful, or neither-pleasant-nor-painful—his unskillful qualities decrease and his skillful qualities grow’?”“Mendicants, have you ever known me to teach the Dhamma like this: no matter what this individual experiences—pleasurable, painful, or neutral—their unskillful qualities decline and their skillful qualities grow?”

“No hetaṁ, bhante”. “No, lord.”“No, sir.”

“Nanu me tumhe, bhikkhave, evaṁ dhammaṁ desitaṁ ājānātha idhekaccassa yaṁ evarūpaṁ sukhaṁ vedanaṁ vedayato akusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti kusalā dhammā parihāyanti, idha panekaccassa evarūpaṁ sukhaṁ vedanaṁ vedayato akusalā dhammā parihāyanti, kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti, idhekaccassa evarūpaṁ dukkhaṁ vedanaṁ vedayato akusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti kusalā dhammā parihāyanti, idha panekaccassa evarūpaṁ dukkhaṁ vedanaṁ vedayato akusalā dhammā parihāyanti kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti, idhekaccassa evarūpaṁ adukkhamasukhaṁ vedanaṁ vedayato akusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhanti kusalā dhammā parihāyanti, idha panekaccassa evarūpaṁ adukkhamasukhaṁ vedanaṁ vedayato akusalā dhammā parihāyanti kusalā dhammā abhivaḍḍhantī”ti? “And haven’t you understood me to teach the Dhamma in this way: ‘For someone feeling a pleasant feeling of this sort, unskillful qualities grow and skillful qualities decrease. “Evaṁ, bhante”. “Yes, lord.”“Yes, sir.”

“Sādhu, bhikkhave. “Good, monks.“Good, mendicants!

Sāpi nāma, bhikkhave, saddhā nāhosi; “Now, monks, there hasn’t been that conviction,Mendicants, there has not been that faith,

tampi nāma, bhikkhave, upasaṅkamanaṁ nāhosi; there hasn’t been that visiting,that approaching,

sāpi nāma, bhikkhave, payirupāsanā nāhosi; there hasn’t been that growing close…that paying homage,

tampi nāma, bhikkhave, sotāvadhānaṁ nāhosi; that lending ear…that listening,

tampi nāma, bhikkhave, dhammassavanaṁ nāhosi; that hearing of the Dhamma…that hearing the teachings,

sāpi nāma, bhikkhave, dhammadhāraṇā nāhosi; that remembering…that remembering the teachings,

sāpi nāma, bhikkhave, atthūpaparikkhā nāhosi; that penetration of the meaning of the teachings…that examining their meaning,

sāpi nāma, bhikkhave, dhammanijjhānakkhanti nāhosi; that agreement through pondering the teachings…that acceptance after deliberation,

sopi nāma, bhikkhave, chando nāhosi; that desire…that enthusiasm,

sopi nāma, bhikkhave, ussāho nāhosi; that willingness…that making an attempt,

sāpi nāma, bhikkhave, tulanā nāhosi; that contemplation…that weighing up,

Vippaṭipannāttha, bhikkhave, micchāpaṭipannāttha, bhikkhave. You have lost the way, monks. You have gone the wrong way, monks.You’re on a bad path, mendicants! You’re practicing the wrong way!

Kīva dūrevime, bhikkhave, moghapurisā apakkantā imamhā dhammavinayā. How far have you strayed, foolish men, from this Dhamma & Discipline!How far these futile men have strayed from this teaching and training!

Atthi, bhikkhave, catuppadaṁ veyyākaraṇaṁ yassuddiṭṭhassa viññū puriso nacirasseva paññāyatthaṁ ājāneyya. “Monks, there is a four-phrased statement that, when it is recited, an observant man will in no long time learn the meaning through discernment.There is an exposition in four statements, which a sensible person would quickly understand when it is recited.

Uddisissāmi vo, bhikkhave, ājānissatha me tan”ti? I will recite it, and you learn it from me.”I shall recite it for you, mendicants. Try to understand it.”

“Ke ca mayaṁ, bhante, ke ca dhammassa aññātāro”ti? “But, lord, who are we to be learners of the Dhamma?”“Sir, who are we next to those who understand the teaching?”

“Yopi so, bhikkhave, satthā āmisagaru āmisadāyādo āmisehi saṁsaṭṭho viharati tassa pāyaṁ evarūpī paṇopaṇaviyā na upeti: “Monks, even with a teacher devoted to material things, an heir of material things, who lives attached to material things, this sort of haggling (by his students) wouldn’t be proper:“Even with a teacher who values things of the flesh, is an heir in things of the flesh, who lives caught up in things of the flesh, you wouldn’t get into such haggling:

‘evañca no assa atha naṁ kareyyāma, na ca no evamassa na naṁ kareyyāmā’ti, kiṁ pana, bhikkhave, yaṁ tathāgato sabbaso āmisehi visaṁsaṭṭho viharati. ‘If we get this, we’ll do it; if we don’t, we won’t.’ So how could it be with regard to the Tathāgata, who dwells entirely detached from material things?‘If we get this, we’ll do that. If we don’t get this, we won’t do it.’ What then of the Realized One, who lives utterly detached from things of the flesh?

Saddhassa, bhikkhave, sāvakassa satthusāsane pariyogāhiya vattato ayamanudhammo hoti: “For a disciple who has conviction in the Teacher’s message & lives to penetrate it, what accords with the Dhamma is this:For a faithful disciple who is practicing to fathom the Teacher’s instructions, this is in line with the teaching:

‘satthā bhagavā, sāvakohamasmi; ‘The Blessed One is the Teacher, I am a disciple.‘The Buddha is my Teacher, I am his disciple.

jānāti bhagavā, nāhaṁ jānāmī’ti. He is the one who knows, not I.’The Buddha knows, I do not know.’

Saddhassa, bhikkhave, sāvakassa satthusāsane pariyogāhiya vattato ruḷhanīyaṁ satthusāsanaṁ hoti ojavantaṁ. For a disciple who has conviction in the Teacher’s message & lives to penetrate it, the Teacher’s message is healing & nourishing.For a faithful disciple who is practicing to fathom the Teacher’s instructions, the Teacher’s instructions are nourishing and nutritious.

Saddhassa, bhikkhave, sāvakassa satthusāsane pariyogāhiya vattato ayamanudhammo hoti: For a disciple who has conviction in the Teacher’s message & lives to penetrate it, what accords with the Dhamma is this:For a faithful disciple who is practicing to fathom the Teacher’s instructions, this is in line with the teaching:

‘kāmaṁ taco ca nhāru ca aṭṭhi ca avasissatu, sarīre upassussatu maṁsalohitaṁ, yaṁ taṁ purisathāmena purisavīriyena purisaparakkamena pattabbaṁ na taṁ apāpuṇitvā vīriyassa saṇṭhānaṁ bhavissatī’ti. ‘Gladly would I let the flesh & blood in my body dry up, leaving just the skin, tendons, & bones, but if I have not attained what can be reached through manly firmness, manly persistence, manly striving, there will be no relaxing my persistence.’‘Gladly, let only skin, sinews, and bones remain! Let the flesh and blood waste away in my body! I will not relax my energy until I have achieved what is possible by human strength, energy, and vigor.’

Saddhassa, bhikkhave, sāvakassa satthusāsane pariyogāhiya vattato dvinnaṁ phalānaṁ aññataraṁ phalaṁ pāṭikaṅkhaṁ—For a disciple who has conviction in the Teacher’s message & lives to penetrate it, one of two fruits can be expected:For a faithful disciple who is practicing to fathom the Teacher’s instructions, one of two results can be expected:

diṭṭheva dhamme aññā, sati vā upādisese anāgāmitā”ti. either gnosis here & now, or—if there be any remnant of clinging-sustenance—non-return.”enlightenment in this very life, or if there’s residue left behind, non-return.”

Idamavoca bhagavā. That is what the Blessed One said.That is what the Buddha said.

Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṁ abhinandunti. Gratified, the monks delighted in the Blessed One’s words.Satisfied, the mendicants approved what the Buddha said.
