Majjhima Nikāya 105 Middle Discourses 105

Sunakkhattasutta With Sunakkhatta

Evaṁ me sutaṁ—Thus I heard:So I have heard.

ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā vesāliyaṁ viharati mahāvane kūṭāgārasālāyaṁ. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Vesālī, in the Great Wood, in the Hall with the Peaked Roof.At one time the Buddha was staying near Vesālī, at the Great Wood, in the hall with the peaked roof.

Tena kho pana samayena sambahulehi bhikkhūhi bhagavato santike aññā byākatā hoti: Now on that occasion final knowledge had been declared by many bhikkhus in the Blessed One’s presence:Now at that time several mendicants had declared their enlightenment in the Buddha’s presence:

“‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyā’ti pajānāmā”ti. “We understand: ‘Birth is exhausted, the life divine has been lived, what was to be done is done, there is no more of this to come.’”“We understand: ‘Rebirth is ended, the spiritual journey has been completed, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing further for this place.’”

Assosi kho sunakkhatto licchaviputto: Sunakkhatta the Licchavi heard:[^1]Sunakkhatta the Licchavi heard about this.

“sambahulehi kira bhikkhūhi bhagavato santike aññā byākatā hoti: “It seems that final knowledge has been declared by many bhikkhus in the Blessed One’s presence:

‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyāti pajānāmā’”ti. ‘We understand: “Birth is exhausted, the life divine has been lived, what was to be done is done, there is no more of this to come.”’”

Atha kho sunakkhatto licchaviputto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavantaṁ abhivādetvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho sunakkhatto licchaviputto bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: Then Sunakkhatta the Licchavi went to the Blessed One, and after paying homage to him, he sat down at one side. When he had done so, Sunakkhatta the Licchavi said to the Blessed One:He went to the Buddha, bowed, sat down to one side, and said to him,

“sutaṁ metaṁ, bhante: “I have heard this, venerable sir:“Sir, I have heard that

‘sambahulehi kira bhikkhūhi bhagavato santike aññā byākatā—‘It seems that final knowledge has been declared by many bhikkhus in the Blessed One’s presence—several mendicants have declared their enlightenment in the Buddha’s presence.

khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyāti pajānāmā’ti. “Birth is exhausted, the life divine has been lived, what was to be done is done, there is no more of this to come”—so we understand.’

Ye te, bhante, bhikkhū bhagavato santike aññaṁ byākaṁsu: Now, venerable sir, do those bhikkhus who declared final knowledge in the Blessed One’s presence:

‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyāti pajānāmā’ti, kacci te, bhante, bhikkhū sammadeva aññaṁ byākaṁsu udāhu santetthekacce bhikkhū adhimānena aññaṁ byākaṁsū”ti? ‘We understand: “Birth is exhausted, the life divine has been lived, what was to be done is done, there is no more of this to come”’—do they, venerable sir, declare final knowledge rightly, or are there some bhikkhus here who declare final knowledge through overestimation?”I trust they did so rightly—or are there some who declared enlightenment out of overestimation?”

“Ye te, sunakkhatta, bhikkhū mama santike aññaṁ byākaṁsu: “Sunakkhatta, of those bhikkhus who declared final knowledge in my presence:

‘khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyāti pajānāmā’ti. ‘We understand: “Birth is exhausted, the life divine has been lived, what was to be done is done, there is no more of this to come”’—

Santetthekacce bhikkhū sammadeva aññaṁ byākaṁsu, santi panidhekacce bhikkhū adhimānenapi aññaṁ byākaṁsu. there are some bhikkhus here who declared final knowledge rightly, but there are also some bhikkhus here who declared final knowledge through overestimation.“Some of them did so rightly, Sunakkhatta, while others did so out of overestimation.

Tatra, sunakkhatta, ye te bhikkhū sammadeva aññaṁ byākaṁsu tesaṁ taṁ tatheva hoti; Now, Sunakkhatta, as to those bhikkhus who declared final knowledge rightly, it is so with them just as they say;Now, when mendicants declare enlightenment rightly, that is their truth.

ye pana te bhikkhū adhimānena aññaṁ byākaṁsu tatra, sunakkhatta, tathāgatassa evaṁ hoti: but as to those bhikkhus who declared final knowledge through overestimation, in that case, Sunakkhatta, it occurs to the Tathāgata:But when mendicants declare enlightenment out of overestimation, the Realized One thinks:

‘dhammaṁ nesaṁ desessan’ti. ‘I shall teach them the Dhamma.’‘I should teach them the Dhamma.’

Evañcettha, sunakkhatta, tathāgatassa hoti: So it is here, Sunakkhatta, that it occurs to the Tathāgata:If the Realized One thinks

‘dhammaṁ nesaṁ desessan’ti. ‘I shall teach them the Dhamma.’he should teach them the Dhamma,

Atha ca panidhekacce moghapurisā pañhaṁ abhisaṅkharitvā abhisaṅkharitvā tathāgataṁ upasaṅkamitvā pucchanti. And yet, here, certain misguided men, having elaborately formulated a question, approach the Tathāgata and ask it.but then certain futile men, having carefully planned a question, approach the Realized One and ask it,

Tatra, sunakkhatta, yampi tathāgatassa evaṁ hoti: In such a case, Sunakkhatta, even though it had occurred to the Tathāgata:then the Realized One

‘dhammaṁ nesaṁ desessan’ti tassapi hoti aññathattan”ti. ‘I shall teach them the Dhamma’—there comes to be a change in that intention.”changes his mind.”

“Etassa bhagavā kālo, etassa sugata kālo, “This is the time, Blessed One, this is the time, Fortunate One,“Now is the time, Blessed One! Now is the time, Holy One!

yaṁ bhagavā dhammaṁ deseyya. Bhagavato sutvā bhikkhū dhāressantī”ti. for the Blessed One to teach the Dhamma. Having heard it from the Blessed One, the bhikkhus will remember it.”Let the Buddha teach the Dhamma. The mendicants will listen and remember it.”

“Tena hi, sunakkhatta, suṇāhi, sādhukaṁ manasi karohi; bhāsissāmī”ti. “Well then, Sunakkhatta, listen and attend closely to what I shall say.”“Well then, Sunakkhatta, listen and apply your mind well, I will speak.”

“Evaṁ, bhante”ti kho sunakkhatto licchaviputto bhagavato paccassosi. “Yes, venerable sir,” Sunakkhatta the Licchavi replied to the Blessed One.“Yes, sir,” replied Sunakkhatta.

Bhagavā etadavoca—The Blessed One said this—The Buddha said this:

Pañca kho ime, sunakkhatta, kāmaguṇā. “There are, Sunakkhatta, these five cords of sensual desire.“Sunakkhatta, there are these five kinds of sensual stimulation.

Katame pañca? What five?What five?

Cakkhuviññeyyā rūpā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṁhitā rajanīyā, Forms cognizable by the eye that are wished for, desired, agreeable, likeable, connected with sensual desire, and provocative of lust,Sights known by the eye, which are likable, desirable, agreeable, pleasant, sensual, and arousing.

sotaviññeyyā saddā …pe… sounds cognizable by the ear …Sounds known by the ear …

ghānaviññeyyā gandhā … odours cognizable by the nose …Smells known by the nose …

jivhāviññeyyā rasā … flavours cognizable by the tongue …Tastes known by the tongue …

kāyaviññeyyā phoṭṭhabbā iṭṭhā kantā manāpā piyarūpā kāmūpasaṁhitā rajanīyā—tangibles cognizable by the body that are wished for, desired, agreeable, likeable, connected with sensual desire, and provocative of lust—Touches known by the body, which are likable, desirable, agreeable, pleasant, sensual, and arousing.

ime kho, sunakkhatta, pañca kāmaguṇā. these, Sunakkhatta, are the five cords of sensual desire.These are the five kinds of sensual stimulation.

Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṁ idhekacco purisapuggalo lokāmisādhimutto assa. Now it is possible, Sunakkhatta, that some individual person here might be intent on worldly material things.It’s possible that a certain individual person may be intent on worldly pleasures of the flesh.

Lokāmisādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṁ bhajati, tena ca vittiṁ āpajjati; For a person intent on worldly material things, Sunakkhatta, only talk that is fitting for this settles his mind, and he thinks and ponders in line with it, he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him;Such an individual engages in pertinent conversation, thinking and considering in line with that. They associate with that kind of person, and they find it rewarding.

āneñjapaṭisaṁyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṁ odahati, na aññā cittaṁ upaṭṭhāpeti, na ca taṁ purisaṁ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṁ āpajjati. but when talk connected with the imperturbable is going on, he does not want to listen, does not give ear, does not apply his mind to understanding it, and he does not associate with that kind of person, and he finds no satisfaction through him.But when talk connected with the imperturbable is going on they don’t want to listen. They don’t actively listen or try to understand. They don’t associate with that kind of person, and they don’t find it rewarding.

Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, puriso sakamhā gāmā vā nigamā vā ciravippavuttho assa. Suppose, Sunakkhatta, a man had long been away from his own village or town.Suppose a person had left their own village or town long ago,

So aññataraṁ purisaṁ passeyya tamhā gāmā vā nigamā vā acirapakkantaṁ. He might see another man who had recently left that village or town.and they saw another person who had only recently left there.

So taṁ purisaṁ tassa gāmassa vā nigamassa vā khematañca subhikkhatañca appābādhatañca puccheyya; He would ask that man about the safety, plenty, and freedom from sickness of that village or town;They would ask about whether their village was safe, with plenty of food and little disease,

tassa so puriso tassa gāmassa vā nigamassa vā khematañca subhikkhatañca appābādhatañca saṁseyya. and that man would tell him about the safety, plenty, and freedom from sickness of that village or town.and the other person would tell them the news.

Taṁ kiṁ maññasi, sunakkhatta, What do you think, Sunakkhatta,What do you think, Sunakkhatta?

api nu so puriso tassa purisassa sussūseyya, sotaṁ odaheyya, aññā cittaṁ upaṭṭhāpeyya, tañca purisaṁ bhajeyya, tena ca vittiṁ āpajjeyyā”ti? would that man listen to that man, give ear, apply his mind to understanding it, associate with that man, and find satisfaction through him?”Would that person want to listen to that other person? Would they actively listen and try to understand? Would they associate with that person, and find it rewarding?”

“Evaṁ, bhante”. “Yes, venerable sir.”“Yes, sir.”

“Evameva kho, sunakkhatta, ṭhānametaṁ vijjati yaṁ idhekacco purisapuggalo lokāmisādhimutto assa. “So too, Sunakkhatta, it is possible that some individual person here might be intent on worldly material things.“In the same way, it’s possible that a certain individual may be intent on worldly pleasures of the flesh.

Lokāmisādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṁ bhajati, tena ca vittiṁ āpajjati; For a person intent on worldly material things, Sunakkhatta, only talk that is fitting for this settles his mind, and he thinks and ponders in line with it, he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him;Such an individual engages in pertinent conversation, thinking and considering in line with that. They associate with that kind of person, and they find it rewarding.

āneñjapaṭisaṁyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṁ odahati, na aññā cittaṁ upaṭṭhāpeti, na ca taṁ purisaṁ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṁ āpajjati. but when talk connected with the imperturbable is going on, he does not want to listen, does not give ear, does not apply his mind to understanding it, and he does not associate with that kind of person, and he finds no satisfaction through him.But when talk connected with the imperturbable is going on they don’t want to listen. They don’t actively listen or try to understand. They don’t associate with that kind of person, and they don’t find it rewarding.

So evamassa veditabbo: He should be understood thus:You should know of them:

‘āneñjasaṁyojanena hi kho visaṁyutto lokāmisādhimutto purisapuggalo’ti. ‘This individual person intent on worldly material things is not freed from the fetter of the imperturbable.’‘That individual is intent on pleasures of the flesh, for they’re detached from things connected with the imperturbable.’

Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṁ idhekacco purisapuggalo āneñjādhimutto assa. Now it is possible, Sunakkhatta, that some individual person here might be intent on the imperturbable.[^2]It’s possible that a certain individual person may be intent on the imperturbable.

Āneñjādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṁ bhajati, tena ca vittiṁ āpajjati; For a person intent on the imperturbable, Sunakkhatta, only talk that is fitting for this settles his mind, and he thinks and ponders in line with it, he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him;Such an individual engages in pertinent conversation, thinking and considering in line with that. lokāmisapaṭisaṁyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṁ odahati, na aññā cittaṁ upaṭṭhāpeti, na ca taṁ purisaṁ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṁ āpajjati. but when talk connected with worldly material things is going on, he does not want to listen, does not give ear, does not apply his mind to understanding it, and he does not associate with that kind of person, and he finds no satisfaction through him.But when talk connected with worldly pleasures of the flesh is going on they don’t want to listen. Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, paṇḍupalāso bandhanā pavutto abhabbo haritattāya; Just as, Sunakkhatta, a yellow leaf, fallen from its stalk, is incapable of becoming green again;Suppose there was a fallen, withered leaf. It’s incapable of becoming green again.

evameva kho, sunakkhatta, āneñjādhimuttassa purisapuggalassa ye lokāmisasaṁyojane se pavutte. so too, Sunakkhatta, for a person intent on the imperturbable, the fetter of worldly material things has fallen away.In the same way, an individual intent on the imperturbable has dropped the connection with worldly pleasures of the flesh.

So evamassa veditabbo: He should be understood thus:You should know of them:

‘lokāmisasaṁyojanena hi kho visaṁyutto āneñjādhimutto purisapuggalo’ti. ‘This individual person intent on the imperturbable is freed from the fetter of worldly material things.’‘That individual is intent on the imperturbable, for they’re detached from things connected with pleasures of the flesh.’

Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṁ idhekacco purisapuggalo ākiñcaññāyatanādhimutto assa. āneñjapaṭisaṁyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṁ odahati, na aññā cittaṁ upaṭṭhāpeti, na ca taṁ purisaṁ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṁ āpajjati. but when talk connected with the imperturbable is going on, he does not want to listen, does not give ear, does not apply his mind to understanding it, and he does not associate with that kind of person, and he finds no satisfaction through him.But when talk connected with the imperturbable is going on they don’t want to listen. They don’t actively listen or try to understand. They don’t associate with that kind of person, and they don’t find it rewarding.

Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, puthusilā dvedhābhinnā appaṭisandhikā hoti; Just as, Sunakkhatta, a large rock, split in two, cannot be joined together again;Suppose there was a broad rock that had been broken in half, so that it could not be put back together again.

evameva kho, sunakkhatta, ākiñcaññāyatanādhimuttassa purisapuggalassa ye āneñjasaṁyojane se bhinne. so too, Sunakkhatta, for a person intent on the base of nothingness, the fetter of the imperturbable is broken.In the same way, an individual intent on the dimension of nothingness has broken the connection with the imperturbable.

So evamassa veditabbo: He should be understood thus:You should know of them:

‘āneñjasaṁyojanena hi kho visaṁyutto ākiñcaññāyatanādhimutto purisapuggalo’ti. ‘This individual person intent on the base of nothingness is freed from the fetter of the imperturbable.’‘That individual is intent on the dimension of nothingness, for they’re detached from things connected with the imperturbable.’

Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṁ idhekacco purisapuggalo nevasaññānāsaññāyatanādhimutto assa. Nevasaññānāsaññāyatanādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṁ bhajati, tena ca vittiṁ āpajjati; For a person intent on the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, Sunakkhatta, only talk that is fitting for this settles his mind, and he thinks and ponders in line with it, he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him;Such an individual engages in pertinent conversation, thinking and considering in line with that. ākiñcaññāyatanapaṭisaṁyuttāya ca pana kathāya kacchamānāya na sussūsati, na sotaṁ odahati, na aññā cittaṁ upaṭṭhāpeti, na ca taṁ purisaṁ bhajati, na ca tena vittiṁ āpajjati. but when talk connected with the base of nothingness is going on, he does not want to listen, does not give ear, does not apply his mind to understanding it, and he does not associate with that kind of person, and he finds no satisfaction through him.But when talk connected with the dimension of nothingness is going on they don’t want to listen. Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, puriso manuññabhojanaṁ bhuttāvī chaḍḍeyya. Suppose, Sunakkhatta, a man had eaten delicious food and then vomited it up.Suppose someone had eaten some satisfying food and thrown it up.

Taṁ kiṁ maññasi, sunakkhatta, What do you think, Sunakkhatta,What do you think, Sunakkhatta?

api nu tassa purisassa tasmiṁ bhatte puna bhottukamyatā assā”ti? would that man have any desire to eat that food again?”Would that person want to eat that food again?”

“No hetaṁ, bhante”. “No, venerable sir.”“No, sir.

“Taṁ kissa hetu”? “Why is that?”Why is that?

“Aduñhi, bhante, bhattaṁ paṭikūlasammatan”ti. “Because, venerable sir, that food is now reckoned as disgusting.”Because that food is deemed repulsive.”

“Evameva kho, sunakkhatta, nevasaññānāsaññāyatanādhimuttassa purisapuggalassa ye ākiñcaññāyatanasaṁyojane se vante. “So too, Sunakkhatta, for a person intent on the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, the fetter of the base of nothingness has been vomited up.“In the same way, an individual intent on the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception has vomited the connection with the dimension of nothingness.

Sammā nibbānādhimuttassa kho, sunakkhatta, purisapuggalassa tappatirūpī ceva kathā saṇṭhāti, tadanudhammañca anuvitakketi, anuvicāreti, tañca purisaṁ bhajati, tena ca vittiṁ āpajjati; For a person rightly intent on Nibbāna, Sunakkhatta, only talk that is fitting for this settles his mind, and he thinks and ponders in line with it, he associates with that kind of person, and he finds satisfaction through him;Such an individual engages in pertinent conversation, thinking and considering in line with that. Seyyathāpi, sunakkhatta, tālo matthakacchinno abhabbo puna viruḷhiyā; Just as, Sunakkhatta, a palm tree with its crown cut off is incapable of growing again;Suppose there was a palm tree with its crown cut off. It’s incapable of further growth.

evameva kho, sunakkhatta, sammā nibbānādhimuttassa purisapuggalassa ye nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaṁyojane se ucchinnamūle tālāvatthukate anabhāvaṅkate āyatiṁ anuppādadhamme. so too, Sunakkhatta, for a person rightly intent on Nibbāna, the fetter of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception has been cut off, cut off at the root, made like a palm stump, done away with, no more subject to future arising.In the same way, an individual rightly intent on extinguishment has cut off the connection with the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception at the root, made it like a palm stump, obliterated it, so it’s unable to arise in the future.

So evamassa veditabbo: He should be understood thus:You should know of them:

‘nevasaññānāsaññāyatanasaṁyojanena hi kho visaṁyutto sammā nibbānādhimutto purisapuggalo’ti. ‘This individual person rightly intent on Nibbāna is freed from the fetter of the base of neither-perception-nor-non-perception.’‘That individual is rightly intent on extinguishment, for they’re detached from things connected with the dimension of neither perception nor non-perception.’

Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, sunakkhatta, vijjati yaṁ idhekaccassa bhikkhuno evamassa: Now it is possible, Sunakkhatta, that a certain bhikkhu here might think thus:It’s possible that a certain mendicant might think:
