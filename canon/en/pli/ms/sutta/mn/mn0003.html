Majjhima Nikāya 3 Middle Discourses 3

Dhammadāyādasutta Heirs in the Teaching

Evaṁ me sutaṁ—So I have heard. Thus I heard:

ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Sāvatthi in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.

Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi: There the Buddha addressed the mendicants, There he addressed the bhikkhus thus:

“bhikkhavo”ti. “Mendicants!” ‘Bhikkhus.’

“Bhadante”ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṁ. “Venerable sir,” they replied. ‘Venerable Sir,’ they replied.

Bhagavā etadavoca: The Buddha said this: The Blessed One said this:

“Dhammadāyādā me, bhikkhave, bhavatha, mā āmisadāyādā. “Mendicants, be my heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh. ‘Bhikkhus, be my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things.

Atthi me tumhesu anukampā: Out of sympathy for you, I think, Out of pity for you I have thought:

‘kinti me sāvakā dhammadāyādā bhaveyyuṁ, no āmisadāyādā’ti. ‘How can my disciples become heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh?’ “How shall my disciples be my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things?”

Tumhe ca me, bhikkhave, āmisadāyādā bhaveyyātha no dhammadāyādā, tumhepi tena ādiyā bhaveyyātha: If you become heirs in things of the flesh, not in the teaching, that will make you liable to the accusation: If you are my heirs in material things, not my heirs in Dhamma, it will be a reproach to you:

‘āmisadāyādā satthusāvakā viharanti, no dhammadāyādā’ti; ‘The Teacher’s disciples live as heirs in things of the flesh, not in the teaching.’ “The Master’s disciples live as his heirs in material things, not as his heirs in Dhamma”,

ahampi tena ādiyo bhaveyyaṁ: And it will make me liable to the accusation: and it will be a reproach to me that

‘āmisadāyādā satthusāvakā viharanti, no dhammadāyādā’ti. ‘The Teacher’s disciples live as heirs in things of the flesh, not in the teaching.’ “The Master’s disciples live as his heirs in material things, not as his heirs in Dhamma”.

Tumhe ca me, bhikkhave, dhammadāyādā bhaveyyātha, no āmisadāyādā, tumhepi tena na ādiyā bhaveyyātha: If you become heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh, that will make you not liable to the accusation: If you are my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things, it will be no reproach to you that

‘dhammadāyādā satthusāvakā viharanti, no āmisadāyādā’ti; ‘The Teacher’s disciples live as heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh.’ “The Master’s disciples live as his heirs in Dhamma, not as his heirs in material things”,

ahampi tena na ādiyo bhaveyyaṁ: And it will make me not liable to the accusation: and it will be no reproach to me that

‘dhammadāyādā satthusāvakā viharanti, no āmisadāyādā’ti. ‘The Teacher’s disciples live as heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh.’ “The Master’s disciples live as his heirs in Dhamma, not as his heirs in material things”.

Tasmātiha me, bhikkhave, dhammadāyādā bhavatha, mā āmisadāyādā. So, mendicants, be my heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh. Therefore, bhikkhus, be my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things.

Atthi me tumhesu anukampā: Out of sympathy for you, I think, Out of pity for you I have thought:

‘kinti me sāvakā dhammadāyādā bhaveyyuṁ, no āmisadāyādā’ti. ‘How can my disciples become heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh?’ “How shall my disciples be my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things?”

Idhāhaṁ, bhikkhave, bhuttāvī assaṁ pavārito paripuṇṇo pariyosito suhito yāvadattho; Suppose that I had eaten and refused more food, being replete, and having had as much as I needed. Now, bhikkhus, suppose that I had eaten, refused more, had my fill, finished, had enough, had what I needed,

siyā ca me piṇḍapāto atirekadhammo chaḍḍanīyadhammo. And there was some extra almsfood that was going to be thrown away. and some alms-food was left over to be thrown away.

Atha dve bhikkhū āgaccheyyuṁ jighacchādubbalyaparetā. Then two mendicants were to come who were weak with hunger. Then two bhikkhus arrived hungry and weak,

Tyāhaṁ evaṁ vadeyyaṁ: I’d say to them, and I told them:

‘ahaṁ khomhi, bhikkhave, bhuttāvī pavārito paripuṇṇo pariyosito suhito yāvadattho; ‘Mendicants, I have eaten and refused more food, being replete, and having had as much as I need. “Bhikkhus, I have eaten, refused more … had what I needed,

atthi ca me ayaṁ piṇḍapāto atirekadhammo chaḍḍanīyadhammo. And there is this extra almsfood that’s going to be thrown away. but there is this alms-food of mine left over to be thrown away;

Sace ākaṅkhatha, bhuñjatha, no ce tumhe bhuñjissatha, idānāhaṁ appaharite vā chaḍḍessāmi, appāṇake vā udake opilāpessāmī’ti. Eat it if you like. Otherwise I’ll throw it out where there is little that grows, or drop it into water that has no living creatures.’ eat if you like; if you do not then I shall throw it now where there is no greenery or drop into water where there is no life”;

Tatrekassa bhikkhuno evamassa: Then one of those mendicants thought, then one bhikkhu thought:

‘bhagavā kho bhuttāvī pavārito paripuṇṇo pariyosito suhito yāvadattho; ‘The Buddha has eaten and refused more food. “The Blessed One has eaten, refused more … had what he needed,

atthi cāyaṁ bhagavato piṇḍapāto atirekadhammo chaḍḍanīyadhammo. And he has some extra almsfood that’s going to be thrown away. but there is this alms-food of the Blessed One left over to be thrown away;

Sace mayaṁ na bhuñjissāma, idāni bhagavā appaharite vā chaḍḍessati, appāṇake vā udake opilāpessati. If we don’t eat it he’ll throw it away. if we do not eat it the Blessed One will throw it away …

Vuttaṁ kho panetaṁ bhagavatā: But the Buddha has also said: but this has been said by the Blessed One:

“dhammadāyādā me, bhikkhave, bhavatha, mā āmisadāyādā”ti. “Be my heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh.” ‘Bhikkhus, be my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things’:

Āmisaññataraṁ kho panetaṁ, yadidaṁ piṇḍapāto. And almsfood is one of the things of the flesh. now this alms-food is one of the material things;

Yannūnāhaṁ imaṁ piṇḍapātaṁ abhuñjitvā imināva jighacchādubbalyena evaṁ imaṁ rattindivaṁ vītināmeyyan’ti. Instead of eating this almsfood, why don’t I spend this day and night weak with hunger?’ suppose that instead of eating this alms-food I pass the night and day hungry and weak?”

So taṁ piṇḍapātaṁ abhuñjitvā teneva jighacchādubbalyena evaṁ taṁ rattindivaṁ vītināmeyya. And that’s what they did. and instead of eating that alms-food he passed that night and day hungry and weak;

Atha dutiyassa bhikkhuno evamassa: Then the second of those mendicants thought, then the second bhikkhu thought:

‘bhagavā kho bhuttāvī pavārito paripuṇṇo pariyosito suhito yāvadattho; ‘The Buddha has eaten and refused more food. “The Blessed One has eaten … had what he needed,

atthi cāyaṁ bhagavato piṇḍapāto atirekadhammo chaḍḍanīyadhammo. And he has some extra almsfood that’s going to be thrown away. but there is this alms-food of the Blessed One’s left over to be thrown away …

Sace mayaṁ na bhuñjissāma, idāni bhagavā appaharite vā chaḍḍessati, appāṇake vā udake opilāpessati. If we don’t eat it he’ll throw it away. …

Yannūnāhaṁ imaṁ piṇḍapātaṁ bhuñjitvā jighacchādubbalyaṁ paṭivinodetvā evaṁ imaṁ rattindivaṁ vītināmeyyan’ti. Why don’t I eat this almsfood, then spend the day and night having dispelled my hunger and weakness?’ Suppose that I eat this alms-food and pass the night and day neither hungry nor weak?”

So taṁ piṇḍapātaṁ bhuñjitvā jighacchādubbalyaṁ paṭivinodetvā evaṁ taṁ rattindivaṁ vītināmeyya. And that’s what they did. And after eating that alms-food he passed the night and day not hungry and weak.

Kiñcāpi so, bhikkhave, bhikkhu taṁ piṇḍapātaṁ bhuñjitvā jighacchādubbalyaṁ paṭivinodetvā evaṁ taṁ rattindivaṁ vītināmeyya, atha kho asuyeva me purimo bhikkhu pujjataro ca pāsaṁsataro ca. Even though that mendicant, after eating the almsfood, spent the day and night having dispelled hunger and weakness, it is the former mendicant who is more worthy of respect and praise. Now although that bhikkhu by eating that alms-food passed the night and day neither hungry nor weak, yet the first bhikkhu is more to be respected and commended by me.

Taṁ kissa hetu? Why is that? Why is that?

Tañhi tassa, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno dīgharattaṁ appicchatāya santuṭṭhiyā sallekhāya subharatāya vīriyārambhāya saṁvattissati. Because for a long time that will conduce to that mendicant being of few wishes, content, self-effacing, unburdensome, and energetic. Because that will for long conduce to his fewness of wishes, contentment, effacement, easy support and arousal of energy.

Tasmātiha me, bhikkhave, dhammadāyādā bhavatha, mā āmisadāyādā. So, mendicants, be my heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh. Therefore, bhikkhus, be my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things.

Atthi me tumhesu anukampā: Out of sympathy for you, I think, Out of pity for you I have thought:

‘kinti me sāvakā dhammadāyādā bhaveyyuṁ, no āmisadāyādā’”ti. ‘How can my disciples become heirs in the teaching, not in things of the flesh?’” “How shall my disciples be my heirs in Dhamma, not my heirs in material things?”’

Idamavoca bhagavā. That is what the Buddha said. That is what the Blessed One said.

Idaṁ vatvāna sugato uṭṭhāyāsanā vihāraṁ pāvisi. When he had spoken, the Holy One rose from his seat and entered his dwelling. Having said that, he rose from his seat and went into his dwelling.

Tatra kho āyasmā sāriputto acirapakkantassa bhagavato bhikkhū āmantesi: Then, not long after the Buddha left, Venerable Sāriputta said to the mendicants, Soon after he had gone, the venerable Sāriputta addressed the bhikkhus thus:

“āvuso bhikkhave”ti. “Reverends, mendicants!” ‘Friends, bhikkhus.’

“Āvuso”ti kho te bhikkhū āyasmato sāriputtassa paccassosuṁ. “Reverend,” they replied. ‘Friend,’ they replied.

Āyasmā sāriputto etadavoca: Sāriputta said this: The venerable Sāriputta said this:

“Kittāvatā nu kho, āvuso, satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekaṁ nānusikkhanti, kittāvatā ca pana satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekamanusikkhantī”ti? “Reverends, how do the disciples of a Teacher who lives in seclusion not train in seclusion? And how do they train in seclusion?” ‘Friends, in what way do disciples of a Master who lives secluded not train in seclusion? And in what way do disciples of a Master who lives secluded train in seclusion?’

“Dūratopi kho mayaṁ, āvuso, āgacchāma āyasmato sāriputtassa santike etassa bhāsitassa atthamaññātuṁ. “Reverend, we would travel a long way to learn the meaning of this statement in the presence of Venerable Sāriputta. ‘Indeed, friend, we would come from far away to learn from the venerable Sāriputta the meaning of this pronouncement.

Sādhu vatāyasmantaṁyeva sāriputtaṁ paṭibhātu etassa bhāsitassa attho; May Venerable Sāriputta himself please clarify the meaning of this. It would be good if the meaning of this pronouncement would occur to the venerable Sāriputta;

āyasmato sāriputtassa sutvā bhikkhū dhāressantī”ti. The mendicants will listen and remember it.” having heard it from him, the bhikkhus will remember it.’

“Tena hāvuso, suṇātha, sādhukaṁ manasi karotha, bhāsissāmī”ti. “Well then, reverends, listen and apply your mind well, I will speak.” ‘Then, listen and heed well what I shall say.’

“Evamāvuso”ti kho te bhikkhū āyasmato sāriputtassa paccassosuṁ. “Yes, reverend,” they replied. ‘Yes friend,’ they replied.

Āyasmā sāriputto etadavoca: Sāriputta said this: The venerable Sāriputta said this:

“Kittāvatā nu kho, āvuso, satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekaṁ nānusikkhanti? “Reverends, how do the disciples of a Teacher who lives in seclusion not train in seclusion? ‘Friends, in what way do disciples of a Master who lives secluded not train in seclusion?

Idhāvuso, satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekaṁ nānusikkhanti, The disciples of a Teacher who lives in seclusion do not train in seclusion. Here, friends, disciples of a Master who lives secluded do not train in seclusion,

yesañca dhammānaṁ satthā pahānamāha, te ca dhamme nappajahanti, They don’t give up what the Teacher tells them to give up. they do not abandon what the Master tells them to abandon,

bāhulikā ca honti, sāthalikā, okkamane pubbaṅgamā, paviveke nikkhittadhurā. They’re indulgent and slack, leaders in backsliding, neglecting seclusion. they are busy and careless, forward in erring, giving up seclusion.

Tatrāvuso, therā bhikkhū tīhi ṭhānehi gārayhā bhavanti. In this case, the senior mendicants should be criticized on three grounds. In this the elder bhikkhus are to be blamed for three reasons.

‘Satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekaṁ nānusikkhantī’ti—‘The disciples of a Teacher who lives in seclusion do not train in seclusion.’ ‘As disciples of a Master who lives secluded they do not train in seclusion’:

iminā paṭhamena ṭhānena therā bhikkhū gārayhā bhavanti. This is the first ground. they are to be blamed for this first reason.

‘Yesañca dhammānaṁ satthā pahānamāha te ca dhamme nappajahantī’ti—‘They don’t give up what the Teacher tells them to give up.’ ‘They do not abandon what the Master tells them to abandon’:

iminā dutiyena ṭhānena therā bhikkhū gārayhā bhavanti. This is the second ground. they are to be blamed for this second reason.

‘Bāhulikā ca, sāthalikā, okkamane pubbaṅgamā, paviveke nikkhittadhurā’ti—‘They’re indulgent and slack, leaders in backsliding, neglecting seclusion.’ ‘They are busy, careless, forward in erring, giving up seclusion’:

iminā tatiyena ṭhānena therā bhikkhū gārayhā bhavanti. This is the third ground. they are to be blamed for this third reason.

Therā, āvuso, bhikkhū imehi tīhi ṭhānehi gārayhā bhavanti. The senior mendicants should be criticized on these three grounds. The elder bhikkhus are to be blamed for these three reasons.

Kittāvatā ca panāvuso, satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekamanusikkhanti? And how do the disciples of a Teacher who lives in seclusion train in seclusion? ‘In what way do disciples of a Master who lives secluded train in seclusion?

Idhāvuso, satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekamanusikkhanti—The disciples of a Teacher who lives in seclusion train in seclusion. Here, disciples of a Master who lives secluded train in seclusion;

yesañca dhammānaṁ satthā pahānamāha te ca dhamme pajahanti; They give up what the Teacher tells them to give up. they abandon what the Master tells them to abandon;

na ca bāhulikā honti, na sāthalikā okkamane nikkhittadhurā paviveke pubbaṅgamā. They’re not indulgent and slack, leaders in backsliding, neglecting seclusion. they are not busy and not careless, they give up erring and are forward in seclusion.

Tatrāvuso, therā bhikkhū tīhi ṭhānehi pāsaṁsā bhavanti. In this case, the senior mendicants should be praised on three grounds. In this the elder bhikkhus are to be commended for three reasons.

‘Satthu pavivittassa viharato sāvakā vivekamanusikkhantī’ti—‘The disciples of a Teacher who lives in seclusion train in seclusion.’ ‘As disciples of a Master who lives secluded they train in seclusion’:

iminā paṭhamena ṭhānena therā bhikkhū pāsaṁsā bhavanti. This is the first ground. they are to be commended for this first reason.

iminā tatiyena ṭhānena therā bhikkhū pāsaṁsā bhavanti. This is the third ground. they are to be commended for this third reason.

Tatrāvuso, lobho ca pāpako doso ca pāpako. The bad thing here is greed and hate. ‘Friends, the evil herein is greed and it is hate.

Lobhassa ca pahānāya dosassa ca pahānāya atthi majjhimā paṭipadā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati. There is a middle way of practice for giving up greed and hate. It gives vision and knowledge, and leads to peace, direct knowledge, awakening, and extinguishment. There is a Middle Way for the abandoning of greed and hate, giving vision, giving knowledge, that leads to peace, to direct knowledge, to enlightenment, to Nibbāna.

Katamā ca sā, āvuso, majjhimā paṭipadā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati? And what is that middle way of practice? And what is that Middle Way?

Ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, seyyathidaṁ—It is simply this noble eightfold path, that is: It is just this Noble Eightfold Path, that is to say,

sammādiṭṭhi sammāsaṅkappo sammāvācā sammākammanto sammāājīvo sammāvāyāmo sammāsati sammāsamādhi. right view, right purpose, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right immersion. right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration.

Ayaṁ kho sā, āvuso, majjhimā paṭipadā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati. This is that middle way of practice, which gives vision and knowledge, and leads to peace, direct knowledge, awakening, and extinguishment. This is the Middle Way giving vision, giving knowledge, that leads to peace, to direct knowledge, to enlightenment, to Nibbāna.

Tatrāvuso, kodho ca pāpako upanāho ca pāpako …pe… The bad thing here is anger and acrimony. … ‘Friends, the evil herein is anger and it is revenge …

makkho ca pāpako paḷāso ca pāpako, disdain and contempt … the evil herein is contempt and it is domineering,

issā ca pāpikā maccherañca pāpakaṁ, jealousy and stinginess … the evil herein is envy and it is avarice,

māyā ca pāpikā sāṭheyyañca pāpakaṁ, deceit and deviousness … the evil herein is deceit and it is fraud,

thambho ca pāpako sārambho ca pāpako, obstinacy and aggression … the evil herein is obduracy and it is presumption,

māno ca pāpako atimāno ca pāpako, conceit and arrogance … the evil herein is conceit and it is pride (haughtiness),

mado ca pāpako pamādo ca pāpako. vanity and negligence. the evil herein is vanity and it is negligence.

Madassa ca pahānāya pamādassa ca pahānāya atthi majjhimā paṭipadā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati. Katamā ca sā, āvuso, majjhimā paṭipadā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattati? And what is that middle way of practice? And what is that Middle Way?

Ayameva ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo, seyyathidaṁ—It is simply this noble eightfold path, that is: It is just this Noble Eightfold Path, that is to say,

sammādiṭṭhi sammāsaṅkappo sammāvācā sammākammanto sammāājīvo sammāvāyāmo sammāsati sammāsamādhi. right view, right purpose, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, and right immersion. right view, right intention, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration.

Ayaṁ kho sā, āvuso, majjhimā paṭipadā cakkhukaraṇī ñāṇakaraṇī upasamāya abhiññāya sambodhāya nibbānāya saṁvattatī”ti. This is that middle way of practice, which gives vision and knowledge, and leads to peace, direct knowledge, awakening, and extinguishment.” This is the Middle Way giving vision, giving knowledge, that leads to peace, to direct knowledge, to enlightenment, to Nibbāna.’”

Idamavocāyasmā sāriputto. That is what Venerable Sāriputta said. That is what the venerable Sāriputta said.

Attamanā te bhikkhū āyasmato sāriputtassa bhāsitaṁ abhinandunti. Satisfied, the mendicants approved what Sāriputta said. The bhikkhus were satisfied, and they delighted in the venerable Sāriputta’s words.

Dhammadāyādasuttaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ tatiyaṁ.
