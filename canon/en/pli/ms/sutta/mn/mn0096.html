Majjhima Nikāya 96 Middle Discourses 96

Esukārīsutta With Esukārī

Evaṁ me sutaṁ—Thus I heard:So I have heard.

ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Sāvatthi in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s Park.At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery.

Atha kho esukārī brāhmaṇo yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavatā saddhiṁ sammodi. Then Esukari the divine went to the Blessed One and exchanged greetings with him.Then Esukārī the brahmin went up to the Buddha, and exchanged greetings with him.

Sammodanīyaṁ kathaṁ sāraṇīyaṁ vītisāretvā ekamantaṁ nisīdi. Ekamantaṁ nisinno kho esukārī brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: When the courteous and amiable talk was finished, he sat down at one side. When he had done so, he said:When the greetings and polite conversation were over, he sat down to one side and said to the Buddha:

“brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, catasso pāricariyā paññapenti—‘Master Gotama, divines describe four (levels of) worship:“Worthy Gotama, the brahmins prescribe four kinds of service:

brāhmaṇassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti, khattiyassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti, vessassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti, suddassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti. They describe the divines’ level of worship, the warrior-nobles’ (level of) worship, the burgess’s (level of) worship, the artisans’ (level of) worship.for a brahmin, an aristocrat, a peasant, and a menial.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti: Now this divines’ (level of) worship that divines describe is this:This is the service they prescribe for a brahmin:

‘brāhmaṇo vā brāhmaṇaṁ paricareyya, khattiyo vā brāhmaṇaṁ paricareyya, vesso vā brāhmaṇaṁ paricareyya, suddo vā brāhmaṇaṁ paricareyyā’ti. a divine can worship a divine, or so can a warrior-noble, or a burgess or an artisan also worship a divine;‘A brahmin, an aristocrat, a peasant, and a menial may all serve a brahmin.’

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti. this is the divines’ (level of) worship that divines describe.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti: The warrior-nobles’ (level of) worship that divines describe is this:This is the service they prescribe for an aristocrat:

‘khattiyo vā khattiyaṁ paricareyya, vesso vā khattiyaṁ paricareyya, suddo vā khattiyaṁ paricareyyā’ti. a warrior-noble can worship a warrior-noble, or so can a burgess or an artisan worship a warrior-noble;‘An aristocrat, a peasant, and a menial may all serve an aristocrat.’

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti. this is the warrior-nobles’ (level of) worship that divines describe.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti: The burgess’s (level of) worship that divines describe is this:This is the service they prescribe for a peasant:

‘vesso vā vessaṁ paricareyya, suddo vā vessaṁ paricareyyā’ti. a burgess can worship a burgess or so can an artisan worship a burgess;‘A peasant or a menial may serve a peasant.’

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti. this is the burgess’s (level of) worship that divines describe.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti: The artisans’ (level of) worship that divines describe is this:This is the service they prescribe for a menial:

‘suddova suddaṁ paricareyya. only an artisan can worship an artisan;‘Only a menial may serve a menial.

Ko panañño suddaṁ paricarissatī’ti? for who else will worship an artisan?For who else will serve a menial?’

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa pāricariyaṁ paññapenti. This is the artisans’ (level of) worship that divines describe.

Brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, imā catasso pāricariyā paññapenti. Divines describe these four (levels of) worship, Master Gotama.These are the four kinds of service that the brahmins prescribe.

Idha bhavaṁ gotamo kimāhā”ti? What does Master Gotama say about that?’What do you say about this?”

“Kiṁ pana, brāhmaṇa, sabbo loko brāhmaṇānaṁ etadabbhanujānāti: ‘imā catasso pāricariyā paññapentū’”ti? ‘How then, divine, does all the world agree that divines should describe these four (levels of) worship?’“But brahmin, did the whole world authorize the brahmins to prescribe these four kinds of service?”

“No hidaṁ, bho gotama”. ‘No, Master Gotama.’“No, worthy Gotama.”

“Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, puriso daliddo assako anāḷhiyo. Tassa akāmassa bilaṁ olaggeyyuṁ: ‘idaṁ te, ambho purisa, maṁsaṁ khāditabbaṁ, mūlañca anuppadātabban’ti. ‘Suppose there were a poor, penniless, destitute man, and they hung a joint (of meat) on him against his will, (saying:) “Good man, you must eat this meat, and money must be paid for it when you do so”;“It’s as if they were to force a chop on a poor, penniless person, telling them, ‘Eat this meat and pay for it!’

Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, brāhmaṇā appaṭiññāya tesaṁ samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṁ, atha ca panimā catasso pāricariyā paññapenti. so too, divine, divines who describe these four (levels of) worship lack the authority of their monks and divines.In the same way, the brahmins have prescribed these four kinds of service without the consent of those ascetics and brahmins.

Nāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṁ paricaritabban’ti vadāmi; nāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṁ na paricaritabban’ti vadāmi. ‘I do not say that all are to be worshipped, nor do I say that all are not to be worshipped;Brahmin, I don’t say that you should serve everyone, nor do I say that you shouldn’t serve anyone.

Yaṁ hissa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, nāhaṁ taṁ ‘paricaritabban’ti vadāmi; for I say that he should not be worshipped for the worshipping of whom a man is worse and not better;I say that you shouldn’t serve someone if serving them makes you worse, not better.

yañca khvāssa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo tamahaṁ ‘paricaritabban’ti vadāmi. and I say that he should be worshipped for the worshipping of whom a man is better and not worse.’And I say that you should serve someone if serving them makes you better, not worse.

Khattiyañcepi, brāhmaṇa, evaṁ puccheyyuṁ: ‘If people were to ask a warrior-noble:If they were to ask an aristocrat this,

‘yaṁ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, yaṁ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo; “Which one of these two should you worship, namely, him for the worshipping of whom you are worse and not better, or him for the worshipping of whom you are better and not worse?”‘Who should you serve? Someone in whose service you get worse, or someone in whose service you get better?’

kamettha paricareyyāsī’ti, khattiyopi hi, brāhmaṇa, sammā byākaramāno evaṁ byākareyya: then, rightly answering, a warrior-noble should answer thus:Answering rightly, an aristocrat would say,

‘yañhi me paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, nāhaṁ taṁ paricareyyaṁ; yañca kho me paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo tamahaṁ paricareyyan’ti. “I should not worship him for the worship of whom I am worse and not better; I should worship him for the worship of whom I am better and not worse.”‘Someone in whose service I get better.’

Brāhmaṇañcepi, brāhmaṇa …pe… ‘If people were to ask a divine …If they were to ask a brahmin …

vessañcepi, brāhmaṇa …pe… ‘If people were to ask a burgess …a peasant …

suddañcepi, brāhmaṇa, evaṁ puccheyyuṁ: ‘If people were to ask an artisanor a menial this,

‘yaṁ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, yaṁ vā te paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo; Which one of these two should you worship, namely, him for the worshipping of whom you are worse and not better, or him for the worshipping of whom you are better and not worse‘Who should you serve? Someone in whose service you get worse, or someone in whose service you get better?’

kamettha paricareyyāsī’ti, suddopi hi, brāhmaṇa, sammā byākaramāno evaṁ byākareyya: Answering rightly, a menial would say,

‘yañhi me paricarato pāricariyāhetu pāpiyo assa na seyyo, nāhaṁ taṁ paricareyyaṁ; yañca kho me paricarato pāricariyāhetu seyyo assa na pāpiyo tamahaṁ paricareyyan’ti. I should not worship him for the worship of whom I am worse and not better; I should worship him for the worship of whom I am better and not worse.”‘Someone in whose service I get better.’

Nāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uccākulīnatā seyyaṁso’ti vadāmi, na panāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uccākulīnatā pāpiyaṁso’ti vadāmi; ‘I do not say that a man is better for (being of) a clan of high rank, nor do I say that he is worse for (being of) a clan of high rank;Brahmin, I don’t say that an eminent family makes you a better or worse person.

nāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷāravaṇṇatā seyyaṁso’ti vadāmi, na panāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷāravaṇṇatā pāpiyaṁso’ti vadāmi; I do not say that he is better for (being of) an upper caste, nor do I say that he is worse for (being of) an upper caste;I don’t say that exceptional beauty makes you a better or worse person.

nāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷārabhogatā seyyaṁso’ti vadāmi, na panāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘uḷārabhogatā pāpiyaṁso’ti vadāmi. I do not say that he is better for (possessing) great property, nor do I say that he is worse for (possessing) great property.I don’t say that exceptional wealth makes you a better or worse person.

Uccākulīnopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātī hoti, adinnādāyī hoti, kāmesumicchācārī hoti, musāvādī hoti, pisuṇāvāco hoti, pharusāvāco hoti, samphappalāpī hoti, abhijjhālu hoti, byāpannacitto hoti, micchādiṭṭhi hoti. ‘Here one of a clan of high rank may be a killer of living beings, a taker of what is not given, misconducted in sexual desires, a speaker of falsehood, malicious in speech, harsh in speech, a gossip, covetous, with a mind of ill will, and wrong in his views.For some people from eminent families kill living creatures, steal, and commit sexual misconduct. They use speech that’s false, backbiting, harsh, or nonsensical. And they’re covetous, malicious, with wrong view.

Tasmā ‘na uccākulīnatā seyyaṁso’ti vadāmi. It is for that, and not for (being of) a clan of high rank, that he is bad, I say.That’s why I don’t say that an eminent family makes you a better person.

Uccākulīnopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti, adinnādānā paṭivirato hoti, kāmesumicchācārā paṭivirato hoti, musāvādā paṭivirato hoti, pisuṇāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, pharusāya vācāya paṭivirato hoti, samphappalāpā paṭivirato hoti, anabhijjhālu hoti, abyāpannacitto hoti, sammādiṭṭhi hoti. And here one of a clan of high rank may abstain from killing living beings, from taking what is not given, from misconduct in sexual desires, from speaking falsehood, from malicious speech, from harsh speech, from gossip, and be uncovetousness, with no mind of ill will, and right in his views.But some people from eminent families also refrain from killing living creatures, stealing, and sexual misconduct. They refrain from using speech that’s false, divisive, harsh, or nonsensical. And they’re content, kind-hearted, with right view.

Tasmā ‘na uccākulīnatā pāpiyaṁso’ti vadāmi. It is for that, and not for (being of) a clan of high rank, that he is good, I say.That’s why I don’t say that an eminent family makes you a worse person.

Uḷāravaṇṇopi hi, brāhmaṇa … ‘Here one of an upper caste may be a killer of living beings …And some people who are exceptionally beautiful …

pe…

uḷārabhogopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātī hoti … ‘Here one possessing great property may be a killer of living beings …And some people who are exceptionally wealthy kill living creatures … and have wrong view.

pe…

micchādiṭṭhi hoti. wrong in his views.

Tasmā ‘na uḷārabhogatā seyyaṁso’ti vadāmi. It is for that, and not for possessing great property, that he is good, I say.That’s why I don’t say that exceptional wealth makes you a better person.

Uḷārabhogopi hi, brāhmaṇa, idhekacco pāṇātipātā paṭivirato hoti …pe… sammādiṭṭhi hoti. But some people who are exceptionally wealthy also refrain from killing living creatures … and have right view.

Tasmā ‘na uḷārabhogatā pāpiyaṁso’ti vadāmi. It is for that, and not for possessing great property, that he is worse, I say.That’s why I don’t say that any of these things makes you a better or worse person.

Nāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṁ paricaritabban’ti vadāmi, na panāhaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ‘sabbaṁ na paricaritabban’ti vadāmi. ‘I do not say that all are to be worshipped; nor do I say that all are not to be worshipped.Brahmin, I don’t say that you should serve everyone, nor do I say that you shouldn’t serve anyone.

Yaṁ hissa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu saddhā vaḍḍhati, sīlaṁ vaḍḍhati, sutaṁ vaḍḍhati, cāgo vaḍḍhati, paññā vaḍḍhati, tamahaṁ ‘paricaritabban’ti vadāmi. For I say that he should be worshipped for the worshipping of whom a man’s faith and his virtue and his learning and his generosity and his understanding increase.’And I say that you should serve someone if serving them makes you grow in faith, ethics, learning, generosity, and wisdom.

Yaṁ hissa, brāhmaṇa, paricarato pāricariyāhetu na saddhā vaḍḍhati, na sīlaṁ vaḍḍhati, na sutaṁ vaḍḍhati, na cāgo vaḍḍhati, na paññā vaḍḍhati, nāhaṁ taṁ ‘paricaritabban’ti vadāmī”ti. I say that you shouldn’t serve someone if serving them doesn’t make you grow in faith, ethics, learning, generosity, and wisdom.”

Evaṁ vutte, esukārī brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: When this was said, Esukārī the divine said:When he had spoken, Esukārī said to him:

“brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, cattāri dhanāni paññapenti—‘Master Gotama, divines describe four (sources of) wealth:“Worthy Gotama, the brahmins prescribe four kinds of wealth:

brāhmaṇassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti, khattiyassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti, vessassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti, suddassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti. the divines own (source of) wealth, the warrior-nobles’ own (source of) wealth, the burgess’s own (source of) wealth, and the artisans’ own (soruce of) wealth.for a brahmin, an aristocrat, a peasant, and a menial.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti bhikkhācariyaṁ; Now the divines’ own (source of) wealth that divines describe is alms gathering;The wealth they prescribe for a brahmin is living on alms.

bhikkhācariyañca pana brāhmaṇo sandhanaṁ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṁ ādiyamānoti. but the divine who oversteps his own (source of) wealth consisting of alms gathering abuses his position as does a caretaker who takes what is not given to him.A brahmin who scorns his own wealth, living on alms, fails in his duty like a guard who steals.

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā brāhmaṇassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti. This is the divines’ own (source of) wealth that divines describe.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti dhanukalāpaṁ; The warrior-nobles’ own (source of) wealth that divines describe is the quiver:The wealth they prescribe for an aristocrat is the bow and quiver.

dhanukalāpañca pana khattiyo sandhanaṁ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṁ ādiyamānoti. but a warrior-noble who oversteps his own (source of) wealth consisting in the quiver abuses his position as does a caretaker who takes what is not given to him.An aristocrat who scorns his own wealth, the bow and quiver, fails in his duty like a guard who steals.

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā khattiyassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti. This is the warrior-nobles’ own (source of) wealth that divines describe.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti kasigorakkhaṁ; The burgess’s own (source of) wealth that divines describe is ploughing and cattle-farming:The wealth they prescribe for a peasant is farming and animal husbandry.

kasigorakkhañca pana vesso sandhanaṁ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṁ ādiyamānoti. but a burgess who oversteps his own (source of) wealth consisting in ploughing and cattle-farming abuses his position as does a caretaker who takes what is not given to him.A peasant who scorns his own wealth, farming and animal husbandry, fails in his duty like a guard who steals.

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā vessassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti. This is the burgess’s own (source of) wealth that divines describe.

Tatridaṁ, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti asitabyābhaṅgiṁ; The artisans’ own (source of) wealth that divines describe is the yoke loaded with mown (harvest)The wealth they prescribe for a menial is the scythe and flail.

asitabyābhaṅgiñca pana suddo sandhanaṁ atimaññamāno akiccakārī hoti gopova adinnaṁ ādiyamānoti. but an artisan who oversteps his own (source of) wealth consisting in the yoke loaded with mown (harvest) abuses his position as does a caretaker who takes what is not given to him.A menial who scorns his own wealth, the scythe and flail, fails in his duty like a guard who steals.

Idaṁ kho, bho gotama, brāhmaṇā suddassa sandhanaṁ paññapenti. This is the artisans’ own (source of) wealth that divines describe.

Brāhmaṇā, bho gotama, imāni cattāri dhanāni paññapenti. Master Gotama, divines describe these four (sources of) wealth.These are the four kinds of wealth that the brahmins prescribe.

Idha bhavaṁ gotamo kimāhā”ti? What does Master Gotama say about that?’What do you say about this?”

“Kiṁ pana, brāhmaṇa, sabbo loko brāhmaṇānaṁ etadabbhanujānāti: ‘imāni cattāri dhanāni paññapentū’”ti? ‘How then, divine, does all the world agree that divines should describe these four (sources of) wealth?’“But brahmin, did the whole world authorize the brahmins to prescribe these four kinds of wealth?”

“No hidaṁ, bho gotama”. ‘No, Master Gotama.’“No, worthy Gotama.”

“Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, puriso daliddo assako anāḷhiyo. Tassa akāmassa bilaṁ olaggeyyuṁ: ‘idaṁ te, ambho purisa, maṁsaṁ khāditabbaṁ, mūlañca anuppadātabban’ti. ‘Suppose there were a poor, penniless, destitute man, and they hung a joint (of meat) upon him against his will, (saying:) “Good man, you must eat this meat, and money must be paid for it when you do so”;“It’s as if they were to force a chop on a poor, penniless person, telling them, ‘Eat this meat and pay for it!’

Evameva kho, brāhmaṇa, brāhmaṇā appaṭiññāya tesaṁ samaṇabrāhmaṇānaṁ, atha ca panimāni cattāri dhanāni paññapenti. so too, divine, divines who describe these four (sources of) wealth lack the authority of their monks and divines.’In the same way, the brahmins have prescribed these four kinds of wealth without the consent of these ascetics and brahmins.

Ariyaṁ kho ahaṁ, brāhmaṇa, lokuttaraṁ dhammaṁ purisassa sandhanaṁ paññapemi. ‘I describe the Noble Supermundane Dhamma as a man’s own source of wealth.I assert that a person’s own wealth is the noble, transcendent teaching.

Porāṇaṁ kho panassa mātāpettikaṁ kulavaṁsaṁ anussarato yattha yattheva attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti tena teneva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati. But wherever the reconception of his selfhood takes place, he is reckoned by that according to the old standing heritages of his parents’ clans.But they are reckoned by recollecting the traditional family customs of their mother and father wherever they are incarnated.

Khattiyakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘khattiyo’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati; If the reconception of his selfhood takes place in a warrior-noble clan, he is reckoned as a warrior-noble.If they incarnate in a family of aristocrats they are reckoned as an aristocrat.

brāhmaṇakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘brāhmaṇo’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati; If the reconception of his selfhood takes place in a divine clan, he is reckoned as a divine.If they incarnate in a family of brahmins they are reckoned as a brahmin.

vessakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘vesso’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati; If the reconception of his selfhood takes place in a burgess clan, he is reckoned as a burgess.If they incarnate in a family of peasants they are reckoned as a peasant.

suddakule ce attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti ‘suddo’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati. If the reconception of his selfhood takes place in an artisan clan, he is reckoned as an artisan.If they incarnate in a family of menials they are reckoned as a menial.

Seyyathāpi, brāhmaṇa, yaṁyadeva paccayaṁ paṭicca aggi jalati tena teneva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati. ‘Just as fire is reckoned only by the conditions dependent on which it burns.It’s like fire, which is reckoned according to the very same condition dependent upon which it burns.

Kaṭṭhañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘kaṭṭhaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati; (When) fire burns dependent on logs, it is reckoned only as a log fire;A fire that burns dependent on logs is reckoned as a log fire.

sakalikañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘sakalikaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati; (when) fire burns dependent on faggots, it is reckoned only as a faggot fire;A fire that burns dependent on twigs is reckoned as a twig fire.

tiṇañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘tiṇaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati; (when) fire burns dependent on grass, it is reckoned only as a grass fire;A fire that burns dependent on grass is reckoned as a grass fire.

gomayañce paṭicca aggi jalati ‘gomayaggi’tveva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati. (when) fire burns dependent on cowdung, it is reckoned only as a cowdung fire.A fire that burns dependent on cow-dung is reckoned as a cow-dung fire.

Evameva kho ahaṁ, brāhmaṇa, ariyaṁ lokuttaraṁ dhammaṁ purisassa sandhanaṁ paññapemi. So too I describe the Noble Supermundane Dhamma as a man’s own (source of) wealth.In the same way, I assert that a person’s own wealth is the noble, transcendent teaching.

Porāṇaṁ kho panassa mātāpettikaṁ kulavaṁsaṁ anussarato yattha yattheva attabhāvassa abhinibbatti hoti tena teneva saṅkhyaṁ gacchati. But wherever the reconception of his selfhood takes place, he is reckoned by that according to the old standing heritages of his parents’ clans.But they are reckoned by recollecting the traditional family customs of their mother and father wherever they are incarnated.
