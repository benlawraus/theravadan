Majjhima Nikāya 11 Middle Discourses 11

Cūḷasīhanādasutta The Shorter Discourse on the Lion’s Roar

Evaṁ me sutaṁ—So I have heard. Thus I heard:

ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā sāvatthiyaṁ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme. At one time the Buddha was staying near Sāvatthī in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapiṇḍika’s monastery. On one occasion the Blessed One was living at Sāvatthi in Jeta’s Grove, Anāthapindika’s Park.

Tatra kho bhagavā bhikkhū āmantesi: There the Buddha addressed the mendicants, There he addressed the bhikkhus thus:

“bhikkhavo”ti. “Mendicants!” ‘Bhikkhus.’

“Bhadante”ti te bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṁ. “Venerable sir,” they replied. ‘Venerable Sir,’ they replied.

Bhagavā etadavoca: The Buddha said this: The Blessed One said this:

“Idheva, bhikkhave, samaṇo, idha dutiyo samaṇo, idha tatiyo samaṇo, idha catuttho samaṇo; “‘Only here is there a true ascetic, here a second ascetic, here a third ascetic, and here a fourth ascetic. ‘Bhikkhus, only here is there a monk, only here a second monk, only here a third monk, only here a fourth monk.

suññā parappavādā samaṇebhi aññehīti. Other sects are empty of ascetics.’ The doctrines of others are devoid of monks:

Evametaṁ, bhikkhave, sammā sīhanādaṁ nadatha. This, mendicants, is how you should rightly roar your lion’s roar. that is how you should rightly roar your lion’s roar.

Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, bhikkhave, vijjati yaṁ aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ vadeyyuṁ: It’s possible that wanderers of other religions might say: ‘It is possible that wanderers of other sects here might ask:

‘ko panāyasmantānaṁ assāso, kiṁ balaṁ, yena tumhe āyasmanto evaṁ vadetha—‘But what is the source of the venerables’ certainty and forcefulness that they say this?’ “But what is the venerable ones’ (source of) comfort and strength that you venerable ones say thus?”

idheva samaṇo, idha dutiyo samaṇo, idha tatiyo samaṇo, idha catuttho samaṇo;

suññā parappavādā samaṇebhi aññehī’ti?

Evaṁvādino, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evamassu vacanīyā: You should say to them: Wanderers of other sects who ask thus may be answered in this way:

‘atthi kho no, āvuso, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena cattāro dhammā akkhātā ye mayaṁ attani sampassamānā evaṁ vadema—‘There are four things explained by the Blessed One, who knows and sees, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha. Seeing these things in ourselves we say that: “Friends, four dhammas have been declared to us by the Blessed One who knows and sees, Arahant and Fully Enlightened, on seeing which in ourselves we say thus:

idheva samaṇo, idha dutiyo samaṇo, idha tatiyo samaṇo, idha catuttho samaṇo; “Only here is there a true ascetic, here a second ascetic, here a third ascetic, and here a fourth ascetic. ‘Only here is there a monk, only here a second monk, only here a third monk, only here a fourth monk.

suññā parappavādā samaṇebhi aññehīti. Other sects are empty of ascetics.” The doctrines of others are devoid of monks.’

Katame cattāro? What four? What are the four?

Atthi kho no, āvuso, satthari pasādo, atthi dhamme pasādo, atthi sīlesu paripūrakāritā; We have confidence in the Teacher, we have confidence in the teaching, and we have fulfilled the precepts. We have confidence in the Master, we have confidence in the Dhamma, we have perfected the virtues,

sahadhammikā kho pana piyā manāpā—And we have love and affection for those who share our path, and those who share the Dhamma are dear to us

gahaṭṭhā ceva pabbajitā ca. both laypeople and renunciates. whether they are laymen or gone forth and they are loved by us.

Ime kho no, āvuso, tena bhagavatā jānatā passatā arahatā sammāsambuddhena cattāro dhammā akkhātā ye mayaṁ attani sampassamānā evaṁ vadema—These are the four things.’ These are the four dhammas declared by the Blessed One who knows and sees, an Arahant and Fully Enlightened, on seeing which in ourselves we say as we do.”

idheva samaṇo, idha dutiyo samaṇo, idha tatiyo samaṇo, idha catuttho samaṇo;

suññā parappavādā samaṇebhi aññehī’ti.

Ṭhānaṁ kho panetaṁ, bhikkhave, vijjati yaṁ aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ vadeyyuṁ: It’s possible that wanderers of other religions might say: ‘It is possible that wanderers of other sects might say thus:

‘amhākampi kho, āvuso, atthi satthari pasādo yo amhākaṁ satthā, amhākampi atthi dhamme pasādo yo amhākaṁ dhammo, mayampi sīlesu paripūrakārino yāni amhākaṁ sīlāni, ‘We too have confidence in the Teacher—our Teacher; we have confidence in the teaching—our teaching; and we have fulfilled the precepts—our precepts. “Friends, we too have confidence in the Master, that is, our Master; we too have confidence in the Dhamma, that is, our Dhamma; we too have perfected the virtues, that is, our virtues;

amhākampi sahadhammikā piyā manāpā—And we have love and affection for those who share our path, and those who share Dhamma are dear to us too

gahaṭṭhā ceva pabbajitā ca. both laypeople and renunciates. whether they are laymen or gone forth and they are loved by us.

Idha no, āvuso, ko viseso ko adhippayāso kiṁ nānākaraṇaṁ yadidaṁ tumhākañceva amhākañcā’ti? What, then, is the difference between you and us?’ what is the distinction here, friends, what is the variance, what is the difference, between you and us?’’

Evaṁvādino, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evamassu vacanīyā: You should say to them: Wanderers of other sects who ask thus may be answered in this way:

‘kiṁ panāvuso, ekā niṭṭhā, udāhu puthu niṭṭhā’ti? ‘Well, reverends, is the goal one or many?’ “How then, friends, is the goal? One or many?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, the wanderers would say: Answering rightly, the wanderers of other sects would answer thus:

‘ekāvuso, niṭṭhā, na puthu niṭṭhā’ti. ‘The goal is one, reverends, not many.’ “Friends, the goal is one, not many.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā sarāgassa udāhu vītarāgassā’ti? ‘But is that goal for the greedy or for those free of greed?’ ”But, friends, is that goal for one affected by lust or free from lust?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, the wanderers would say: Answering rightly, the wanderers from other sects would answer thus:

‘vītarāgassāvuso, sā niṭṭhā, na sā niṭṭhā sarāgassā’ti. ‘That goal is for those free of greed, not for the greedy.’ “Friends, the goal is for one free from lust, not affected by lust.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā sadosassa udāhu vītadosassā’ti? ‘Is it for the hateful or those free of hate?’ ”But, friends is that goal for one affected by hate or free from hate?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, they would answer:

‘vītadosassāvuso, sā niṭṭhā, na sā niṭṭhā sadosassā’ti. ‘It’s for those free of hate.’ “Friends, the goal is for one free from hate, not affected by hate.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā samohassa udāhu vītamohassā’ti? ‘Is it for the delusional or those free of delusion?’ ”But, friends, is that goal for one affected by delusion or free from delusion?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, they would answer:

‘vītamohassāvuso, sā niṭṭhā, na sā niṭṭhā samohassā’ti. ‘It’s for those free of delusion.’ “Friends, the goal is for one free from delusion, not affected by delusion.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā sataṇhassa udāhu vītataṇhassā’ti? ‘Is it for those who crave or those rid of craving?’ ”But, friends, is that goal for one affected by craving or free from craving?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, they would answer:

‘vītataṇhassāvuso, sā niṭṭhā, na sā niṭṭhā sataṇhassā’ti. ‘It’s for those rid of craving.’ “Friends, the goal is for one free from craving, not affected by craving.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā saupādānassa udāhu anupādānassā’ti? ‘Is it for those who have fuel for grasping or those who do not?’ ”But, friends, is that goal for one affected by clinging, or free from clinging?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, they would answer:

‘anupādānassāvuso, sā niṭṭhā, na sā niṭṭhā saupādānassā’ti. ‘It’s for those who do not have fuel for grasping.’ “Friends, the goal is for one free from clinging, not affected by clinging.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā viddasuno udāhu aviddasuno’ti? ‘Is it for the knowledgeable or the ignorant?’ ”But, friends, is that goal for one who has vision or for one without vision?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, they would answer:

‘viddasuno, āvuso, sā niṭṭhā, na sā niṭṭhā aviddasuno’ti. ‘It’s for the knowledgeable.’ “Friends, that goal is for one with vision, not for one without vision.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā anuruddhappaṭiviruddhassa udāhu ananuruddhaappaṭiviruddhassā’ti? ‘Is it for those who favor and oppose or for those who don’t favor and oppose?’ ”But, friends, is that goal for one who favours and opposes, or for one who does not favour and oppose?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, they would answer:

‘ananuruddhaappaṭiviruddhassāvuso, sā niṭṭhā, na sā niṭṭhā anuruddhappaṭiviruddhassā’ti. ‘It’s for those who don’t favor and oppose.’ “Friends, the goal is for one who does not favour and oppose, not for one who favours and opposes.”-

‘Sā panāvuso, niṭṭhā papañcārāmassa papañcaratino udāhu nippapañcārāmassa nippapañcaratino’ti? ‘But is that goal for those who enjoy proliferation or for those who enjoy non-proliferation?’ ”But, friend, is that goal for one who delights in diversification or for one who has no delight in diversification?’’

Sammā byākaramānā, bhikkhave, aññatitthiyā paribbājakā evaṁ byākareyyuṁ: Answering rightly, the wanderers would say: Answering rightly, they would answer:

‘nippapañcārāmassāvuso, sā niṭṭhā nippapañcaratino, na sā niṭṭhā papañcārāmassa papañcaratino’ti. ‘It’s for those who enjoy non-proliferation, not for those who enjoy proliferation.’ “Friends, the goal is for one who has no delight in diversification, not for one who delights in diversification.”

Dvemā, bhikkhave, diṭṭhiyo—Mendicants, there are these two views: ‘Bhikkhus, there are these two kinds of views:

bhavadiṭṭhi ca vibhavadiṭṭhi ca. views favoring existence and views favoring nonexistence. the view of being and the view of non-being.

Ye hi keci, bhikkhave, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā bhavadiṭṭhiṁ allīnā bhavadiṭṭhiṁ upagatā bhavadiṭṭhiṁ ajjhositā, vibhavadiṭṭhiyā te paṭiviruddhā. Any ascetics or brahmins who resort to, draw near to, and cling to a view favoring existence will oppose a view favoring nonexistence. Any monks or divines who rely on the view of being, adopt the view of being and accept the view of being, are opposed to the view of non-being.

Ye hi keci, bhikkhave, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā vibhavadiṭṭhiṁ allīnā vibhavadiṭṭhiṁ upagatā vibhavadiṭṭhiṁ ajjhositā, bhavadiṭṭhiyā te paṭiviruddhā. Any ascetics or brahmins who resort to, draw near to, and cling to a view favoring nonexistence will oppose a view favoring existence. Any monks or divines who rely on the view of non-being, adopt the view of non-being and accept the view of non-being, are opposed to the view of being.

Ye hi keci, bhikkhave, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā imāsaṁ dvinnaṁ diṭṭhīnaṁ samudayañca atthaṅgamañca assādañca ādīnavañca nissaraṇañca yathābhūtaṁ nappajānanti, te sarāgā te sadosā te samohā te sataṇhā te saupādānā te aviddasuno te anuruddhappaṭiviruddhā te papañcārāmā papañcaratino; There are some ascetics and brahmins who don’t truly understand these two views’ origin, disappearance, gratification, drawback, and escape. They’re greedy, hateful, delusional, craving, grasping, and ignorant. They favor and oppose, and they enjoy proliferation. Any monks or divines who do not understand as they actually are the origin and the disappearance and the gratification and the danger and the escape in (the case of) these two kinds of views, are affected by lust, affected by hate, affected by delusion, affected by craving, affected by clinging, without vision, given over to favouring and opposing, and who delight in diversification, enjoy diversification;

te na parimuccanti jātiyā jarāya maraṇena sokehi paridevehi dukkhehi domanassehi upāyāsehi; They’re not freed from rebirth, old age, and death, from sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. they are not liberated from birth, ageing and death, from sorrow and lamentation, pain, grief and despair,

‘na parimuccanti dukkhasmā’ti vadāmi. They’re not freed from suffering, I say. they are not liberated from suffering, I say.

Ye ca kho keci, bhikkhave, samaṇā vā brāhmaṇā vā imāsaṁ dvinnaṁ diṭṭhīnaṁ samudayañca atthaṅgamañca assādañca ādīnavañca nissaraṇañca yathābhūtaṁ pajānanti, te vītarāgā te vītadosā te vītamohā te vītataṇhā te anupādānā te viddasuno te ananuruddhaappaṭiviruddhā te nippapañcārāmā nippapañcaratino; There are some ascetics and brahmins who do truly understand these two views’ origin, disappearance, gratification, drawback, and escape. They’re rid of greed, hate, delusion, craving, grasping, and ignorance. They don’t favor and oppose, and they enjoy non-proliferation. Any monks or divines who understand as they actually are the origin and the disappearance, and the gratification and the danger and the escape in (the case of) these two kinds of views, are without lust, without hate, without delusion, without craving, without clinging, with vision, not given over to favouring and opposing, and who have no delight in diversification, no enjoyment in diversification;

te parimuccanti jātiyā jarāya maraṇena sokehi paridevehi dukkhehi domanassehi upāyāsehi; They’re freed from rebirth, old age, and death, from sorrow, lamentation, pain, sadness, and distress. they are liberated from birth, ageing and death, from sorrow and lamentation, pain, grief and despair,

‘parimuccanti dukkhasmā’ti vadāmi. They’re freed from suffering, I say. they are liberated from suffering, I say.

Cattārimāni, bhikkhave, upādānāni. There are these four kinds of grasping. Bhikkhus, there are these four kinds clinging:

Katamāni cattāri? What four?

Kāmupādānaṁ, diṭṭhupādānaṁ, sīlabbatupādānaṁ, attavādupādānaṁ. Grasping at sensual pleasures, views, precepts and observances, and theories of a self. clinging to sensual desires, clinging to wrong views, clinging to rites and rituals and clinging to a self-doctrine.

Santi, bhikkhave, eke samaṇabrāhmaṇā sabbupādānapariññāvādā paṭijānamānā. There are some ascetics and brahmins who claim to propound the complete understanding of all kinds of grasping. When certain monks and divines claim to propound the full knowledge of all kinds of clinging,

Te na sammā sabbupādānapariññaṁ paññapenti—But they don’t correctly describe the complete understanding of all kinds of grasping. they do not completely describe full knowledge of all kinds of clinging.

kāmupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti, na diṭṭhupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti, na sīlabbatupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti, na attavādupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti. They describe the complete understanding of grasping at sensual pleasures, but not views, precepts and observances, and theories of a self. They describe full knowledge of clinging to sensual desires without describing clinging to (wrong) views, clinging to rites and rituals, or clinging to a self-doctrine.

Taṁ kissa hetu? Why is that? Why is that?

Imāni hi te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā tīṇi ṭhānāni yathābhūtaṁ nappajānanti. Because those gentlemen don’t truly understand these three things. Those good monks and divines do not understand these three instances (of clinging) as they actually are.

Tasmā te bhonto samaṇabrāhmaṇā sabbupādānapariññāvādā paṭijānamānā; That’s why they claim to propound the complete understanding of all kinds of grasping, Consequently while these good monks and divines claim to propound full knowledge of all kinds of clinging,

te na sammā sabbupādānapariññaṁ paññapenti—but they don’t really.

kāmupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti, na diṭṭhupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti, na sīlabbatupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti, na attavādupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapenti. they describe (only) full knowledge of clinging to sensual desires without describing clinging to views, clinging to rites and rituals and clinging to a self-doctrine.

Evarūpe kho, bhikkhave, dhammavinaye yo satthari pasādo so na sammaggato akkhāyati; In such a teaching and training, confidence in the Teacher is said to be not rightly placed. ‘Bhikkhus, in such a Dhamma and Discipline as that it is plain that confidence in the Master is not rightly directed,

yo dhamme pasādo so na sammaggato akkhāyati; Likewise, confidence in the teaching, it is plain that confidence in the Dhamma is not rightly directed,

yā sīlesu paripūrakāritā sā na sammaggatā akkhāyati; fulfillment of the precepts, that perfecting of the virtues is not rightly directed,

yā sahadhammikesu piyamanāpatā sā na sammaggatā akkhāyati. and love and affection for those sharing the same path are said to be not rightly placed. and that the dearness and love among those who share the Dhamma are not rightly directed.

Taṁ kissa hetu? Why is that? Why is that?

Evañhetaṁ, bhikkhave, hoti yathā taṁ durakkhāte dhammavinaye duppavedite aniyyānike anupasamasaṁvattanike asammāsambuddhappavedite. It’s because that teaching and training is poorly explained and poorly propounded, not emancipating, not leading to peace, proclaimed by someone who is not a fully awakened Buddha. Because that is how it is when the Dhamma and the Discipline is ill-expounded, ill-proclaimed, leading nowhere, unconducive to peace and proclaimed by one not fully enlightened.

Tathāgato ca kho, bhikkhave, arahaṁ sammāsambuddho sabbupādānapariññāvādo paṭijānamāno sammā sabbupādānapariññaṁ paññapeti—The Realized One, the perfected one, the fully awakened Buddha claims to propound the complete understanding of all kinds of grasping. ‘Bhikkhus, when a Tathāgata, an Arahant and Fully Enlightened One, claims to propound full knowledge of all kinds of clinging, he completely describes all kinds of clinging:

kāmupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapeti, diṭṭhupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapeti, sīlabbatupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapeti, attavādupādānassa pariññaṁ paññapeti. Taṁ kissa hetu? Why is that? Why is that?

Evañhetaṁ, bhikkhave, hoti yathā taṁ svākkhāte dhammavinaye suppavedite niyyānike upasamasaṁvattanike sammāsambuddhappavedite. It’s because that teaching and training is well explained and well propounded, emancipating, leading to peace, proclaimed by a fully awakened Buddha. Because that is how it is when the Dhamma and the Discipline is well-expounded, well-proclaimed, leading onward, conducive to peace, and proclaimed by one fully enlightened.

Ime ca, bhikkhave, cattāro upādānā. Kiṁnidānā kiṁsamudayā kiṁjātikā kiṁpabhavā? What is the source, origin, birthplace, and inception of these four kinds of grasping? ‘Now these four kind of clinging have what as their source, what as their origin, from what are they born, by what are they produced?

Ime cattāro upādānā taṇhānidānā taṇhāsamudayā taṇhājātikā taṇhāpabhavā. Craving. ‘These four kinds of clinging have craving as their source, craving as their origin, they are born from craving, and produced by craving.

Taṇhā cāyaṁ, bhikkhave, kiṁnidānā kiṁsamudayā kiṁjātikā kiṁpabhavā? And what is the source, origin, birthplace, and inception of craving? ‘Craving has what as its source...?

Taṇhā vedanānidānā vedanāsamudayā vedanājātikā vedanāpabhavā. Feeling. ‘Craving has feeling as its source... produced by feeling.

Vedanā cāyaṁ, bhikkhave, kiṁnidānā kiṁsamudayā kiṁjātikā kiṁpabhavā? And what is the source of feeling? ‘Feeling has what as its source...?

Vedanā phassanidānā phassasamudayā phassajātikā phassapabhavā. Contact. ‘Feeling has contact as its source... produced by contact.

Phasso cāyaṁ, bhikkhave, kiṁnidāno kiṁsamudayo kiṁjātiko kiṁpabhavo? And what is the source of contact? ‘Contact has what as its source...?

Phasso saḷāyatananidāno saḷāyatanasamudayo saḷāyatanajātiko saḷāyatanapabhavo. The six sense fields. ‘Contact has the sixfold base as its source... produced by the sixfold base.

Saḷāyatanañcidaṁ, bhikkhave, kiṁnidānaṁ kiṁsamudayaṁ kiṁjātikaṁ kiṁpabhavaṁ? And what is the source of the six sense fields? ‘The sixfold base has what as its source...?

Saḷāyatanaṁ nāmarūpanidānaṁ nāmarūpasamudayaṁ nāmarūpajātikaṁ nāmarūpapabhavaṁ. Name and form. ‘The sixfold base has name-and-form as its source... produced by name-and-form.

Nāmarūpañcidaṁ, bhikkhave, kiṁnidānaṁ kiṁsamudayaṁ kiṁjātikaṁ kiṁpabhavaṁ? And what is the source of name and form? ‘Name-and-form has what as its source...?

Nāmarūpaṁ viññāṇanidānaṁ viññāṇasamudayaṁ viññāṇajātikaṁ viññāṇapabhavaṁ. Consciousness. ‘Name-and-form has consciousness as its source... produced by consciousness.

Viññāṇañcidaṁ, bhikkhave, kiṁnidānaṁ kiṁsamudayaṁ kiṁjātikaṁ kiṁpabhavaṁ? And what is the source of consciousness? ‘Consciousness has what as its source...?

Viññāṇaṁ saṅkhāranidānaṁ saṅkhārasamudayaṁ saṅkhārajātikaṁ saṅkhārapabhavaṁ. Choices. ‘Consciousness has formations as its source... produced by formations.

Saṅkhārā cime, bhikkhave, kiṁnidānā kiṁsamudayā kiṁjātikā kiṁpabhavā? And what is the source of choices? ‘Formations have what as their source...?

Saṅkhārā avijjānidānā avijjāsamudayā avijjājātikā avijjāpabhavā. Ignorance. ‘Formations have ignorance as their source, ignorance as their origin, they are born from ignorance, and produced by ignorance.

Yato ca kho, bhikkhave, bhikkhuno avijjā pahīnā hoti vijjā uppannā, so avijjāvirāgā vijjuppādā neva kāmupādānaṁ upādiyati, na diṭṭhupādānaṁ upādiyati, na sīlabbatupādānaṁ upādiyati, na attavādupādānaṁ upādiyati. When that mendicant has given up ignorance and given rise to knowledge, with the fading away of ignorance and the arising of knowledge they don’t grasp at sensual pleasures, views, precepts and observances, or theories of a self. ‘Bhikkhus, as soon as ignorance is abandoned and true knowledge has arisen in a bhikkhu, then with the fading of ignorance and the arising of true knowledge he no longer clings to sensual desires (as fuel for) clinging, or to (wrong) views (as fuel for) clinging, or to rites and rituals (as fuel for) clinging, or to a self-doctrine (as fuel for) clinging.

Anupādiyaṁ na paritassati, aparitassaṁ paccattaññeva parinibbāyati. Not grasping, they’re not anxious. Not being anxious, they personally become extinguished. When he does not cling, he has no anguish. When he has no anguish, he attains Nibbāna for himself:

‘Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyā’ti pajānātī”ti. They understand: ‘Rebirth is ended, the spiritual journey has been completed, what had to be done has been done, there is nothing further for this place.’” Birth is is exhausted, the life divine has been lived out, what can be done is done, there is no more of this in the beyond.’

Idamavoca bhagavā. That is what the Buddha said. That is what the Blessed One said.

Attamanā te bhikkhū bhagavato bhāsitaṁ abhinandunti. Satisfied, the mendicants approved what the Buddha said. The bhikkhus were satisfied, and they delighted in the Blessed One’s words.

Cūḷasīhanādasuttaṁ niṭṭhitaṁ paṭhamaṁ.
