Sutta Nipāta 4.7 Sutta Nipāta 4.7Anthology of Discourses 4.7

Tissametteyyasutta To Tissa-metteyyaWith Tissametteyya

“Methunamanuyuttassa, “For one given over to sexual intercourse,“When someone indulges in sex,”

(iccāyasmā tisso metteyyo) (Tissa-metteyya)said Venerable Tissametteyya,

Vighātaṁ brūhi mārisa; tell the damage, dear sir;“tell us, good fellow: what trouble befalls them?

Sutvāna tava sāsanaṁ, Having heard your teaching,After hearing your instruction,

Viveke sikkhissāmase”. we’ll train in seclusion.”we shall train in seclusion.”

“Methunamanuyuttassa, “In one given over to sexual intercourse,“When someone indulges in sex,”

(metteyyāti bhagavā) (the Buddha)replied the Buddha,

Mussate vāpi sāsanaṁ; the teaching is muddled“they forget their instructions

Micchā ca paṭipajjati, and he practices wrongly:and go the wrong way—

Etaṁ tasmiṁ anāriyaṁ. This is ignoble in him.that is something ignoble in them.

Eko pubbe caritvāna, Whoever once went alone,Someone who formerly lived alone

methunaṁ yo nisevati; but then resorts to sexual intercourseand then resorts to sex

Yānaṁ bhantaṁva taṁ loke, —like a carriage out of control, in the world—is like a chariot careening off-track;

hīnamāhu puthujjanaṁ. is called vile, a person run-of-the-mill.in the world they call them a low, ordinary person.

Yaso kitti ca yā pubbe, His earlier honor & dignity:Their former fame and reputation

hāyate vāpi tassa sā; lost.also fall away.

Etampi disvā sikkhetha, Seeing this, he should train himselfSeeing this, they’d train

methunaṁ vippahātave. to abandon sexual intercourse.to give up sex.

Saṅkappehi pareto so, Overcome by resolves,Oppressed by thoughts,

kapaṇo viya jhāyati; he broods like a miserable wretch.they brood like a wretch.

Sutvā paresaṁ nigghosaṁ, Hearing the scorn of others,When they hear what others are saying,

maṅku hoti tathāvidho. he’s chagrined.that sort is embarrassed.

Atha satthāni kurute, He makes weapons,Then they lash out with verbal daggers

paravādehi codito; attacked by the words of others.when reproached by others.

Esa khvassa mahāgedho, This, for him, is a great entanglement.This is their great blind spot;

mosavajjaṁ pagāhati. He sinks into lies.they sink to lies.

Paṇḍitoti samaññāto, They thought him wiseThey once were considered astute,

ekacariyaṁ adhiṭṭhito; when he committed himself to the life alone,committed to the solitary life.

Athāpi methune yutto, but now that he’s given to sexual intercourseBut then they indulged in sex,

mandova parikissati. they declare him a dullard.dragged along by desire like a dullard.

Etamādīnavaṁ ñatvā, Knowing these drawbacks, the sageKnowing this danger

Muni pubbāpare idha; here—before & after—in falling from a former state here,

Ekacariyaṁ daḷhaṁ kayirā, stays firm in the life alone;a sage would firmly resolve to wander alone,

Na nisevetha methunaṁ. doesn’t resort to sexual intercourse;and would not resort to sex.

Vivekaññeva sikkhetha, would train himself in seclusion—They’d train themselves only in seclusion;

etaṁ ariyānamuttamaṁ; this, for the noble, is highest.this, for the noble ones, is highest.

Na tena seṭṭho maññetha, He wouldn’t, because of that, suppose himself to be better than others:One who’d not think themselves ‘best’ due to that

sa ve nibbānasantike. He’s on the verge of unbinding.has truly drawn near to extinguishment.

Rittassa munino carato, a sage remote, leading his lifePeople tied to sensual pleasures envy them:

Kāmesu anapekkhino; unconcerned for sensual pleasuresthe isolated, wandering sage

Oghatiṇṇassa pihayanti, —one who’s crossed over the flood—envy him,who has crossed the flood,

Kāmesu gadhitā pajā”ti. people enmeshed in sensual pleasures.”unconcerned for sensual pleasures.”

Tissametteyyasuttaṁ sattamaṁ. To Tissa-metteyya, the seventh.
