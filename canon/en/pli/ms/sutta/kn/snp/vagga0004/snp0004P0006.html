Sutta Nipāta 4.6 Sutta Nipāta 4.6Anthology of Discourses 4.6

Jarāsutta Old AgeOld Age

Appaṁ vata jīvitaṁ idaṁ, How short this life!Short, alas, is this life;

Oraṁ vassasatāpi miyyati; You die this side of a century,you die before a hundred years.

Yo cepi aticca jīvati, but even if you live past,Even if you live a little longer,

Atha kho so jarasāpi miyyati. you die of old age.you still die of old age.

Socanti janā mamāyite, People grieve for what they see as mine,People grieve over belongings,

Na hi santi niccā pariggahā; for nothing possessed is constant, nothing is constantly possessed.yet there is no such thing as permanent possessions.

Vinābhāvasantamevidaṁ, Seeing this separation simply as it is,Separation is a fact of life; when you see this,

Iti disvā nāgāramāvase. one shouldn’t follow the household life.you wouldn’t stay living at home.

Maraṇenapi taṁ pahīyati, At death a person abandonsWhatever a person thinks of as belonging to them,

Yaṁ puriso mamidanti maññati; what he supposes as mine.that too is given up when they die.

Etampi viditvā paṇḍito, Realizing this, the wiseKnowing this, an astute follower of mine

Na mamattāya nametha māmako. shouldn’t incline to be devoted to mine-ness.would not be bent on ownership.

Supinena yathāpi saṅgataṁ, what he met in a dream,Just as, upon awakening, a person does not see

Paṭibuddho puriso na passati; Just as a man doesn’t see on awakening,what they encountered in a dream;

Evampi piyāyitaṁ janaṁ, even so, those he held dear,so too you do not see your loved ones

Petaṁ kālaṅkataṁ na passati. he doesn’t see when they are dead —their time done—.when they are dead and gone.

Diṭṭhāpi sutāpi te janā, When they are seen & heard,You used to see and hear those folk,

Yesaṁ nāmamidaṁ pavuccati; people are called by this name or that,and call them by their name.

Nāmaṁyevāvasissati, but only the name remainsYet the name is all that’s left to tell

Akkheyyaṁ petassa jantuno. to be pointed to when they are dead.of a personage when they’re gone.

Sokapparidevamaccharaṁ, Grief, lamentation, & selfishnessThose who are greedy for belongings

Na jahanti giddhā mamāyite; are not let go by those greedy for mine,don’t give up sorrow, lamentation, and stinginess.

Tasmā munayo pariggahaṁ, so sages, letting go of possessions,That’s why the sages, seers of sanctuary,

Hitvā acariṁsu khemadassino. go about seeing the Secure.left possessions behind and wandered.

Patilīnacarassa bhikkhuno, A monk, living withdrawn,For a mendicant who lives withdrawn,

Bhajamānassa vivittamāsanaṁ; enjoying a dwelling secluded:frequenting a secluded seat,

Sāmaggiyamāhu tassa taṁ, They say it’s congenial for him,they say it’s fitting

Yo attānaṁ bhavane na dassaye. he who wouldn’t, in any realm, display self.to not show themselves in a home.

Sabbattha munī anissito, Everywhere the sage independentThe sage is independent everywhere,

Na piyaṁ kubbati nopi appiyaṁ; makes nothing dear or undear.they don’t form likes or dislikes.

Tasmiṁ paridevamaccharaṁ, In him lamentation & selfishness,Lamentation and stinginess

Paṇṇe vāri yathā na limpati. like water on a white lotus, do not adhere.slip off them like water from a leaf.

Udabindu yathāpi pokkhare, As a water bead on a lotus leaf,Like a droplet slips from a lotus-leaf,

Padume vāri yathā na limpati; as water on a red lily, doesn’t adhere,like water from a lotus flower;

Evaṁ muni nopalimpati, so the sage doesn’t adherethe sage doesn’t cling to that

Yadidaṁ diṭṭhasutaṁ mutesu vā. to the seen, the heard, or the sensed;which is seen or heard or thought.

Dhono na hi tena maññati, for, cleansed, he doesn’t supposeFor the one who is cleansed does not conceive

Yadidaṁ diṭṭhasutaṁ mutesu vā; in connection with the seen, the heard, or the sensed.in terms of things seen, heard, or thought.

Nāññena visuddhimicchati, In no other way does he wish for purity,They do not wish to be purified by another;

Na hi so rajjati no virajjatīti. for he neither takes on passion nor puts it away.they are neither passionate nor growing dispassioned.

Jarāsuttaṁ chaṭṭhaṁ. Old Age, the sixth.
