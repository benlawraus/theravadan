Sutta Nipāta 4.13 Sutta Nipāta 4.13Anthology of Discourses 4.13

Mahābyūhasutta The Great ArrayThe Longer Discourse on Arrayed for Battle

“Ye kecime diṭṭhiparibbasānā, “Those who, dwelling on views,“Regarding those who maintain their own view,

Idameva saccanti vivādayanti; dispute, saying, ‘Only this is true’:disputing that, ‘This is the only truth’:

Sabbeva te nindamanvānayanti, Do they all incur blame,are all of them subject only to criticism,

Atho pasaṁsampi labhanti tattha”. or also earn praise there?”or do some also win praise for that?”

“Appañhi etaṁ na alaṁ samāya, “[The praise:] It’s such a small thing, not at all appeasing.“That is a small thing, insufficient for peace,

Duve vivādassa phalāni brūmi; I speak of two fruits of dispute;these two fruits of dispute, I say.

Etampi disvā na vivādayetha, and seeing this, you shouldn’t dispute—Seeing this, one ought not dispute,

Khemābhipassaṁ avivādabhūmiṁ”. seeing the state where there’s no dispute as secure.”looking for sanctuary in the land of no dispute.

“Yā kācimā sammutiyo puthujjā, “One who knows doesn’t enter intoOne who knows does not get involved

Sabbāva etā na upeti vidvā; any conventions born of the run-of-the mill at all.with any of the many different conventions.

Anūpayo so upayaṁ kimeyya, One who’s uninvolved: when he’s forming no predilectionWhy would the uninvolved get involved,

Diṭṭhe sute khantimakubbamāno”. for what’s seen, for what’s heard, why would he get involved?”since they do not believe based on the seen or the heard?

“Sīluttamā saññamenāhu suddhiṁ, “Those for whom habits are ultimate say that purity’s a matter of self-restraint.Those for whom ethics are the highest speak of purity through self-control;

Vataṁ samādāya upaṭṭhitāse; Undertaking a practice, they devote themselves to it:having undertaken a vow, they stick to it:

Idheva sikkhema athassa suddhiṁ, ‘Let’s train just in this, and then there would be purity.’‘Let us train right here, then we will be pure.’

Bhavūpanītā kusalāvadānā. Those who say they are skilled are [thus] led on to becoming.Professing to be experts, they are led on to future lives.

Sace cuto sīlavatato hoti, But if one of them falls from his habits or practice,If they fall away from their precepts and vows,

Pavedhatī kamma virādhayitvā; he trembles, having failed in his actions.they tremble, having failed in their task.

Pajappatī patthayatī ca suddhiṁ, He hopes for, longs for, purity,They pray and long for purity,

Satthāva hīno pavasaṁ gharamhā. like a caravan leader lost far from home.like one who has lost their caravan while journeying far from home.

Sīlabbataṁ vāpi pahāya sabbaṁ, But one who’s abandoned habits & practices —all—But having given up all precepts and vows,

Kammañca sāvajjanavajjametaṁ; things that are blamable, blameless,and these deeds blameworthy or blameless;

Suddhiṁ asuddhinti apatthayāno, not hoping for ‘pure’ or ‘impure,’not longing for ‘purity’ or ‘impurity’,

Virato care santimanuggahāya. would live in kindness & peace, without taking up peace, detached.live detached, fostering peace.

Tamūpanissāya jigucchitaṁ vā, Dependent on taboos, austerities,Relying on mortification in disgust of sin,

Atha vāpi diṭṭhaṁ va sutaṁ mutaṁ vā; or what’s seen, heard, or sensed,or else on what is seen, heard, or thought,

Uddhaṁsarā suddhimanutthunanti, they speak of purity through wandering further on through becoming & not-,they moan that purification comes through heading upstream,

Avītataṇhāse bhavābhavesu. their craving not gone for becoming & not-.not rid of craving for life after life.

Patthayamānassa hi jappitāni, For one who aspires has longingsFor one who longs there are muttered prayers,

Pavedhitaṁ vāpi pakappitesu; & trembling with regard to theorizings.and trembling too over ideas they have formed.

Cutūpapāto idha yassa natthi, But one who here has no passing away & arising:But one here for whom there is no passing away or reappearing:

Sa kena vedheyya kuhiṁ va jappe”. Why would he tremble? For what would he long?”why would they tremble? For what would they pray?”

“Yamāhu dhammaṁ paramanti eke, “The teaching some say is ‘supreme,’“The very same teaching that some say is ‘ultimate’,

Tameva hīnanti panāhu aññe; is the very one others call ‘lowly.’others say is inferior.

Sacco nu vādo katamo imesaṁ, Which statement is trueWhich of these statements is true,

Sabbeva hīme kusalāvadānā”. when all of these claim to be skilled?”for all of them profess to be experts?”

“Sakañhi dhammaṁ paripuṇṇamāhu, “They say their own teaching is perfect“They say their own teaching is perfect,

Aññassa dhammaṁ pana hīnamāhu; while the doctrine of others is lowly.while the teaching of others is inferior.

Evampi viggayha vivādayanti, Thus quarreling, they dispute,So arguing, they dispute,

Sakaṁ sakaṁ sammutimāhu saccaṁ. each saying his agreed-on opinion is true.each saying their own conventions are the truth.

Parassa ce vambhayitena hīno, If something, because of an opponent’s say-so, were lowly,If someone else’s disparagement makes you inferior,

Na koci dhammesu visesi assa; then none among teachings would be superlative,no one in any teaching would be distinguished.

Puthū hi aññassa vadanti dhammaṁ, for many say that another’s teaching’s inferiorFor each of them says the other’s teaching is lacking,

Nihīnato samhi daḷhaṁ vadānā. when firmly asserting their own.while forcefully advocating their own.

Saddhammapūjāpi nesaṁ tatheva, If their worship of their teaching were true,But if they honor their own teachings

Yathā pasaṁsanti sakāyanāni; in line with the way they praise their own path,just as they praise their own journeys,

Sabbeva vādā tathiyā bhaveyyuṁ, then all doctrines would be true—then all doctrines would be equally valid,

Suddhī hi nesaṁ paccattameva. for purity’s theirs, according to each.and purity would be specific to them.

Na brāhmaṇassa paraneyyamatthi, The brahman has nothing led by another,After judging among the teachings, a brahmin has adopted nothing

Dhammesu niccheyya samuggahītaṁ; when considering what’s grasped among doctrines.that requires interpretation by another.

Tasmā vivādāni upātivatto, Thus he has gone beyond disputes,That’s why they’ve gotten over disputes,

Na hi seṭṭhato passati dhammamaññaṁ. for he doesn’t regard as best the knowledge of a doctrine, any other doctrine.for they see no other doctrine as best.

Jānāmi passāmi tatheva etaṁ, ‘I know. I see. That’s just how it is!’—Saying, ‘I know, I see, that’s how it is’,

Diṭṭhiyā eke paccenti suddhiṁ; Some believe purity’s in terms of view.some believe that purity stems from view.

Addakkhi ce kiñhi tumassa tena, But even if a person has seen, what good does it do him?But if they’ve really seen, what use is that view to them?

Atisitvā aññena vadanti suddhiṁ. Having slipped past, they speak of purity in connection with something or somebody else.Overlooking what matters, they say purity comes from another.

Passaṁ naro dakkhati nāmarūpaṁ, A person, in seeing, sees name-&-form.When a person sees, they see name and form,

Disvāna vā ñassati tānimeva; Having seen, he’ll know only these things.and having seen, they will know just these things.

Kāmaṁ bahuṁ passatu appakaṁ vā, No matter if he’s seen little, a lot,Gladly let them see much or little,

Na hi tena suddhiṁ kusalā vadanti. the skilled don’t say purity’s in connection with that.for those who are skilled say this is no way to purity.

Nivissavādī na hi subbināyo, A person entrenched in his teachings,It’s not easy to educate someone who is dogmatic,

Pakappitaṁ diṭṭhi purakkharāno; preferring a theorized view, isn’t easy to discipline.promoting a view they have formulated.

Yaṁ nissito tattha subhaṁ vadāno, Whatever he depends on he describes it as lovely,Speaking of the beauty in that on which they depend,

Suddhiṁ vado tattha tathaddasā so. says that it’s purity, that there he saw truth.they talk of purity in accord with what they saw there.

Na brāhmaṇo kappamupeti saṅkhā, The brahman, evaluating, doesn’t enter into a theory,The brahmin does not get involved with formulating and calculating;

Na diṭṭhisārī napi ñāṇabandhu; doesn’t follow views, isn’t tied even to knowledge.they’re not followers of views, nor kinsmen of notions.

Ñatvā ca so sammutiyo puthujjā, And on knowing whatever’s conventional, commonplace,Having understood the many different conventions,

Upekkhatī uggahaṇanti maññe. he remains equanimous: ‘That’s what others hold onto.’they look on when others grasp.

Vissajja ganthāni munīdha loke, Having untied the knots here in the world,Having untied the knots here in the world,

Vivādajātesu na vaggasārī; the sage here in the world doesn’t follow a faction when disputes have arisen.the sage takes no side among disputes.

Santo asantesu upekkhako so, At peace among those not at peace, he’s equanimous,Peaceful among the peaceless, equanimous,

Anuggaho uggahaṇanti maññe. doesn’t hold on: ‘That’s what others hold onto.’they don’t grasp when others grasp.

Pubbāsave hitvā nave akubbaṁ, Giving up old effluents, not forming new,Having given up former defilements and not making new ones,

Na chandagū nopi nivissavādī; neither pursuing desire, nor entrenched in his teachings,not swayed by preference, nor a proponent of dogma,

Sa vippamutto diṭṭhigatehi dhīro, he’s totally released from viewpoints, enlightened.that attentive one is released from views,

Na lippati loke anattagarahī. He doesn’t adhere to the world, is without self-rebuke;not clinging to the world, nor reproaching themselves.

Sa sabbadhammesu visenibhūto, is enemy-free with regard to all thingsThey are remote from all things

Yaṁ kiñci diṭṭhaṁ va sutaṁ mutaṁ vā; seen, heard, or sensed.seen, heard, or thought.

Sa pannabhāro muni vippamutto, His burden laid down, the sage totally releasedWith burden put down, the sage is released:

Na kappiyo nūparato na patthiyo”ti. is improper :: is theory-free hasn’t stopped :: isn’t impassioned isn’t worth wanting :: doesn’t desire,” the Blessed One said.not formulating, not abstaining, not longing.”

Mahābyūhasuttaṁ terasamaṁ. The Great Array, the thirteenth.
