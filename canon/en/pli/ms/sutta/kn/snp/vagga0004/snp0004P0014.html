Sutta Nipāta 4.14 Anthology of Discourses 4.14 Sutta Nipāta 4.14

Tuvaṭakasutta Speedy Quickly

“Pucchāmi taṁ ādiccabandhu, “Great seer, I ask you, the Kinsman of the Sun, “I ask the Kinsman of the Sun, the Great Seer,

Vivekaṁ santipadañca mahesi; about seclusion and the state of peace. about seclusion & the state of peace.

Kathaṁ disvā nibbāti bhikkhu, How, having seen, is a mendicant extinguished, Seeing in what way is a monk unbound,

Anupādiyāno lokasmiṁ kiñci”. not grasping anything in this world?” clinging to nothing in the world?”

“Mūlaṁ papañcasaṅkhāya, “They would cut off the idea, ‘I am the thinker’,” “He should put an entire stop to the root of objectification-classifications:

(iti bhagavā) said the Buddha, (the Buddha)

Mantā asmīti sabbamuparundhe; “which is the root of all judgments that emerge from proliferation. ‘I am the thinker.’

Yā kāci taṇhā ajjhattaṁ, Ever mindful, they would train to remove He should train, always mindful,

Tāsaṁ vinayā sadā sato sikkhe. any internal cravings. to subdue any craving inside him.

Yaṁ kiñci dhammamabhijaññā, Regardless of what things they know, Whatever truth he may know,

Ajjhattaṁ atha vāpi bahiddhā; whether internal or external, within or without,

Na tena thāmaṁ kubbetha, they wouldn’t be proud because of that, he shouldn’t, because of it, make himself hardened,

Na hi sā nibbuti sataṁ vuttā. for that is not quenching, say the good. for that isn’t called unbinding by the good.

Seyyo na tena maññeyya, They wouldn’t let that make them conceited, He shouldn’t, because of it, think himself

Nīceyyo atha vāpi sarikkho; thinking themselves better or worse or alike. better, lower, or equal.

Phuṭṭho anekarūpehi, When questioned in many ways, Touched by contact in various ways,

Nātumānaṁ vikappayaṁ tiṭṭhe. they wouldn’t keep justifying themselves. he shouldn’t keep theorizing about self.

Ajjhattamevupasame, A mendicant would be settled internally, Stilled right within,

Na aññato bhikkhu santimeseyya; and not seek peace from another. a monk shouldn’t seek peace from another, from anything else.

Ajjhattaṁ upasantassa, For one at peace inside themselves, For one stilled right within,

Natthi attā kuto nirattā vā. there’s no picking up, whence putting down? there’s nothing embraced, so how rejected?

Majjhe yathā samuddassa, Just as, in the middle of the ocean, As in the middle of the sea

Ūmi no jāyatī ṭhito hoti; no waves arise, it stays still; it is still, with no waves upwelling,

Evaṁ ṭhito anejassa, so too one unstirred is still—so the monk—unperturbed, still—

Ussadaṁ bhikkhu na kareyya kuhiñci”. a mendicant would make no pretension regarding anything.” should not swell himself anywhere.”

“Akittayī vivaṭacakkhu, “He whose eyes are open has explained “He whose eyes are open has described

Sakkhidhammaṁ parissayavinayaṁ; the truth he witnessed, where adversities are removed. the Dhamma he’s witnessed, subduing danger.

Paṭipadaṁ vadehi bhaddante, Please now speak of the practice, sir, Now tell us, sir, the practice:

Pātimokkhaṁ atha vāpi samādhiṁ”. the monastic code and immersion in samādhi.” the Pāṭimokkha & concentration.”

“Cakkhūhi neva lolassa, “With eyes not wanton, “One shouldn’t be careless with his eyes,

Gāmakathāya āvaraye sotaṁ; they’d turn their ears from village gossip. should close his ears to village-talk,

Rase ca nānugijjheyya, They wouldn’t be greedy for flavors, shouldn’t hunger for flavors,

Na ca mamāyetha kiñci lokasmiṁ. nor possessive about anything in the world. or view anything in the world as mine.

Phassena yadā phuṭṭhassa, Though struck by contacts, When touched by contact,

Paridevaṁ bhikkhu na kareyya kuhiñci; a mendicant would not lament at all. he shouldn’t lament,

Bhavañca nābhijappeyya, They wouldn’t pray for another life, shouldn’t covet anywhere any states of becoming,

Bheravesu ca na sampavedheyya. nor tremble in the face of dangers. or tremble at terrors.

Annānamatho pānānaṁ, When they receive food and drink, When gaining food & drink,

Khādanīyānaṁ athopi vatthānaṁ; edibles and clothes, staples & cloth,

Laddhā na sannidhiṁ kayirā, they wouldn’t store them up, he should not make a hoard.

Na ca parittase tāni alabhamāno. nor worry about not getting them. Nor should he be upset when receiving no gains.

Jhāyī na pādalolassa, Meditative, not footloose, Doing jhāna, not footloose,

Virame kukkuccā nappamajjeyya; they’d avoid remorse and not be negligent. he should refrain from restlessness,

Athāsanesu sayanesu, Then a mendicant would stay shouldn’t be heedless,

Appasaddesu bhikkhu vihareyya. in quiet places to sit and rest. should live in a noise-less abode.

Niddaṁ na bahulīkareyya, They wouldn’t take much sleep, Not making much of sleep,

Jāgariyaṁ bhajeyya ātāpī; but, being keen, would apply themselves to wakefulness. ardent, given to wakefulness,

Tandiṁ māyaṁ hassaṁ khiḍḍaṁ, They’d give up sloth, illusion, mirth, and play, he should abandon weariness, deception,

Methunaṁ vippajahe savibhūsaṁ. and sex and ornamentation. laughter, sports, sexual intercourse, & all that goes with it;

Āthabbaṇaṁ supinaṁ lakkhaṇaṁ, They wouldn’t cast Artharvaṇa spells, interpret dreams should not practice casting spells,

No vidahe athopi nakkhattaṁ; or omens, or practice astrology. interpret dreams, physical marks,

Virutañca gabbhakaraṇaṁ, My followers would not study ominous animal cries, the stars, animal cries;

Tikicchaṁ māmako na seveyya. work as a doctor, or treat an impacted fetus. should not be devoted to doing cures or inducing fertility.

Nindāya nappavedheyya, Not shaken by criticism, A monk shouldn’t tremble at blame

Na uṇṇameyya pasaṁsito bhikkhu; a mendicant would not gloat when praised. or grow haughty with praise;

Lobhaṁ saha macchariyena, They’d cast aside greed and stinginess, should dispel stinginess, greed,

Kodhaṁ pesuṇiyañca panudeyya. anger, and slander. divisive speech, anger;

Kayavikkaye na tiṭṭheyya, They’d not stand for buying and selling; shouldn’t buy or sell

Upavādaṁ bhikkhu na kareyya kuhiñci; a mendicant would not speak ill at all. or revile anyone anywhere;

Gāme ca nābhisajjeyya, They wouldn’t linger in the village, shouldn’t linger in villages,

Lābhakamyā janaṁ na lapayeyya. nor cajole people from desire for profit. or flatter people in hope of gains.

Na ca katthitā siyā bhikkhu, A mendicant would be no boaster, A monk shouldn’t boast

Na ca vācaṁ payuttaṁ bhāseyya; nor would they speak suggestively. or speak with ulterior motive,

Pāgabbhiyaṁ na sikkheyya, They wouldn’t train in impudence, shouldn’t train in insolence

Kathaṁ viggāhikaṁ na kathayeyya. nor speak argumentatively. or speak quarrelsome words;

Mosavajje na nīyetha, They wouldn’t be led into lying, shouldn’t engage in lies

Sampajāno saṭhāni na kayirā; nor be deliberately devious. or knowingly cheat;

Atha jīvitena paññāya, And they’d never look down on another shouldn’t despise others for their life, discernment,

Sīlabbatena nāññamatimaññe. because of livelihood, wisdom, or precepts and vows. habits, or practices.

Sutvā rusito bahuṁ vācaṁ, Though provoked from hearing much talk Annoyed on hearing many words

Samaṇānaṁ vā puthujanānaṁ; from ascetics saying all different things, from contemplatives or ordinary people,

Pharusena ne na paṭivajjā, they wouldn’t react harshly, he shouldn’t respond harshly,

Na hi santo paṭisenikaronti. for the virtuous do not retaliate. for those who retaliate aren’t calm.

Etañca dhammamaññāya, Having understood this teaching, Knowing this teaching,

Vicinaṁ bhikkhu sadā sato sikkhe; inquiring, a mendicant would always train mindfully. a monk inquiring should always train in it mindfully.

Santīti nibbutiṁ ñatvā, Knowing quenching as peace, Knowing unbinding as peace,

Sāsane gotamassa na pamajjeyya. they’d not be negligent in Gotama’s bidding. he shouldn’t be heedless of Gotama’s message—

Abhibhū hi so anabhibhūto, For he is the vanquisher, the unvanquished, for he, the Conqueror unconquered,

Sakkhidhammamanītihamadassī; seer of the truth as witness, not by legends—witnessed the Dhamma, not by hearsay, but directly, himself.

Tasmā hi tassa bhagavato sāsane, that’s why, being diligent, they would always train So, heedful, you should always do homage & train

Appamatto sadā namassamanusikkhe”ti. respectfully in the Buddha’s teaching.” in line with that Blessed One’s message,” the Blessed One said.

Tuvaṭakasuttaṁ cuddasamaṁ. Quickly, the fourteenth.
