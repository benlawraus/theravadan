Sutta Nipāta 4.2 Anthology of Discourses 4.2 Sutta Nipāta 4.2

Guhaṭṭhakasutta Eight on the Cave The Cave Octet

Satto guhāyaṁ bahunābhichanno, Trapped in a cave, thickly overspread, Staying attached to the cave, covered heavily over,

Tiṭṭhaṁ naro mohanasmiṁ pagāḷho; sunk in delusion they stay. a person sunk in confusion

Dūre vivekā hi tathāvidho so, That sort is far from seclusion, is far from seclusion—

Kāmā hi loke na hi suppahāyā. for sensual pleasures in the world are not easy to give up. for sensual pleasures, sensual desires, in the world, are not lightly let go.

Icchānidānā bhavasātabaddhā, The chains of desire, the bonds of life’s pleasures Those chained by desire, bound by becoming’s allure,

Te duppamuñcā na hi aññamokkhā; are hard to escape, for one cannot free another. aren’t easily released for there’s no liberation by others.

Pacchā pure vāpi apekkhamānā, Looking to the past or the future, Intent, in front or behind,

Ime va kāme purime va jappaṁ. they pray for these pleasures or former ones. on hunger for sensual pleasures here or before—

Kāmesu giddhā pasutā pamūḷhā, Greedy, fixated, infatuated by sensual pleasures, greedy for sensual pleasures, busy, deluded,

Avadāniyā te visame niviṭṭhā; they are incorrigible, habitually immoral. ungenerous, entrenched in the discordant way,

Dukkhūpanītā paridevayanti, When led to suffering they lament, they—impelled into pain—lament:

Kiṁsū bhavissāma ito cutāse. “What will become of us when we pass away from here?” “What will we be when we pass on from here?”

Tasmā hi sikkhetha idheva jantu, That’s why a personage should train in this life: So a person should train right here-&-now.

Yaṁ kiñci jaññā visamanti loke; should you know that anything in the world is wrong, Whatever you know as discordant in the world,

Na tassa hetū visamaṁ careyya, don’t act wrongly on account of that; don’t, for its sake, act discordantly,

Appañhidaṁ jīvitamāhu dhīrā. for the attentive say this life is short. for that life, the enlightened say, is short.

Passāmi loke pariphandamānaṁ, I see the world’s population floundering, I see them, in the world, floundering around,

Pajaṁ imaṁ taṇhagataṁ bhavesu; given to craving for future lives. people immersed in craving for states of becoming.

Hīnā narā maccumukhe lapanti, Base men wail in the jaws of death, Base people moan in the mouth of death,

Avītataṇhāse bhavābhavesu. not rid of craving for life after life. their craving, for states of becoming & not-, unallayed.

Mamāyite passatha phandamāne, See them flounder over belongings, See them, floundering in their sense of mine,

Maccheva appodake khīṇasote; like fish in puddles of a dried-up stream. like fish in the puddles of a dried-up stream—

Etampi disvā amamo careyya, Seeing this, live unselfishly, and, seeing this, live with no mine,

Bhavesu āsattimakubbamāno. forming no attachment to future lives. not forming attachment for states of becoming.

Ubhosu antesu vineyya chandaṁ, Rid of desire for both ends, Subdue desire for both sides,

Phassaṁ pariññāya anānugiddho; having completely understood contact, free of greed, comprehending sensory contact, with no greed.

Yadattagarahī tadakubbamāno, doing nothing for which they’d blame themselves, Doing nothing for which he himself would rebuke himself,

Na lippatī diṭṭhasutesu dhīro. the attentive don’t cling to the seen and the heard. the enlightened person doesn’t adhere to what’s seen, to what’s heard.

Saññaṁ pariññā vitareyya oghaṁ, Having completely understood perception and having crossed the flood, Comprehending perception, he’d cross over the flood—

Pariggahesu muni nopalitto; the sage, not clinging to possessions, the sage not stuck on possessions.

Abbūḷhasallo caramappamatto, with dart plucked out, living diligently, Then, with arrow removed, living heedfully,

Nāsīsatī lokamimaṁ parañcāti. does not hope for this world or the next. he longs for neither—this world, the next.

Guhaṭṭhakasuttaṁ dutiyaṁ. The Cave Octet, the second.
