Sutta Nipāta 4.15 Anthology of Discourses 4.15 Sutta Nipāta 4.15

Attadaṇḍasutta Taking Up Arms The Rod Embraced

“Attadaṇḍā bhayaṁ jātaṁ, Peril stems from those who take up arms—“When embraced, the rod of violence breeds danger & fear:

janaṁ passatha medhagaṁ; just look how people conflict! Look at people in strife.

Saṁvegaṁ kittayissāmi, I shall extol how I came to be I will tell how I experienced

yathā saṁvijitaṁ mayā. stirred with a sense of urgency. terror:

Phandamānaṁ pajaṁ disvā, I saw this population flounder, Seeing people floundering

macche appodake yathā; like a fish in a little puddle. like fish in small puddles,

Aññamaññehi byāruddhe, Seeing them at odds with each other, competing with one another—

disvā maṁ bhayamāvisi. fear came upon me. as I saw this, fear came into me.

Samantamasāro loko, The world around was volatile, The world was entirely without substance.

disā sabbā sameritā; all directions were in turmoil. All the directions were knocked out of line.

Icchaṁ bhavanamattano, Wanting a home for myself, Wanting a haven for myself,

nāddasāsiṁ anositaṁ. I saw nowhere unsettled. I saw nothing that wasn’t laid claim to.

Osāne tveva byāruddhe, Yet even in their settlement they were at odds—Seeing nothing in the end but competition,

Disvā me aratī ahu; seeing that, I grew uneasy. I felt discontent.

Athettha sallamaddakkhiṁ, Then I saw a dart there, And then I saw an arrow here,

Duddasaṁ hadayanissitaṁ. so hard to see, stuck in the heart. so very hard to see, embedded in the heart.

Yena sallena otiṇṇo, When struck by that dart, Overcome by this arrow

disā sabbā vidhāvati; you run around in all directions. you run in all directions.

Tameva sallamabbuyha, But when that same dart has been plucked out, But simply on pulling it out

na dhāvati na sīdati. you neither run around nor sink down. you don’t run, you don’t sink.

Tattha sikkhānugīyanti, (On that topic, the trainings are recited.) [Here the trainings are recited.]

yāni loke gadhitāni; Whatever attachments there are in the world, Whatever things are tied down in the world,

Na tesu pasuto siyā, don’t pursue them. you shouldn’t be set on them.

nibbijjha sabbaso kāme; Having pierced through sensual pleasures in every way, Having totally penetrated sensual pleasures, sensual passions,

Sikkhe nibbānamattano. train yourself for extinguishment. you should train for your own unbinding.

Sacco siyā appagabbho, Be truthful, not rude, Be truthful, not insolent,

amāyo rittapesuṇo; free of deceit, and rid of slander; not deceptive, remote from divisiveness.

Akkodhano lobhapāpaṁ, without anger, a sage would cross over Without anger, the sage should cross over the evil

vevicchaṁ vitare muni. the evils of greed and avarice. of greed & avarice.

Niddaṁ tandiṁ sahe thīnaṁ, Prevail over sleepiness, sloth, and drowsiness, He should conquer drowsiness, weariness, sloth;

pamādena na saṁvase; don’t abide in negligence, shouldn’t consort with heedlessness,

Atimāne na tiṭṭheyya, A person intent on quenching shouldn’t stand firm in his pride—

nibbānamanaso naro. would not stand for arrogance. the man with his heart set on unbinding.

Mosavajje na nīyetha, Don’t be led into lying, He shouldn’t engage in lying,

rūpe snehaṁ na kubbaye; or get caught up in affection for form. shouldn’t create affection for form,

Mānañca parijāneyya, Completely understand conceit, should fully fathom conceit,

sāhasā virato care. and desist from hasty conduct. and live refraining from impulsiveness;

Purāṇaṁ nābhinandeyya, Don’t relish the old, shouldn’t delight in what’s old,

Nave khantiṁ na kubbaye; or welcome the new. prefer what’s new,

Hiyyamāne na soceyya, Don’t grieve for what is running out, grieve over decline,

Ākāsaṁ na sito siyā. or get attached to things that pull you in. get entangled in what’s dazzling & bright.

Gedhaṁ brūmi mahoghoti, Greed, I say, is the great flood, I call greed a great flood;

Ājavaṁ brūmi jappanaṁ; and longing is the current—hunger, a swift current.

Ārammaṇaṁ pakappanaṁ, the basis, the compulsion, Preoccupations are ripples;

Kāmapaṅko duraccayo. the swamp of sensuality so hard to get past. sensuality, a bog hard to cross over.

Saccā avokkamma muni, The sage never strays from the truth; Not deviating from truth, a sage

Thale tiṭṭhati brāhmaṇo; the brahmin stands on solid ground. stands on high ground: a brahman.

Sabbaṁ so paṭinissajja, Having given up everything, Having relinquished in every way,

Sa ve santoti vuccati. they are said to be at peace. he is said to be at peace;

Sa ve vidvā sa vedagū, They have truly known, they’re a knowledge master, having clearly known, he is an attainer-of-knowledge;

Ñatvā dhammaṁ anissito; understanding the teaching, they are independent. knowing the Dhamma, he’s independent.

Sammā so loke iriyāno, They rightly proceed in the world, Moving rightly through the world,

Na pihetīdha kassaci. not coveting anything here. he doesn’t envy anyone here.

Yodha kāme accatari, One who has crossed over sensuality here, Whoever here has gone beyond sensual passions—

Saṅgaṁ loke duraccayaṁ; the chain in the world so hard to get past, an attachment hard to transcend in the world—

Na so socati nājjheti, grieves not, nor hopes; doesn’t sorrow, doesn’t fret.

Chinnasoto abandhano. they’ve cut the strings, they’re no longer bound. He, his stream cut, is free from bonds.

Yaṁ pubbe taṁ visosehi, What came before, let wither away, Burn up what’s before,

Pacchā te māhu kiñcanaṁ; and after, let there be nothing. and have nothing for after.

Majjhe ce no gahessasi, If you don’t grasp at the middle, If you don’t grasp at what’s in between,

Upasanto carissasi. you will live at peace. you will go about, calm.

Sabbaso nāmarūpasmiṁ, One who has no sense of ownership For whom, in name-&-form, in every way,

Yassa natthi mamāyitaṁ; in the whole realm of name and form, there’s no sense of mine,

Asatā ca na socati, does not grieve for that which is not, and who doesn’t grieve over what is not:

Sa ve loke na jīyati. they suffer no loss in the world. He, in the world, isn’t defeated, suffers no loss.

Yassa natthi idaṁ meti, If you don’t think of anything To whom there doesn’t occur ‘This is mine,’

Paresaṁ vāpi kiñcanaṁ; as belonging to yourself or others, for whom nothing is others’:

Mamattaṁ so asaṁvindaṁ, not finding any possessiveness, He, feeling no sense of mine-ness,

Natthi meti na socati. you won’t grieve, thinking “I don’t have it”. doesn’t grieve at the thought ‘I have nothing.’

Aniṭṭhurī ananugiddho, Not bitter, not fawning, Not harsh, not greedy,

Anejo sabbadhī samo; unstirred, everywhere even; not perturbed, everywhere concordant:

Tamānisaṁsaṁ pabrūmi, when asked about one who is unwavering, This is the reward —I say when asked—

Pucchito avikampinaṁ. I declare that that is the benefit. for those who are free from theorizing.

Anejassa vijānato, For the unstirred who understand, For one unperturbed —who knows—

Natthi kāci nisaṅkhati; there’s no performance of deeds. there’s no accumulating.

Virato so viyārabbhā, Desisting from instigation, Abstaining, unaroused,

Khemaṁ passati sabbadhi. they see sanctuary everywhere. he everywhere sees security.

Na samesu na omesu, A sage doesn’t speak of themselves as being The sage doesn’t speak of himself

Na ussesu vadate muni; among superiors, inferiors, or equals. as among those who are higher, equal, or lower.

Santo so vītamaccharo, Peaceful, rid of stinginess, At peace, free of stinginess,

Nādeti na nirassatī”ti. they are neither ditching nor taking up. he doesn’t embrace, doesn’t reject,” the Blessed One said.

Attadaṇḍasuttaṁ pannarasamaṁ. The Rod Embraced, the fifteenth.
