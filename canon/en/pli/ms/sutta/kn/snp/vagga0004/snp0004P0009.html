Sutta Nipāta 4.9 Anthology of Discourses 4.9 Sutta Nipāta 4.9

Māgaṇḍiyasutta With Māgaṇḍiya To Māgandiya

“Disvāna taṇhaṁ aratiṁ ragañca, “Even when I saw the sirens Craving, Malignancy, and Lust, “On seeing [the daughters of Māra] —Discontent, Craving, & Passion—

Nāhosi chando api methunasmiṁ; I had no desire for sex. there wasn’t even the desire for sex.

Kimevidaṁ muttakarīsapuṇṇaṁ, What is this body full of piss and shit? So what would I want with this, filled with urine & excrement?

Pādāpi naṁ samphusituṁ na icche”. I wouldn’t even want to touch it with my foot.” I wouldn’t want to touch it even with my foot.”

“Etādisañce ratanaṁ na icchasi, “If you do not want a gem such as this, “If you don’t want this gem of a woman, coveted

Nāriṁ narindehi bahūhi patthitaṁ; a lady desired by many lords of men, by many kings,

Diṭṭhigataṁ sīlavataṁ nu jīvitaṁ, then what manner of theory, precepts and vows, livelihood, then for what sort of viewpoint, habit, practice, life,

Bhavūpapattiñca vadesi kīdisaṁ”. and rebirth in a new life do you assert?” attainment of [further] becoming do you argue?”

“Idaṁ vadāmīti na tassa hoti, “After judging among the teachings,” “‘I argue for this’ doesn’t occur to one

(māgaṇḍiyāti bhagavā) said the Buddha to Māgaṇḍiya, (the Buddha)

Dhammesu niccheyya samuggahītaṁ; “none have been adopted thinking, ‘I assert this.’ when considering what’s grasped among doctrines.

Passañca diṭṭhīsu anuggahāya, Seeing views without adopting any, Looking for what is ungrasped with regard to views,

Ajjhattasantiṁ pacinaṁ adassaṁ”. searching, I saw inner peace.” and detecting inner peace, I saw.”

“Vinicchayā yāni pakappitāni, “O sage, you speak of judgments you have formed,” “At any theorized judgments,

(iti māgaṇḍiyo) said Māgaṇḍiya, (Māgandiya)

Te ve munī brūsi anuggahāya; “without having adopted any of those views. Sage, you speak of not grasping.

Ajjhattasantīti yametamatthaṁ, As to that matter of ‘inner peace’—This ‘inner peace’: what does it mean,

Kathaṁ nu dhīrehi paveditaṁ taṁ”. how is that described by the attentive?” how is it, by the enlightened, proclaimed?”

“Na diṭṭhiyā na sutiyā na ñāṇena, “Purity is spoken of not as due to view,” “Not by view, not by learning, not by knowledge,

(māgaṇḍiyāti bhagavā) said the Buddha to Māgaṇḍiya, (the Buddha)

Sīlabbatenāpi na suddhimāha; “learning, knowledge, or precepts and vows; nor by habit or practice does he speak of purity;

Adiṭṭhiyā assutiyā añāṇā, nor without view, learning, Nor is it found by a person through lack of view,

Asīlatā abbatā nopi tena; knowledge, or precepts and vows. of learning, of knowledge, of habit or practice.

Ete ca nissajja anuggahāya, Having relinquished these, not adopting them, Letting these go, without grasping,

Santo anissāya bhavaṁ na jappe”. peaceful, independent, one would not pray for another life.” at peace, independent, one wouldn’t long for becoming.”

“No ce kira diṭṭhiyā na sutiyā na ñāṇena, “It seems purity is spoken of not as due to view,” “Well, if not by view, not by learning, not by knowledge,

(iti māgaṇḍiyo) said Māgaṇḍiya, (Māgandiya)

Sīlabbatenāpi na suddhimāha; “learning, knowledge, or precepts and vows; nor by habit or practice does he speak of purity;

Adiṭṭhiyā assutiyā añāṇā, nor without view, learning, and it isn’t found by a person through lack of view,

Asīlatā abbatā nopi tena; knowledge, or precepts and vows. of learning, of knowledge, of habit or practice,

Maññāmahaṁ momuhameva dhammaṁ, If so, I think this teaching is sheer folly; it seems to me that this teaching’s simply confused,

Diṭṭhiyā eke paccenti suddhiṁ”. some believe that purity comes from view.” for some assume a purity in terms of —by means of— a view.”

“Diṭṭhañca nissāya anupucchamāno, “Continuing to question while relying on a view,” “Asking questions dependent on view,

(māgaṇḍiyāti bhagavā) said the Buddha to Māgaṇḍiya, (the Buddha)

Samuggahītesu pamohamāgā; “you’ve become confused by all you’ve adopted. you’re confused by the things you have grasped.

Ito ca nāddakkhi aṇumpi saññaṁ, From this you’ve not glimpsed the slightest idea, And so you don’t glimpse even the slightest notion [of what I am saying].

Tasmā tuvaṁ momuhato dahāsi. which is why you consider the teaching folly. That’s why you think it’s confused.

Samo visesī uda vā nihīno, If you think that ‘I’m equal, ‘Equal,’ ‘superior,’ or ‘inferior,’

Yo maññati so vivadetha tena; special, or worse’, you’ll dispute about that. whoever supposes this, by that he’d dispute;

Tīsu vidhāsu avikampamāno, Not wavering due to the three discriminations, whereas to one unaffected by these three,

Samo visesīti na tassa hoti. they have no thought, ‘I’m equal or special’. ‘equal,’ ‘superior,’ do not occur.

Saccanti so brāhmaṇo kiṁ vadeyya, Why would that brahmin say, ‘It’s true’, Of what would the brahman say ‘true’

Musāti vā so vivadetha kena; or with whom would they dispute, ‘It’s false’? or ‘false,’ with whom would he dispute?

Yasmiṁ samaṁ visamaṁ vāpi natthi, There is no equal or unequal in them, he in whom ‘equal,’ ‘unequal’ are not—

Sa kena vādaṁ paṭisaṁyujeyya. so who would they take on in debate? with whom would he join in dispute?

Okaṁ pahāya aniketasārī, After leaving their bastion to migrate without abode, Having abandoned home, living free from society,

Gāme akubbaṁ muni santhavāni; a sage doesn’t get close to anyone in town. the sage in villages creates no intimacies.

Kāmehi ritto apurakkharāno, Rid of sensual pleasures, not expecting, Remote from sensuality, not preferring,

Kathaṁ na viggayha janena kayirā. they wouldn’t get in arguments with people. he wouldn’t engage with people in quarrelsome debate.

Yehi vivitto vicareyya loke, A spiritual giant would not take up for debate Those things aloof from which he should go about in the world:

Na tāni uggayha vadeyya nāgo; the things in the world from which they live secluded. The Nāga wouldn’t take them up & argue for them.

Jalambujaṁ kaṇḍakavārijaṁ yathā, As a prickly lotus born in the water As the prickly lotus

Jalena paṅkena canūpalittaṁ; is unsullied by water and mud, is unsmeared by water & mud,

Evaṁ munī santivādo agiddho, so the greedless sage, proponent of peace, so the sage, an exponent of peace, without greed,

Kāme ca loke ca anūpalitto. is unsmeared by sensuality and the world. is unsmeared by sensuality & the world.

Na vedagū diṭṭhiyāyako na mutiyā, A knowledge master does not become conceited An attainer-of-knowledge isn’t measured, made proud,

Sa mānameti na hi tammayo so; due to view or thought, for they are not determined by that. by views or what’s thought, for he isn’t fashioned of them.

Na kammunā nopi sutena neyyo, They’ve no need for deeds or learning, He wouldn’t be led by action, learning;

Anūpanīto sa nivesanesu. they’re not indoctrinated in dogmas. doesn’t reach a conclusion in any entrenchments.

Saññāvirattassa na santi ganthā, There are no ties for one detached from ideas; For one dispassionate toward perception there are no snares;

Paññāvimuttassa na santi mohā; there are no delusions for one freed by wisdom. for one released by discernment, no delusions.

Saññañca diṭṭhiñca ye aggahesuṁ, But those who have adopted ideas and views Those who grasp at perceptions & views

Te ghaṭṭayantā vicaranti loke”ti. wander the world causing conflict.” go about clashing in the world.”

Māgaṇḍiyasuttaṁ navamaṁ. To Māgandiya, the ninth.
