Sutta Nipāta 4.4 Sutta Nipāta 4.4Anthology of Discourses 4.4

Suddhaṭṭhakasutta The Pure OctetEight on the Pure

Passāmi suddhaṁ paramaṁ arogaṁ, “I see the pure, the supreme, free from disease.“I see someone pure, perfectly healthy;

Diṭṭhena saṁsuddhi narassa hoti; It’s in connection with what’s seen that a person’s purity is.”it is vision that grants a person purity.”

Evābhijānaṁ paramanti ñatvā, Understanding thus, having known the “supreme,”Recalling this notion of the ultimate,

Suddhānupassīti pacceti ñāṇaṁ. & remaining focused on purity, one falls back on that knowledge.they believe in the notion that there is one who observes purity.

Diṭṭhena ce suddhi narassa hoti, If it’s in connection with what is seen that a person’s purity is,If a person were granted purity through what is seen,

Ñāṇena vā so pajahāti dukkhaṁ; or if stress is abandoned in connection with knowledge,or if by a notion they could give up suffering,

Aññena so sujjhati sopadhīko, then a person with acquisitions is purified in connection with something else,then one with attachments is purified by another:

Diṭṭhī hi naṁ pāva tathā vadānaṁ. for his view betrays that in the way he asserts it.their view betrays them as one who asserts thus.

Na brāhmaṇo aññato suddhimāha, No brahman says purity comes in connection with anything else—The brahmin speaks not of purity from another

Diṭṭhe sute sīlavate mute vā; in what’s seen, heard, sensed, habits or practices—in terms of what has been seen, heard, or thought; or by precepts or vows.

Puññe ca pāpe ca anūpalitto, unsmeared by merit or evil,They are unsullied in the midst of good and evil,

Attañjaho nayidha pakubbamāno. not creating anything here, he’s let go of what he’d embraced.letting go what was picked up, without creating anything new here.

Purimaṁ pahāya aparaṁ sitāse, Abandoning what’s first, they depend on what’s next.Having let go the last they lay hold of the next;

Ejānugā te na taranti saṅgaṁ; Following perturbation, they don’t cross over the bond.following impulse, they don’t pass the chain.

Te uggahāyanti nirassajanti, They embrace & rejectThey grab on and let loose like a monkey

Kapīva sākhaṁ pamuñcaṁ gahāyaṁ. —like a monkey releasing a branch to seize at another—grabbing and releasing a branch.

Sayaṁ samādāya vatāni jantu, a person undertaking practices on his own,Having undertaken their own vows, a personage

Uccāvacaṁ gacchati saññasatto; goes high & low, latched onto perception.visits various teachers, being attached to perception.

Vidvā ca vedehi samecca dhammaṁ, But having clearly known through vedas, having encountered the Dhamma,One who knows, having comprehended the truth through the knowledges,

Na uccāvacaṁ gacchati bhūripañño. one deeply discerning doesn’t go high & low.does not visit various teachers, being of vast wisdom.

Sa sabbadhammesu visenibhūto, He’s enemy-free with regard to all thingsThey are remote from all things

Yaṁ kiñci diṭṭhaṁ va sutaṁ mutaṁ vā; seen, heard, or sensed.seen, heard, or thought.

Tameva dassiṁ vivaṭaṁ carantaṁ, —one who has seen in this way, who goes around open—Seeing them living openly,

Kenīdha lokasmi vikappayeyya. By whom, with what, should he be pigeonholed here in the world?how could anyone in this world judge them?

Na kappayanti na purekkharonti, They don’t theorize, don’t yearn,They don’t make things up or promote them,

Accantasuddhīti na te vadanti; don’t proclaim “utter purity.”or speak of the uttermost purity.

Ādānaganthaṁ gathitaṁ visajja, Untying the tied-up knot of grasping,After untying the tight knot of grasping

Āsaṁ na kubbanti kuhiñci loke. they don’t form a desire anywhere in the world.they long for nothing in the world.

Sīmātigo brāhmaṇo tassa natthi, The brahman gone beyond territories, has nothing thatThe brahmin has stepped over the perimeter;

Ñatvā va disvā va samuggahītaṁ; —on knowing or seeing—he’s grasped.knowing and seeing, they adopt nothing.

Na rāgarāgī na virāgaratto, Unimpassionate for passion, not impassioned for dis-,Neither in love with passion nor besotted by dispassion,

Tassīdha natthi paramuggahītanti. he has nothing here that he’s grasped as supreme.there is nothing here they adopt as the ultimate.

Suddhaṭṭhakasuttaṁ catutthaṁ. The Pure Octet, the fourth.
