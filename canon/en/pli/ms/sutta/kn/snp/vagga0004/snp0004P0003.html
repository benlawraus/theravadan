Sutta Nipāta 4.3 Sutta Nipāta 4.3Anthology of Discourses 4.3

Duṭṭhaṭṭhakasutta The Corrupted OctetEight on Malice

Vadanti ve duṭṭhamanāpi eke, There are some who dispute corrupted at heart,Some speak with malicious intent,

Athopi ve saccamanā vadanti; and those who dispute their hearts set on truth,while others speak set on truth.

Vādañca jātaṁ muni no upeti, but a sage doesn’t enter a dispute that’s arisen,When disputes come up a sage does not get involved,

Tasmā munī natthi khilo kuhiñci. which is why he has no rigidity anywhere at all.which is why they’ve no barrenness at all.

Sakañhi diṭṭhiṁ kathamaccayeyya, Now, how would one overcome his own views?How can you transcend your own view

Chandānunīto ruciyā niviṭṭho; led on by desire, entrenched in his likes,when you’re led by preference, dogmatic in belief?

Sayaṁ samattāni pakubbamāno, forming his own conclusions,Inventing your own undertakings,

Yathā hi jāneyya tathā vadeyya. He’d dispute in line with the way that he knows.you’d speak according to your notion.

Yo attano sīlavatāni jantu, Whoever, of his own practices, habits,Some personages, unasked, tell others

Anānupuṭṭhova paresa pāva; boasts to others, unasked,of their own precepts and vows.

Anariyadhammaṁ kusalā tamāhu, is, say the skilled, ignoble by nature—They have an ignoble nature, say those who are skilled,

Yo ātumānaṁ sayameva pāva. he who speaks of himself of his own accord.since they speak on themselves of their own accord.

Santo ca bhikkhu abhinibbutatto, But a monk at peace, fully unbound in himself,A mendicant, peaceful, quenched,

Itihanti sīlesu akatthamāno; not boasting of his habits —“That’s how I am”—never boasts “thus am I” of their precepts.

Tamariyadhammaṁ kusalā vadanti, he, say the skilled, is noble by nature—They have a noble nature, say those who are skilled,

Yassussadā natthi kuhiñci loke. he with no vanity anywhere in the world.who have no pretensions about anything in the world.

Pakappitā saṅkhatā yassa dhammā, One whose doctrines, fabricated, formed,For one who formulates and creates teachings,

Purakkhatā santi avīvadātā; given preference, aren’t clean—and promotes them despite their defects,

Yadattani passati ānisaṁsaṁ, when he sees it to his own advantage—if they see an advantage for themselves,

Taṁ nissito kuppapaṭiccasantiṁ. relies on a peace dependent on the provoked.they become dependent on that, relying on unstable peace.

Diṭṭhīnivesā na hi svātivattā, Because entrenchments in views aren’t easily overcomeIt’s not easy to get over dogmatic views

Dhammesu niccheyya samuggahītaṁ; when considering what’s grasped among doctrines,adopted after judging among the teachings.

Tasmā naro tesu nivesanesu, that’s why a person, in light of these very entrenchments,That’s why, among all these dogmas, a person

Nirassatī ādiyatī ca dhammaṁ. embraces or rejects a doctrine.ditches one teaching and takes up another.

Dhonassa hi natthi kuhiñci loke, Now, one who is cleansed has nothing anywhere in the world—The cleansed one has no formulated view

Pakappitā diṭṭhi bhavābhavesu; no theorized view about states of becoming or not-,at all in the world about the different realms.

Māyañca mānañca pahāya dhono, Having abandoned conceit & illusion,Having given up illusion and conceit,

Sa kena gaccheyya anūpayo so. by what means would he go? He isn’t involved,by what path would they go? They are not involved.

Upayo hi dhammesu upeti vādaṁ, for one who’s involved enters into disputes over doctrines.For one who gets involved is embroiled in disputes about teachings—

Anūpayaṁ kena kathaṁ vadeyya; But how—in connection with what—would you argue with one uninvolved?but how to dispute with the uninvolved? About what?

Attā nirattā na hi tassa atthi, He has nothing embraced or rejected,For picking up and putting down is not what they do;

Adhosi so diṭṭhimidheva sabbanti. has sloughed off every view right here—every one.they have shaken off all views in this very life.

Duṭṭhaṭṭhakasuttaṁ tatiyaṁ. The Corrupted Octet, the third.
