Sutta Nipāta 4.16 Anthology of Discourses 4.16 Sutta Nipāta 4.16

Sāriputtasutta With Sāriputta To Sāriputta

“Na me diṭṭho ito pubbe, “Never before have I seen,” “Never before have I seen

(iccāyasmā sāriputto) said Venerable Sāriputta, (Ven. Sāriputta)

Na suto uda kassaci; “or heard from anyone or heard from anyone

Evaṁ vagguvado satthā, about a teacher of such graceful speech, of a teacher with such lovely speech

Tusitā gaṇimāgato. come from Tusita heaven to lead a community. come, together with his following from Tusita heaven,

Sadevakassa lokassa, To all the world with its gods as the One with Eyes

yathā dissati cakkhumā; he appears as a Clear-eyed One who appears to the world with its devas,

Sabbaṁ tamaṁ vinodetvā, who has dispelled all darkness, having dispelled all darkness,

ekova ratimajjhagā. and alone attained to bliss. having arrived at delight all alone.

Taṁ buddhaṁ asitaṁ tādiṁ, On behalf of the many here still bound, To that One Awakened— unentangled, Such,

akuhaṁ gaṇimāgataṁ; I have come seeking with a question un-deceptive, come with his following—

Bahūnamidha baddhānaṁ, to that Buddha, unattached and impartial, I have come desiring a question on behalf of the many

atthi pañhena āgamaṁ. free of deceit, come to lead a community. here who are fettered:

Bhikkhuno vijigucchato, Suppose a mendicant who loathes attachment For a monk disaffected,

bhajato rittamāsanaṁ; frequents a lonely lodging—frequenting a place remote—

Rukkhamūlaṁ susānaṁ vā, the root of a tree, a charnel ground, the root of a tree, a cemetery,

pabbatānaṁ guhāsu vā. on mountains, or in caves. in mountain caves various places to stay—

Uccāvacesu sayanesu, In these many different lodgings, In his various lodgings,

kīvanto tattha bheravā; how many dangers are there how many are the fears there

Yehi bhikkhu na vedheyya, at which a mendicant in their silent lodging at which he shouldn’t tremble

nigghose sayanāsane. ought not tremble? —there in his noiseless abode—

Katī parissayā loke, On their journey to the untrodden place, how many the dangers in the world

gacchato agataṁ disaṁ; how many adversities are there in the world for the monk going the direction he never has gone

Ye bhikkhu abhisambhave, that must they overcome over which he should prevail

pantamhi sayanāsane. in their remote lodging? there in his isolated abode?

Kyāssa byappathayo assu, What ways of speech should they have? What should be the ways of his speech?

kyāssassu idha gocarā; Of what kind is their alms resort? What should be his range there of action?

Kāni sīlabbatānāssu, What precepts and vows What should be a resolute monk’s

pahitattassa bhikkhuno. should a resolute mendicant uphold? habits & practices?

Kaṁ so sikkhaṁ samādāya, Having undertaken what training, Undertaking what training

ekodi nipako sato; unified, alert, and mindful, — mindful, astute, alone —

Kammāro rajatasseva, would they purge their own stains, would he blow away his own impurities

niddhame malamattano”. like a smith smelting silver?” as a silver smith, those in molten silver?”

“Vijigucchamānassa yadidaṁ phāsu, “If one who loathes attachment frequents a lonely lodging,” “what is comfort for one disaffected,

(sāriputtāti bhagavā) said the Buddha to Sāriputta, (the Buddha)

Rittāsanaṁ sayanaṁ sevato ce; “in their search for awakening—as accords with the teaching—if he’s resorting to a place remote,

Sambodhikāmassa yathānudhammaṁ, I shall tell you, as I understand it, desiring self-awakening in line with the Dhamma,

Taṁ te pavakkhāmi yathā pajānaṁ. what is comfortable for them. I will tell you, as one who knows.

Pañcannaṁ dhīro bhayānaṁ na bhāye, An attentive one, a mindful mendicant living on the periphery An enlightened monk shouldn’t fear the five fears:

Bhikkhu sato sapariyantacārī; should not be afraid of five perils: living circumscribed, mindful,

Ḍaṁsādhipātānaṁ sarīsapānaṁ, flies, mosquitoes, reptiles, of horseflies, mosquitoes, snakes,

Manussaphassānaṁ catuppadānaṁ. human contact, or four-legged creatures. human contact, four-footed beings;

Paradhammikānampi na santaseyya, Nor should they fear followers of other teachings, shouldn’t be fazed by those following another’s teaching

Disvāpi tesaṁ bahubheravāni; even having seen the many threats they pose. even on seeing their manifold threats;

Athāparāni abhisambhaveyya, And then one seeking the good should prevail over still other further dangers

Parissayāni kusalānuesī. should overcome any other adversities. as he seeks what is skillful.

Ātaṅkaphassena khudāya phuṭṭho, Afflicted by illness and hunger, Touched by the touch of disease, hunger,

Sītaṁ athuṇhaṁ adhivāsayeyya; they should endure cold and excessive heat. he should endure cold & inordinate heat.

So tehi phuṭṭho bahudhā anoko, Though afflicted by many such things, the homeless one He with no home, in many ways touched by these things,

Viriyaṁ parakkammadaḷhaṁ kareyya. should exert energy, firmly striving. striving, should make firm his persistence.

Theyyaṁ na kāre na musā bhaṇeyya, They must not steal or lie; He shouldn’t commit a theft, shouldn’t speak a lie,

Mettāya phasse tasathāvarāni; and should touch creatures firm or frail with love. should touch with thoughts of goodwill beings firm & infirm.

Yadāvilattaṁ manaso vijaññā, If they notice any clouding of the mind, Conscious of when his mind is stirred up & turbid,

Kaṇhassa pakkhoti vinodayeyya. they should dispel it as Māra’s ally. he should dispel it: ‘It’s on the side of the Dark One.’

Kodhātimānassa vasaṁ na gacche, They must not fall under the sway of anger or arrogance; He shouldn’t come under the sway of anger or pride.

Mūlampi tesaṁ palikhañña tiṭṭhe; having dug them out by the root, they would stand firm. Having dug up their root he would stand firm.

Athappiyaṁ vā pana appiyaṁ vā, Then, faced with likes and dislikes, Then, when prevailing —yes—

Addhā bhavanto abhisambhaveyya. they would surely overcome. he’d prevail over notions of dear & undear.

Paññaṁ purakkhatvā kalyāṇapīti, Putting wisdom in the foremost place, rejoicing in goodness, Deferring to discernment enraptured with what’s admirable,

Vikkhambhaye tāni parissayāni; they would put an end to those adversities. he should overcome these dangers,

Aratiṁ sahetha sayanamhi pante, They’d vanquish discontent in their remote lodging. should conquer discontent in his isolated spot,

Caturo sahetha paridevadhamme. And they’d vanquish the four lamentations: should conquer these four thoughts of lament:

Kiṁsū asissāmi kuva vā asissaṁ, ‘What will I eat? Where will I eat? ‘What will I eat, or where will I eat?

Dukkhaṁ vata settha kvajja sessaṁ; Oh, I slept badly! Where will I sleep?’ How badly I slept. Tonight where will I sleep?’

Ete vitakke paridevaneyye, The trainee, the migrant without abode, These lamenting thoughts

Vinayetha sekho aniketacārī. would dispel these lamentable thoughts. he should subdue— one under training, wandering without home.

Annañca laddhā vasanañca kāle, Receiving food and clothes in due season, Receiving food & cloth at appropriate times,

Mattaṁ so jaññā idha tosanatthaṁ; they would know moderation for the sake of contentment. he should have a sense of enough for the sake of contentment.

So tesu gutto yatacāri gāme, Guarded in these things, walking restrained in the village, Guarded in regard to these things going restrained into a village,

Rusitopi vācaṁ pharusaṁ na vajjā. they wouldn’t speak harshly even when provoked. even when harassed he shouldn’t say a harsh word.

Okkhittacakkhu na ca pādalolo, Eyes downcast, not footloose, With eyes downcast, & not footloose,

Jhānānuyutto bahujāgarassa; devoted to absorption, they’d be very wakeful. committed to jhāna, he should be continually wakeful.

Upekkhamārabbha samāhitatto, Grounded in equanimity, serene, Arousing equanimity, centered within,

Takkāsayaṁ kukkucciyūpachinde. they should cut off worrisome habits of thought. he should cut off any penchant to conjecture or worry.

Cudito vacībhi satimābhinande, A mindful one would welcome reproach, When reprimanded with words, he should—mindful— rejoice;

Sabrahmacārīsu khilaṁ pabhinde; and would dissolve hard-heartedness toward their spiritual companions. should smash any rigidity toward his fellows in the holy life;

Vācaṁ pamuñce kusalaṁ nātivelaṁ, They may utter skillful speech, but not for too long, should utter skillful words that are not untimely;

Janavādadhammāya na cetayeyya. and they shouldn’t provoke people to blame. should give no mind to the gossip people might say.

Athāparaṁ pañca rajāni loke, And there are five more taints in the world, And then there are in the world the five kinds of dust

Yesaṁ satīmā vinayāya sikkhe; for the removal of which the mindful one should train, for whose subduing, mindful, he should train:

Rūpesu saddesu atho rasesu, vanquishing desire for sights, With regard to forms, sounds, tastes,

Gandhesu phassesu sahetha rāgaṁ. sounds, flavors, smells, and touches. smells, & tactile sensations he should conquer passion;

Etesu dhammesu vineyya chandaṁ, Having removed desire for these things, with regard to these things he should subdue his desire.

Bhikkhu satimā suvimuttacitto; a mindful mendicant, their heart well freed, A monk, mindful, his mind well released,

Kālena so sammā dhammaṁ parivīmaṁsamāno, rightly investigating the Dhamma in good time, contemplating the right Dhamma at the right times,

Ekodibhūto vihane tamaṁ so”ti. at one, would banish the darkness.” on coming to oneness should annihilate darkness,” the Blessed One said.

Sāriputtasuttaṁ soḷasamaṁ. To Sāriputta, the sixteenth.

Aṭṭhakavaggo catuttho. The Chapter of Eights, the fourth.

Tassuddānaṁ Its summary:

Kāmaṁ guhañca duṭṭhā ca, Sensuality, the Cave, and the Corrupted,

suddhañca paramā jarā; the Pure, the Supreme, and Old Age;

Metteyyo ca pasūro ca, To Metteyya, and to Pasūra,

māgaṇḍi purābhedanaṁ. To Māgandiya, and Before the Break-up;

Kalahaṁ dve ca byūhāni, Quarrels, and the two Arrays,

punadeva tuvaṭṭakaṁ; and then Quickly;

Attadaṇḍavaraṁ suttaṁ, The excellent Rod Embraced sutta,

therapuṭṭhena soḷasa; and the one Asked by the Elder—sixteen.

Iti etāni suttāni, Thus these suttas,

sabbānaṭṭhakavaggikāti. all belong to the Aṭṭhaka Vagga.
