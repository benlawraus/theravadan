Sutta Nipāta 1.4 Sutta Nipāta 1.4Anthology of Discourses 1.4

Kasibhāradvājasutta Kasibhāradvāja Sutta: To Kasi BhāradvājaWith Bhāradvāja the Farmer

Evaṁ me sutaṁ—I have heard thatSo I have heard.

ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā magadhesu viharati dakkhiṇāgirismiṁ ekanāḷāyaṁ brāhmaṇagāme. on one occasion the Blessed One was staying among the Magadhans in Dakkhiṇāgiri near the brahman village of Ekanāḷā.At one time the Buddha was staying in the land of the Magadhans in the Southern Hills near the brahmin village of Ekanāḷa.

Tena kho pana samayena kasibhāradvājassa brāhmaṇassa pañcamattāni naṅgalasatāni payuttāni honti vappakāle. Now at that time approximately 500 of the brahman Kasi [Plowing] Bhāradvāja’s plows were yoked at the sowing time.Now at that time the brahmin Bhāradvāja the Farmer had harnessed around five hundred plows, it being the season for sowing.

Atha kho bhagavā pubbaṇhasamayaṁ nivāsetvā pattacīvaramādāya yena kasibhāradvājassa brāhmaṇassa kammanto tenupasaṅkami. Then, in the early morning, after adjusting his lower robe and carrying his bowl & outer robe, the Blessed One went to where Kasi Bhāradvāja was working.Then the Buddha robed up in the morning and, taking his bowl and robe, went to where Bhāradvāja the Farmer was working.

Tena kho pana samayena kasibhāradvājassa brāhmaṇassa parivesanā vattati. Now at that time Kasi Bhāradvāja’s food-distribution was underway.Now at that time Bhāradvāja the Farmer was distributing food.

Atha kho bhagavā yena parivesanā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā ekamantaṁ aṭṭhāsi. So the Blessed One went to Kasi Bhāradvāja’s food-distribution and, on arrival, stood to one side.Then the Buddha went to where the distribution was taking place and stood to one side.

Addasā kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṁ piṇḍāya ṭhitaṁ. Kasi Bhāradvāja saw the Blessed One standing for alms,Bhāradvāja the Farmer saw him standing for alms

Disvāna bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: and on seeing him, said to him,and said to him,

“ahaṁ kho, samaṇa, kasāmi ca vapāmi ca; kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjāmi. “I, contemplative, plow & sow. Having plowed & sown, I eat.“I plough and sow, ascetic, and then I eat.

Tvampi, samaṇa, kasassu ca vapassu ca; kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjassū”ti. You, too, contemplative, should plow & sow. Having plowed & sown, you (will) eat.”You too should plough and sow, then you may eat.”

“Ahampi kho, brāhmaṇa, kasāmi ca vapāmi ca; kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjāmī”ti. “I, too, brahman, plow & sow. Having plowed & sown, I eat.”“I too plough and sow, brahmin, and then I eat.”

“Na kho pana mayaṁ passāma bhoto gotamassa yugaṁ vā naṅgalaṁ vā phālaṁ vā pācanaṁ vā balībadde vā. “But, contemplative, we don’t see the Master Gotama’s yoke or plow, plowshare, goad, or oxen,“I don’t see the worthy Gotama with a yoke or plow or plowshare or goad or oxen, yet he says:

Atha ca pana bhavaṁ gotamo evamāha: and yet the Master Gotama says this:

‘ahampi kho, brāhmaṇa, kasāmi ca vapāmi ca; kasitvā ca vapitvā ca bhuñjāmī’”ti. ‘I, too, brahman, plow & sow. Having plowed & sown, I eat.’”‘I too plough and sow, brahmin, and then I eat.’”

Atha kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo bhagavantaṁ gāthāya ajjhabhāsi: Then Kasi Bhāradvāja addressed the Blessed One with a verse:Then Bhāradvāja the Farmer addressed the Buddha in verse:

“Kassako paṭijānāsi, “You claim to be a plowman,“You claim to be a farmer,

na ca passāma te kasiṁ; but we don’t see your plowing.but I don’t see you farming.

Kasiṁ no pucchito brūhi, Being asked, tell us about your plowingTell me your farming when asked,

yathā jānemu te kasiṁ”. so that we may know your plowing.”so I can recognize your farming.”

“Saddhā bījaṁ tapo vuṭṭhi, “Conviction’s my seed, austerity my rain,“Faith is my seed, fervor my rain,

paññā me yuganaṅgalaṁ; discernment my yoke & plow,and wisdom is my yoke and plough.

Hirī īsā mano yottaṁ, shame my pole, mind my yoke-tie,Conscience is my draft-pole, mind my strap,

sati me phālapācanaṁ. mindfulness my plowshare & goad.mindfulness my plowshare and goad.

Kāyagutto vacīgutto, Guarded in body, guarded in speech,Guarded in body and speech,

āhāre udare yato; restrained in terms of belly & food,I restrict my intake of food.

Saccaṁ karomi niddānaṁ, I make truth a weeding-hook,I use truth as my scythe,

soraccaṁ me pamocanaṁ. and composure my unyoking.and gentleness is my release.

Vīriyaṁ me dhuradhorayhaṁ, Persistence, my beast of burden,Energy is my harnessed beast,

yogakkhemādhivāhanaṁ; bearing me toward rest from the yoke,transporting me to sanctuary from the yoke.

Gacchati anivattantaṁ, takes me, without turning back,Without turning back, it goes

yattha gantvā na socati. to where, having gone, one doesn’t grieve.to where there is no sorrow.

Evamesā kasī kaṭṭhā, That’s how my plowing is plowed.That’s how to do the farming

Sā hoti amatapphalā; It has as its fruit the deathless.that has freedom from death as its fruit.

Etaṁ kasiṁ kasitvāna, Having plowed this plowing,When you finish this farming

Sabbadukkhā pamuccatī”ti. one is unyoked from all suffering & stress.”you’re released from all suffering.”

Atha kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo mahatiyā kaṁsapātiyā pāyasaṁ vaḍḍhetvā bhagavato upanāmesi: Then Kasi Bhāradvāja, having heaped up milk-rice in a large bronze serving bowl, offered it to the Blessed One, [saying,]Then Bhāradvāja the Farmer poured milk-rice in a large bronze cup and presented it to the Buddha:

“bhuñjatu bhavaṁ gotamo pāyasaṁ. “May Master Gotama eat [this] milk-rice.“May the worthy Gotama eat the milk-rice!

Kassako bhavaṁ; The master is a plowman,The worthy one is a farmer.

yañhi bhavaṁ gotamo amatapphalaṁ kasiṁ kasatī”ti. for the Master Gotama plows the plowing that has as its fruit the deathless.”For the worthy Gotama does the farming that has freedom from death as its fruit.”

“Gāthābhigītaṁ me abhojaneyyaṁ, “What’s been chanted over with verses shouldn’t be eaten by me.“Food enchanted by a verse isn’t fit for me to eat.

Sampassataṁ brāhmaṇa nesa dhammo; That’s not the nature, brahman, of one who’s seen rightly.That’s not the principle of those who see, brahmin.

Gāthābhigītaṁ panudanti buddhā, What’s been chanted over with verses Awakened Ones reject.The Buddhas cast aside things enchanted with verses.

Dhamme satī brāhmaṇa vuttiresā. That being their Dhamma, brahman, this is their way of life.Since there is such a principle, brahmin, that’s how they live.

Aññena ca kevalinaṁ mahesiṁ, A fully-perfected great seer,Serve with other food and drink

Khīṇāsavaṁ kukkuccavūpasantaṁ; his effluents ended, his anxiety stilled—the consummate one, the great seer,

Annena pānena upaṭṭhahassu, serve him with other food & drink,with defilements ended and remorse stilled.

Khettaṁ hi taṁ puññapekkhassa hotī”ti. for that is the field for one looking for merit.”For he is the field for the seeker of merit.”

“Atha kassa cāhaṁ, bho gotama, imaṁ pāyasaṁ dammī”ti? “Then to whom, Master Gotama, should I give this milk-rice?”“Then, worthy Gotama, to whom should I give the milk-rice?”

“Na khvāhaṁ taṁ, brāhmaṇa, passāmi sadevake loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya, yassa so pāyaso bhutto sammā pariṇāmaṁ gaccheyya, aññatra tathāgatassa vā tathāgatasāvakassa vā. “Brahman, I don’t see that person in this world—with its devas, Māras, & Brahmās, in this generation with its contemplatives & brahmans, its royalty & commonfolk—by whom this milk-rice, having been eaten, would be rightly digested, aside from a Tathāgata or a Tathāgata’s disciple.“Brahmin, I don’t see anyone in this world—with its gods, Māras, and divinities, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, its gods and humans—who can properly digest this milk-rice, except the Realized One or one of his disciples.

Tena hi tvaṁ, brāhmaṇa, taṁ pāyasaṁ appaharite vā chaḍḍehi appāṇake vā udake opilāpehī”ti. In that case, brahman, throw the milk-rice away in a place without vegetation, or dump it in water with no living beings.”Well then, brahmin, throw out the milk-rice where there is little that grows, or drop it into water that has no living creatures.”

Atha kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo taṁ pāyasaṁ appāṇake udake opilāpesi. So Kasi Bhāradvāja dumped the milk-rice in water with no living beings.So Bhāradvāja the Farmer dropped the milk-rice in water that had no living creatures.

Atha kho so pāyaso udake pakkhitto cicciṭāyati ciṭiciṭāyati sandhūpāyati sampadhūpāyati. And the milk-rice, when dropped in the water, hissed & sizzled, seethed & steamed.And when the milk-rice was placed in the water, it sizzled and hissed, steaming and fuming.

Seyyathāpi nāma phālo divasaṁ santatto udake pakkhitto cicciṭāyati ciṭiciṭāyati sandhūpāyati sampadhūpāyati; Just as an iron ball heated all day, when tossed in the water, hisses & sizzles, seethes & steams,Suppose there was an iron cauldron that had been heated all day. If you placed it in the water, it would sizzle and hiss, steaming and fuming.

evamevaṁ so pāyaso udake pakkhitto cicciṭāyati ciṭiciṭāyati sandhūpāyati sampadhūpāyati. in the same way the milk-rice, when dropped in the water, hissed & sizzled, seethed & steamed.In the same way, when the milk-rice was placed in the water, it sizzled and hissed, steaming and fuming.

Atha kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo saṁviggo lomahaṭṭhajāto yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavato pādesu sirasā nipatitvā bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: Then Kasi Bhāradvāja—in awe, his hair standing on end—went to the Blessed One and, on arrival, throwing himself down with his head at the Blessed One’s feet, said to him,Then Bhāradvāja the Farmer, shocked and awestruck, went up to the Buddha, bowed down with his head to the Buddha’s feet, and said,

“abhikkantaṁ, bho gotama, abhikkantaṁ, bho gotama. “Magnificent, Master Gotama! Magnificent!“Excellent, worthy Gotama! Excellent!

Seyyathāpi, bho gotama, nikkujjitaṁ vā ukkujjeyya, paṭicchannaṁ vā vivareyya, mūḷhassa vā maggaṁ ācikkheyya, andhakāre vā telapajjotaṁ dhāreyya, ‘cakkhumanto rūpāni dakkhantī’ti; evamevaṁ bhotā gotamena anekapariyāyena dhammo pakāsito. Just as if he were to place upright what was overturned, to reveal what was hidden, to show the way to one who was lost, or to carry a lamp into the dark so that those with eyes could see forms, in the same way has Master Gotama—through many lines of reasoning—made the Dhamma clear.As if he were righting the overturned, or revealing the hidden, or pointing out the path to the lost, or lighting a lamp in the dark so people with clear eyes can see what’s there, worthy Gotama has made the teaching clear in many ways.

Esāhaṁ bhavantaṁ gotamaṁ saraṇaṁ gacchāmi dhammañca bhikkhusaṅghañca, I go to Master Gotama for refuge, to the Dhamma, & to the Saṅgha of monks. May Master Gotama remember me as a lay follower who has gone for refuge from this day forward, for life.I go for refuge to the worthy Gotama, to the teaching, and to the mendicant Saṅgha.

labheyyāhaṁ bhoto gotamassa santike pabbajjaṁ, labheyyaṁ upasampadan”ti. Let me obtain the Going-forth in Master Gotama’s presence, let me obtain Acceptance (into the Bhikkhu Saṅgha).”May I receive the going forth, the ordination in the worthy Gotama’s presence?”

Alattha kho kasibhāradvājo brāhmaṇo bhagavato santike pabbajjaṁ, alattha upasampadaṁ. Then the brahman Kasi Bhāradvāja obtained the Going-forth in the Blessed One’s presence, he obtained Acceptance.And Bhāradvāja the Farmer received the going forth, the ordination in the Buddha’s presence.

Acirūpasampanno kho panāyasmā bhāradvājo eko vūpakaṭṭho appamatto ātāpī pahitatto viharanto nacirasseva—yassatthāya kulaputtā sammadeva agārasmā anagāriyaṁ pabbajanti, tadanuttaraṁ—brahmacariyapariyosānaṁ diṭṭheva dhamme sayaṁ abhiññā sacchikatvā upasampajja vihāsi. And not long after his Acceptance—dwelling alone, secluded, heedful, ardent, & resolute—he in no long time reached & remained in the supreme goal of the holy life, for which clansmen rightly go forth from home into homelessness, knowing & realizing it for himself in the here-&-now.Not long after his ordination, Venerable Bhāradvāja, living alone, withdrawn, diligent, keen, and resolute, soon realized the supreme culmination of the spiritual path in this very life. He lived having achieved with his own insight the goal for which gentlemen rightly go forth from the lay life to homelessness.

“Khīṇā jāti, vusitaṁ brahmacariyaṁ, kataṁ karaṇīyaṁ, nāparaṁ itthattāyā”ti abbhaññāsi. He knew: “Birth is ended, the holy life fulfilled, the task done. There is nothing further for the sake of this world.”He understood: “Rebirth is ended; the spiritual journey has been completed; what had to be done has been done; there is nothing further for this place.”

Aññataro ca panāyasmā bhāradvājo arahataṁ ahosīti. And so Ven. Bhāradvāja became another one of the arahants.And Venerable Bhāradvāja became one of the perfected.

Kasibhāradvājasuttaṁ catutthaṁ. The Kasibhāradvāja Sutta, the fourth.
