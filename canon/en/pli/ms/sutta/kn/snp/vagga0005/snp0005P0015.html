Sutta Nipāta 5.15 Sutta Nipāta 5.14Anthology of Discourses 5.15

Posālamāṇavapucchā Posāla’s QuestionThe Question of Posāla

“Yo atītaṁ ādisati, “To one who reveals the past“To the one who reveals the past,”

(iccāyasmā posālo) (Posāla)said Venerable Posāla,

Anejo chinnasaṁsayo; —unperturbed, his doubts cut through—“who is imperturbable, with doubts cut off,

Pāraguṁ sabbadhammānaṁ, who has gone to the beyond of all phenomena,and who has gone beyond all things,

Atthi pañhena āgamaṁ. I’ve come with a desire for a question.I have come seeking with a question.

Vibhūtarūpasaññissa, of one devoid of perception of forms,Consider one who perceives that form has vanished,

sabbakāyappahāyino; who has abandoned all the body, every body,who has entirely given up the body,

Ajjhattañca bahiddhā ca, who sees, within & without,and who sees nothing at all

natthi kiñcīti passato; ‘There is nothing’:internally and externally.

Ñāṇaṁ sakkānupucchāmi, I ask the Sakyan about the knowledgeI ask the Sakyan about knowledge for them;

kathaṁ neyyo tathāvidho”. How is he to be led further on?”how should that sort be guided?”

“Viññāṇaṭṭhitiyo sabbā, “The Tathāgata, knowing directly“The Realized One directly knows,”

(posālāti bhagavā) (the Buddha)said the Buddha,

Abhijānaṁ tathāgato; all stations of consciousness,“all planes of consciousness.

Tiṭṭhantamenaṁ jānāti, knows for one stationed in themAnd he knows this one who remains,

Vimuttaṁ tapparāyaṇaṁ. release & the steps leading there.committed to that as their final goal.

Ākiñcaññasambhavaṁ ñatvā, Knowing directly the origin of nothingnessUnderstanding that desire for rebirth

Nandī saṁyojanaṁ iti; to be the fetter of delight,in the dimension of nothingness is a fetter,

Evametaṁ abhiññāya, one then sees thereand directly knowing what this really means,

Tato tattha vipassati; clearly.one then discerns that matter clearly.

Etaṁ ñāṇaṁ tathaṁ tassa, That’s his genuine knowledge—That is their knowledge of truth,

Brāhmaṇassa vusīmato”ti. the brahman who has lived to fulfillment.”the brahmin who has lived the life.”

Posālamāṇavapucchā cuddasamā. Posāla’s Questions, the fourteenth.
