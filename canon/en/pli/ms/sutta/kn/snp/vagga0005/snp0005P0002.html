Sutta Nipāta 5.2 Anthology of Discourses 5.2 Sutta Nipāta 5.2

Ajitamāṇavapucchā The Questions of Ajita Ajita’s Questions

“Kenassu nivuto loko, “By what is the world shrouded?” “With what is the world shrouded?

(iccāyasmā ajito) said Venerable Ajita. (Ajita)

Kenassu nappakāsati; “Why does it not shine? Because of what doesn’t it shine?

Kissābhilepanaṁ brūsi, Tell me, what is its sullying? With what is it smeared? Tell me.

Kiṁsu tassa mahabbhayaṁ”. What is its greatest fear?” What is its great danger & fear?”

“Avijjāya nivuto loko, “The world is shrouded in ignorance.” “With ignorance the world is shrouded.

(ajitāti bhagavā) replied the Buddha. (the Buddha)

Vevicchā pamādā nappakāsati; “Avarice and negligence make it not shine. Because of stinginess, heedlessness, it doesn’t shine.

Jappābhilepanaṁ brūmi, Muttered prayer is its sullying, I say. With longing it’s smeared—I tell you.

Dukkhamassa mahabbhayaṁ”. Suffering is its greatest fear.” Suffering-stress: its great danger & fear.”

“Savanti sabbadhi sotā, “The streams flow everywhere,” “They flow every which way, the streams.

(iccāyasmā ajito) said Venerable Ajita. (Ajita)

Sotānaṁ kiṁ nivāraṇaṁ; “What is there to block them? What is their blocking,

Sotānaṁ saṁvaraṁ brūhi, And tell me the restraint of streams—what their restraint—tell me—

Kena sotā pidhiyyare”. by what are they locked out?” with what are they finally stopped?”

“Yāni sotāni lokasmiṁ, “The streams in the world,” “Whatever streams there are in the world:

(ajitāti bhagavā) replied the Buddha, (the Buddha)

Sati tesaṁ nivāraṇaṁ; “are blocked by mindfulness. Their blocking is mindfulness,

Sotānaṁ saṁvaraṁ brūmi, I tell you the restraint of streams—mindfulness is their restraint—I tell you—

Paññāyete pidhiyyare”. they are locked out by wisdom.” with discernment they’re finally stopped.”

“Paññā ceva sati yañca, “That wisdom and mindfulness,” “Discernment & mindfulness,

(iccāyasmā ajito) said Venerable Ajita, (Ajita)

Nāmarūpañca mārisa; “and that which is name and form, good fellow; name-&-form, dear sir:

Etaṁ me puṭṭho pabrūhi, when questioned, please tell me of this: Tell me, when asked this,

Katthetaṁ uparujjhati”. where does this all cease?” where are they brought to a halt?”

“Yametaṁ pañhaṁ apucchi, “This question which you have asked, “This question you’ve asked, Ajita,

ajita taṁ vadāmi te; I shall answer you, Ajita. I’ll answer it for you—

Yattha nāmañca rūpañca, Where name and form where name-&-form

asesaṁ uparujjhati; cease with no residue left behind—are brought to a halt without trace:

Viññāṇassa nirodhena, with the cessation of consciousness, With the cessation of consciousness

etthetaṁ uparujjhati”. here they cease.” they’re here brought to a halt.”

“Ye ca saṅkhātadhammāse, “There are those who have appraised the teaching, “Those here who have fathomed the Dhamma,

ye ca sekhā puthū idha; and many kinds of trainees here. those who are learners, those who are run-of-the-mill:

Tesaṁ me nipako iriyaṁ, Now you, being alert, are asked When you, dear sir, astute,

puṭṭho pabrūhi mārisa”. to tell me their behavior, good fellow.” are asked this, tell me their manner of life.”

“Kāmesu nābhigijjheyya, “Not greedy for sensual pleasures, “He should not hanker for sensual pleasures,

Manasānāvilo siyā; their mind would be unclouded. should be limpid in mind.

Kusalo sabbadhammānaṁ, Skilled in all things, Skilled in all mental qualities,

Sato bhikkhu paribbaje”ti. a mendicant would wander mindful.” he, the monk, should wander mindfully.”

Ajitamāṇavapucchā paṭhamā. Ajita’s Questions, the first.
