Sutta Nipāta 5.4 Anthology of Discourses 5.4 Sutta Nipāta 5.4

Puṇṇakamāṇavapucchā The Questions of Puṇṇaka Puṇṇaka’s Questions

“Anejaṁ mūladassāviṁ, “To the imperturbable, the seer of the root,” “To the one unperturbed, who has seen the roots,

(iccāyasmā puṇṇako) said Venerable Puṇṇaka, (Puṇṇaka)

Atthi pañhena āgamaṁ; “I have come seeking with a question. I have come with a question.

Kiṁ nissitā isayo manujā, On what grounds have seers and men, Because of what have many human seers

Khattiyā brāhmaṇā devatānaṁ; aristocrats and brahmins here in the world —noble warriors, brahmans—

Yaññamakappayiṁsu puthūdha loke, performed so many different sacrifices to the gods? offered sacrifices to devas here in the world?

Pucchāmi taṁ bhagavā brūhi me taṁ”. I ask you, Blessed One; please tell me this.” I ask you, Blessed One. Please tell me.”

“Ye kecime isayo manujā, “Whatever seers and men,” “Those many human seers

(puṇṇakāti bhagavā) replied the Buddha, (the Buddha)

Khattiyā brāhmaṇā devatānaṁ; “aristocrats and brahmins here in the world —noble warriors, brahmans—

Yaññamakappayiṁsu puthūdha loke, have performed so many different sacrifices to the gods: who have offered sacrifices to devas here in the world, Puṇṇaka,

Āsīsamānā puṇṇaka itthattaṁ; all performed sacrifices bound to old age, hoping for more of this state of being,

Jaraṁ sitā yaññamakappayiṁsu”. longing for this place.” offered their sacrifices because of aging.”

“Ye kecime isayo manujā, “As to those seers and men,” “These many human seers

(iccāyasmā puṇṇako) said Venerable Puṇṇaka, (Puṇṇaka)

Khattiyā brāhmaṇā devatānaṁ; “and aristocrats and brahmins here in the world —noble warriors, brahmans—

Yaññamakappayiṁsu puthūdha loke, who have performed so many different sacrifices to the gods: who have offered sacrifices to devas here in the world:

Kaccissu te bhagavā yaññapathe appamattā; being diligent in the technique of sacrifice, Have they, Blessed One, heeding the path of sacrifice, dear sir,

Atāruṁ jātiñca jarañca mārisa, have they crossed over rebirth and old age, good fellow? crossed over birth & aging?

Pucchāmi taṁ bhagavā brūhi me taṁ”. I ask you, Blessed One; please tell me this.” I ask you, Blessed One. Please tell me.”

“Āsīsanti thomayanti abhijappanti juhanti, “Hoping, invoking, praying, and worshiping,” “They hoped for, liked, longed for, so they sacrificed—

(puṇṇakāti bhagavā) replied the Buddha, (the Buddha)

Kāmābhijappanti paṭicca lābhaṁ; “they pray for pleasure derived from profit. they longed for sensuality, dependent on gain.

Te yājayogā bhavarāgarattā, Devoted to sacrifice, besotted by rebirth, I tell you: Those who take on the yoke of sacrifice, impassioned with the passion for becoming,

Nātariṁsu jātijaranti brūmi”. they’ve not crossed over rebirth and old age, I declare.” have not crossed over birth & aging.”

“Te ce nātariṁsu yājayogā, “If those devoted to sacrifice,” “If those who take on the yoke of sacrifice

(iccāyasmā puṇṇako) said Venerable Puṇṇaka, (Puṇṇaka)

Yaññehi jātiñca jarañca mārisa; “have not, by sacrificing, crossed over rebirth and old age, haven’t crossed over birth & aging,

Atha ko carahi devamanussaloke, who then in the world of gods and humans then who in the world, dear sir, of beings divine & human

Atāri jātiñca jarañca mārisa; has crossed over rebirth and old age, good fellow? has crossed over birth & aging?

Pucchāmi taṁ bhagavā brūhi me taṁ”. I ask you, Blessed One; please tell me this.” I ask you, Blessed One. Please tell me.”

“Saṅkhāya lokasmi paroparāni, “Having appraised the world high and low,” “He who has fathomed the high & low in the world,

(puṇṇakāti bhagavā) replied the Buddha, (the Buddha)

Yassiñjitaṁ natthi kuhiñci loke; “there is nothing in the world that disturbs them. for whom there is nothing perturbing in the world—

Santo vidhūmo anīgho nirāso, Peaceful, unclouded, untroubled, with no need for hope—evaporated, undesiring, untroubled, at peace—

Atāri so jātijaranti brūmī”ti. they’ve crossed over rebirth and old age, I declare.” he, I tell you, has crossed over birth & aging.”

Puṇṇakamāṇavapucchā tatiyā. Puṇṇaka’s Questions, the third.
