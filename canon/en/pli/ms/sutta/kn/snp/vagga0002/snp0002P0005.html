Sutta Nipāta 2.5 Sutta Nipāta 2.5Anthology of Discourses 2.5

Sūcilomasutta SucilomaWith Spiky

Evaṁ me sutaṁ—I have heard thatSo I have heard.

ekaṁ samayaṁ bhagavā gayāyaṁ viharati ṭaṅkitamañce sūcilomassa yakkhassa bhavane. on one occasion the Blessed One was staying in Gayā at Ṭaṅkitamañca, the haunt of Suciloma the yakkha.At one time the Buddha was staying near Gayā on the cut-stone ledge in the haunt of Spiky the native spirit.

Tena kho pana samayena kharo ca yakkho sūcilomo ca yakkho bhagavato avidūre atikkamanti. And on that occasion Khara the yakkha and Suciloma the yakkha passed by not far from the Blessed One.Now at that time the native spirits Shaggy and Spiky were passing by not far from the Buddha.

Atha kho kharo yakkho sūcilomaṁ yakkhaṁ etadavoca: Then Khara the yakkha said to Suciloma the yakkha:So Shaggy said to Spiky,

“eso samaṇo”ti. “That’s a contemplative.”“That’s an ascetic.”

“Neso samaṇo, samaṇako eso. “That’s not a contemplative. That’s a fake contemplative.“That’s no ascetic, he’s a faker!

Yāvāhaṁ jānāmi yadi vā so samaṇo, yadi vā so samaṇako”ti. I’ll find out whether that’s a contemplative or a fake contemplative.”I’ll soon find out whether he’s an ascetic or a faker.”

Atha kho sūcilomo yakkho yena bhagavā tenupasaṅkami; upasaṅkamitvā bhagavato kāyaṁ upanāmesi. So Suciloma the yakkha approached the Blessed One and on arrival leaned his body up against the Blessed One.Then Spiky went up to the Buddha and leaned up against his body,

Atha kho bhagavā kāyaṁ apanāmesi. The Blessed One leaned his body away.but the Buddha pulled away.

Atha kho sūcilomo yakkho bhagavantaṁ etadavoca: So Suciloma the yakkha said to the Blessed One:Then Spiky said to the Buddha,

“bhāyasi maṁ, samaṇā”ti? “Are you afraid of me, contemplative?”“Are you afraid, ascetic?”

“Na khvāhaṁ taṁ, āvuso, bhāyāmi; “No, I’m not afraid of you, friend,“No, respectable sir, I’m not afraid.

api ca te samphasso pāpako”ti. just that your touch is evil.”But your touch is nasty.”

“Pañhaṁ taṁ, samaṇa, pucchissāmi. “I will ask you a question, contemplative.“I will ask you a question, ascetic.

Sace me na byākarissasi, cittaṁ vā te khipissāmi, hadayaṁ vā te phālessāmi, pādesu vā gahetvā pāragaṅgāya khipissāmī”ti. If you can’t answer me, I will hurl out your mind or rip open your heart or, grabbing you by the feet, hurl you across the Ganges.”If you don’t answer me, I’ll drive you insane, or explode your heart, or grab you by the feet and throw you to the far shore of the Ganges!”

“Na khvāhaṁ taṁ, āvuso, passāmi sadevake loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya yo me cittaṁ vā khipeyya hadayaṁ vā phāleyya pādesu vā gahetvā pāragaṅgāya khipeyya. “My friend, I see no one in the cosmos with its devas, Māras, & Brahmās, in this generation with its contemplatives & brahmans, its royalty & commonfolk, who could hurl out my mind or rip open my heart or, grabbing me by the feet, hurl me across the Ganges.“I don’t see anyone in this world with its gods, Māras, and divinities, this population with its ascetics and brahmins, its gods and humans who could do that to me.

Api ca tvaṁ, āvuso, puccha yadākaṅkhasī”ti. But nevertheless, ask me what you wish.”But anyway, ask what you wish.”

Atha kho sūcilomo yakkho bhagavantaṁ gāthāya ajjhabhāsi: So Suciloma the yakkha addressed the Blessed One in verse:Then Spiky addressed the Buddha in verse:

“Rāgo ca doso ca kutonidānā, “Passion & aversion come from what cause?“Where do greed and hate come from?

Aratī ratī lomahaṁso kutojā; Displeasure, delight, horripilation are born from what?From where spring discontent, desire, and terror?

Kuto samuṭṭhāya manovitakkā, Arising from what do thoughts fling the mind around,Where do the mind’s thoughts originate,

Kumārakā dhaṅkamivossajanti”. as boys, a (captive) crow?”like a crow let loose by boys.”

“Rāgo ca doso ca itonidānā, “Passion & aversion come from this1 as a cause;“Greed and hate come from here;

Aratī ratī lomahaṁso itojā; displeasure, delight, horripilation are born from this;from here spring discontent, desire, and terror;

Ito samuṭṭhāya manovitakkā, arising from this thoughts fling the mind around,here’s where the mind’s thoughts originate,

Kumārakā dhaṅkamivossajanti. as boys, a (captive) crow:like a crow let loose by boys.

Snehajā attasambhūtā, They’re born from affection arisen from the mind, from oneself,2Born of affection, originating in oneself,

Nigrodhasseva khandhajā; like the trunk-born (shoots) of a banyan tree,like prop roots sprouting from a banyan;

Puthū visattā kāmesu, thick, attached to sensuality,the many kinds of attachment to sensual pleasures

Māluvāva vitatāvane. like a māluva vine spread in a forest.are like camel’s foot creeper creeping through the woods.

Ye naṁ pajānanti yatonidānaṁ, Those who discern where it’s bornThose who understand where they come from

Te naṁ vinodenti suṇohi yakkha; drive it out—listen, yakkha!dispel them—listen up, spirit!

Te duttaraṁ oghamimaṁ taranti, They cross over this flood, so hard to cross,They cross this flood so hard to cross,

Atiṇṇapubbaṁ apunabbhavāyā”ti. never crossed before, for the sake of no further becoming.”not crossed before, so as to not be reborn.”

Sūcilomasuttaṁ pañcamaṁ. The Suciloma Sutta, the fifth.
