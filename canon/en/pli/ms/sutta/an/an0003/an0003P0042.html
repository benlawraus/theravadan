5. Cūḷavagga 5. The Lesser Chapter 5. The Lesser Chapter

Tiṭhānasutta Instances Three Grounds

“Tīhi, bhikkhave, ṭhānehi saddho pasanno veditabbo. “In these three instances, monks, one can be recognized as a person of conviction & confidence. “There are three grounds, mendicants, by which a person with faith and confidence can be known.

Katamehi tīhi? Which three? What three?

Sīlavantānaṁ dassanakāmo hoti, saddhammaṁ sotukāmo hoti, vigatamalamaccherena cetasā agāraṁ ajjhāvasati muttacāgo payatapāṇi vossaggarato yācayogo dānasaṁvibhāgarato. One wants to see virtuous people. One wants to hear the True Dhamma. One’s awareness cleansed of the stain of stinginess, one lives at home, freely generous, openhanded, delighting in being magnanimous, responsive to requests, delighting in the distribution of alms. They like to see ethical people. They like to hear the true teaching. And they live at home with heart rid of the stain of stinginess, freely generous, open-handed, loving to let go, committed to charity, loving to give and to share.

Imehi kho, bhikkhave, tīhi ṭhānehi saddho pasanno veditabbo. In these three instances, monks, one can be recognized as a person of conviction & confidence.” These are the three grounds by which a person with faith and confidence can be known.

Dassanakāmo sīlavataṁ, One who wants to see virtuous ones, They like to see ethical people;

saddhammaṁ sotumicchati; & wants to hear the True Dhamma they want to hear the true teaching;

Vinaye maccheramalaṁ, —having subdued the stain of stinginess— they’ve driven out the stain of stinginess:

sa ve saddhoti vuccatī”ti. is said to have conviction. that’s who’s called an individual of faith.”

Dutiyaṁ.
