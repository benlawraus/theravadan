Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.30 Numbered Discourses 3.30Numbered Discourses 3.30

3. Puggalavagga 3. Persons 3. Individuals

Avakujjasutta Upside Down Upside-down

“Tayome, bhikkhave, puggalā santo saṁvijjamānā lokasmiṁ. “Monks, there are these three types of persons to be found existing in the world. “These three individuals are found in the world.

Katame tayo? Which three? What three?

Avakujjapañño puggalo, ucchaṅgapañño puggalo, puthupañño puggalo. The person of upside down discernment, the person of lap discernment, and the person of wide-open discernment. One with upside-down wisdom, one with wisdom on their lap, and one with widespread wisdom.

Katamo ca, bhikkhave, avakujjapañño puggalo? “And which is the person of upside-down discernment? And who is the individual with upside-down wisdom?

Idha, bhikkhave, ekacco puggalo ārāmaṁ gantā hoti abhikkhaṇaṁ bhikkhūnaṁ santike dhammassavanāya. There is the case where a person, having gone to a monastery, often listens to the Dhamma in the presence of the monks. It’s an individual who often goes to the monastery to hear the teaching in the presence of the mendicants.

Tassa bhikkhū dhammaṁ desenti ādikalyāṇaṁ majjhekalyāṇaṁ pariyosānakalyāṇaṁ sātthaṁ sabyañjanaṁ, kevalaparipuṇṇaṁ parisuddhaṁ brahmacariyaṁ pakāsenti. The monks teach him the Dhamma admirable in the beginning, admirable in the middle, admirable in the end. They expound the holy life both in its particulars & in its meaning, entirely perfect, surpassingly pure. The mendicants teach them Dhamma that’s good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, meaningful and well-phrased. And they reveal a spiritual practice that’s entirely full and pure.

So tasmiṁ āsane nisinno tassā kathāya neva ādiṁ manasi karoti, na majjhaṁ manasi karoti, na pariyosānaṁ manasi karoti; He, while sitting in that seat, doesn’t attend to the beginning of that talk, doesn’t attend to the middle, doesn’t attend to the end. But even while sitting there, that person doesn’t apply the mind to the beginning, middle, or end of the discussion.

vuṭṭhitopi tamhā āsanā tassā kathāya neva ādiṁ manasi karoti, na majjhaṁ manasi karoti, na pariyosānaṁ manasi karoti. Having gotten up from that seat, he doesn’t attend to the beginning of that talk, doesn’t attend to the middle, doesn’t attend to the end. And when they rise from their seat, they don’t apply the mind to the beginning, middle, or end of the discussion.

Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, kumbho nikujjo tatra udakaṁ āsittaṁ vivaṭṭati, no saṇṭhāti; Just as when a pot is turned upside down, water poured there runs off and doesn’t stay; It’s like when a pot full of water is tipped over, so the water drains out and doesn’t stay.

evamevaṁ kho, bhikkhave, idhekacco puggalo ārāmaṁ gantā hoti abhikkhaṇaṁ bhikkhūnaṁ santike dhammassavanāya. in the same way, there is the case where a person, having gone to a monastery, often listens to the Dhamma in the presence of the monks. So tasmiṁ āsane nisinno tassā kathāya neva ādiṁ manasi karoti, na majjhaṁ manasi karoti, na pariyosānaṁ manasi karoti; He, while sitting in that seat, doesn’t attend to the beginning of that talk, doesn’t attend to the middle, doesn’t attend to the end. But even while sitting there, that person doesn’t apply the mind to the discussion in the beginning, middle, or end.

vuṭṭhitopi tamhā āsanā tassā kathāya neva ādiṁ manasi karoti, na majjhaṁ manasi karoti, na pariyosānaṁ manasi karoti. Having gotten up from that seat, he doesn’t attend to the beginning of that talk, doesn’t attend to the middle, doesn’t attend to the end. And when they rise from their seat, they don’t apply the mind to the beginning, middle, or end of the discussion.

Ayaṁ vuccati, bhikkhave, avakujjapañño puggalo. This is called a person of upside down discernment. This is called an individual with upside-down wisdom.

Seyyathāpi, bhikkhave, purisassa ucchaṅge nānākhajjakāni ākiṇṇāni—Just as when a person has various foods strewn over his lap— It’s like a person who has different kinds of food crammed on their lap—

tilā taṇḍulā modakā badarā. sesame seeds, husked rice, cakes, & jujubes— such as sesame, rice, sweets, or jujube—

So tamhā āsanā vuṭṭhahanto satisammosā pakireyya. and when getting up, his mindfulness lapsed, he would scatter them; so that if they rise from the seat without mindfulness, everything gets scattered.

Ime kho, bhikkhave, tayo puggalā santo saṁvijjamānā lokasminti. These are the three types of persons to be found existing in the world. These are the three individuals found in the world.

Avakujjapañño puriso, A man of upside down discernment— A person with upside-down wisdom,

dummedho avicakkhaṇo; stupid, injudicious, is a simpleton and cannot see,

Abhikkhaṇampi ce hoti, even if he often goes and even if they frequently

gantā bhikkhūna santike. in the presence of the monks, go into the mendicants’ presence,

Ādiṁ kathāya majjhañca, he can’t grasp anything such a person can’t learn

pariyosānañca tādiso; like the beginning, middle, or end of a talk, the beginning, middle, or end

Uggahetuṁ na sakkoti, for discernment of the discussion,

paññā hissa na vijjati. isn’t found in him. for their wisdom is lacking.

Ucchaṅgapañño puriso, A man of lap discernment the individual with wisdom on their lap

seyyo etena vuccati; is said to be better than that one. is better than that, it’s said;

Abhikkhaṇampi ce hoti, If he often goes but even if they frequently

gantā bhikkhūna santike. in the presence of the monks, go into the mendicants’ presence,

Ādiṁ kathāya majjhañca, while sitting in that seat, grasps the words such a person can only learn

pariyosānañca tādiso; of the beginning, middle, & end of the talk, the beginning, middle, and end

Nisinno āsane tasmiṁ, but getting up, while sitting in that seat;

uggahetvāna byañjanaṁ; he doesn’t discern anything like that, but they’ve only grasped the phrasing,

Vuṭṭhito nappajānāti, for he forgets for when they rise their understanding fails,

gahitaṁ hissa mussati. what he had grasped. and what they’ve learned is lost.

Puthupañño ca puriso, But a man of wide open discernment The person with widespread wisdom

seyyo etehi vuccati; is said to be better than those ones. is better than that, it’s said;

Abhikkhaṇampi ce hoti, If he often goes and if they, too, frequently

gantā bhikkhūna santike. in the presence of the monks, go into the mendicants’ presence,

Ādiṁ kathāya majjhañca, while sitting in that seat, he grasps the words one such can learn

pariyosānañca tādiso; of the beginning, middle, & end of the talk. the beginning, middle, and end

Nisinno āsane tasmiṁ, He remembers— while sitting in that seat;

uggahetvāna byañjanaṁ. the person of undivided mind, and when they’ve grasped the phrasing,

Dhāreti seṭṭhasaṅkappo, with the best of resolves. they remember it with the best of intentions.

Abyaggamānaso naro; Practicing the Dhamma in line with the Dhamma, That peaceful-hearted man,

Dhammānudhammappaṭipanno, he’ll put an end practicing in line with the teaching,

Dukkhassantakaro siyā”ti. to suffering & stress. would make an end of suffering.”

Dasamaṁ.

Puggalavaggo tatiyo.

Tassuddānaṁ

Samiddha gilāna saṅkhārā,

bahukārā vajirena ca;

Sevi jiguccha gūthabhāṇī,

andho ca avakujjatāti.
